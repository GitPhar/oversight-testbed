# One page, plain words

Sean Pharris · 2026-11-04 · long version: `paper/plain-words.md`.

**What it is.** An AI (Claude) runs small research projects and writes down its odds before each test. A separate Claude session tries to break every result, and a public record scores the odds against what happened. Sean steers. Can either be trusted to choose research?

**What the four programs found.**
- *Can quantum maths tell when a model is wrong?* No: a tie with ordinary maths on two Claude models, Haiku and Sonnet.
- *Does a model hold a fixed belief its confidence reports?* On Haiku, mostly not: question order moves its rating by about 25 points out of 100. Hide its earlier answer and it repeats it far less, on all three models. Opus partly contradicts the rest.
- *Does a model reason about other minds like its own?* No test passed (Haiku).
- *Can a quantum chip grade the odds?* A 3 November surprise never came back; no scored test passed.
- *Can Claude tell which way new published findings came out?* Yes, 76% right. Not yet repeated; a "which version flatters the paper?" shortcut comes close on harder items.

**The AI's score.** 0.259 on 19 tests, where a coin scores 0.25. Its odds don't separate passes from misses, and shift when reworded.

**The checks.** Every number held up, but 38 of 39 early write-ups overstated. The checks caught real set-up errors, changed verdicts, and caught 6 of 6 mistakes planted to test them.

**Strong:** rules written before data, plain failures, cheap (about $64), fully rebuildable.
**Weak:** too few tests to judge anything, no human odds, and nearly everything done by Claude.
