# Audit of ng-p3-15: what made 3 November different, the barrier or the shot grouping?

## In plain English

The verdict stands: the bet fails. Putting the barrier back did not bring back 3 November's 50% "yes" rate. The barrier block read 0.311 against 0.319 without the barrier in run 1, and 0.282 against 0.311 in run 2. The tables regenerate exactly from the archived counts, the seeds and PUB order recompute from the beacon, the circuits match their committed hashes, and the QPU spend (8 s of the 10 s approved) is as stated. Two sentences have to change. The first is the plain-English grouping sentence. Between the run-1 and run-2 write-ups it was changed from the pre-registered "may make a smaller difference … which this job cannot pin down" to "made a smaller difference … because the two 1,000-shot PUBs executed first". That promotes an uncorrected, one-run secondary (z = 2.07, unstable to dropping one piece) to a finding and gives it a cause. Run 2, by the file's own account, refutes that cause: the blocks again ran first, and grouping made −0.002. The second is the barrier-null sentence. It holds only for the average over the two groupings, so it must say so. What would break the remaining reading is a test of the one 3 November feature no arm reproduced: the circuit alone in its own job.

## The case that this result is false

The headline is "restoring the barrier did not bring back 3 November's result … the surprise was not the barrier", with three hours of stability on top. Argued from the other side:

1. **The barrier was never tested in 3 November's setting.** On 3 November the circuit ran alone in its own job. Here BB was one of 32 PUBs, and the execution spans show the server ran BB and NB together in one span, ahead of the 30 small pieces, in both runs. That happened even in run 2, where the seeded order put them last. So the server overrode the seeded order. Whatever the server does when it shares a span between PUBs (the order of shots, the rep-delay, how it schedules) applied to BB and to NB alike. If the barrier matters only for a lone circuit, this design cannot see it, and "the surprise was not the barrier" is a statement about a different set-up.
2. **The null is an average that may hide a crossover.** The sentence "the barrier made no difference larger than 0.05" comes from the pooled contrast. Unpooled, the barrier raises P(yes) in the interleaved pieces in both runs (+0.022, +0.027) and lowers it in the blocks in both runs (−0.008, −0.029). The interaction is −0.030 and −0.056 (z −1.0, −1.9). Neither interleaved CI lies inside ±0.05. A barrier effect that depends on grouping is exactly the kind of thing that could differ again for a lone 1,000-shot job.
3. **The "barrier is inert" story is untested at its root.** Whether IBM schedules measurements ALAP is not observed. If it does, the barrier is a no-op on the executed schedule both days, and "the surprise was not the barrier" holds only under the stated assumption, which nothing checks.
4. **The prediction is not independent across runs.** Run 2's "live calibration" prediction has T1, T2 and readout error identical to run 1's on all nine qubits, to 14 decimals, while the measured per-qubit marginals moved by about 0.2. "Every arm above the prediction" is therefore one comparison made twice, not two.
5. **The write-up already overreaches where the data are weakest.** The grouping sentence states a cause for a one-run, z = 2.07 secondary that did not repeat.

The checklist below decides how much of this holds. Points 1 and 3 are limits the pre-registration states (§6) and the D11 assumption clause carries. They do not reverse a fail, because a fail needs only BB < 0.40, which holds in both runs. Points 2 and 5 are real and produce the two cuts. Point 4 is a note.

## What would break it next

The one 3 November feature no arm reproduced is that the circuit ran **alone in its own job**. In both runs here, the server put BB and NB together in a shared span. The cheapest test that could overturn "the surprise was not the barrier (nor the grouping)" is two single-circuit jobs, each 1,000 shots on the same nine qubits: one with the barrier (3 November exactly) and one without. Submit them back to back and in a seeded order, with a live-target prediction committed before. Estimated cost by the program's formula: 2 × (0.28 ms × 1,000 + 2 s) ≈ 4.6 QPU s, $0 API. Pass if the barrier job reads ≥ 0.40 and beats the no-barrier job by ≥ 0.10 at z ≥ 3. A second, cheaper follow-up would settle the B6 lead: a run with 20 × 100 pieces per arm, about 4 QPU s, to tighten BI − NI and the block × barrier interaction (currently −0.043, z ≈ −2.1 pooled over both runs, post hoc). It also needs a check that IBM's reported calibration actually changes between reads, because run 2's matched run 1's to the last digit.
