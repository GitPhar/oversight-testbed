# Audit of ng-p3-2 and ng-p3-3: twirling/DD and alternative layouts on the Stage 1 circuit (one ibm_fez job)

## In plain English

Both "untestable" verdicts stand. Each pre-registration said in advance that the bet could not be scored if today's chip-versus-simulator gap was small: under 0.125 for P3-2 and under 0.10 for P3-3. Today's gap is 0.023. Its 95% interval runs to about 0.04, so the verdicts are nowhere near the thresholds. Everything regenerates. The analysis reproduces byte for byte, and I rebuilt the simulator predictions (0.26894, 0.26486, 0.26352) exactly, offline, from the committed calibration. The T1/T2 bug found in the ng-p3-1 audit is not in this code path. The predictions were taken from IBM's live device description, not through the faulty `overwrite_calibration`. Had the bug been in play, S would have moved by only 0.0002. No plausible simulator choice gets near the thresholds. A simulator with no noise at all gives 0.276.

The order was right. Each pre-registration was followed by its seed commit, then the predictions and all 200 circuits were committed, and only then was the job submitted. The job was submitted 18 s after the predictions commit and started on IBM's side 22 s after it. I rebuilt all 200 circuits, and their order, exactly from the beacon seeds.

What has to be cut is the reading around the verdicts.

- The write-up calls today's arm "the identical circuit" as 3 November's. It is not quite. Program 1's circuit had a barrier before measurement, and this one has none. The shots were also grouped differently: one block of 1,000 then, 20 interleaved blocks of 100 now.
- With two runs, nobody can say which afternoon was "unusual".
- "The disagreement did not come back" holds for P(yes) only. Qubit 125 still reads 0.14 below the simulator, about 12 standard errors. Each alternative layout also has one qubit about 0.12–0.14 low.
- The post-hoc mirror simulation has no committed code. I reproduced its 13.6% exactly with the prediction-time model, ALAP-scheduled, at seed 7. Unscheduled, the same model gives 19.8%, so the number depends on a choice the record does not state. The chip's 2.2% is far below every variant (lowest 11.7%). The comparison is a lead, not a finding.
- The "twirling overshoots the simulator, so it changed the error" sentence rests on about 2.3 standard errors.

## The case that this result is false

*(Written before the checklist.)* "Untestable" is the verdict that cannot be wrong in the usual sense, so the case against it has to attack the gate. The gate is |P_A − S| < 0.125, where S is the "corrected simulator". The ng-p3-1 audit showed that the corrected simulator's code does not write in T1/T2 and does not apply ZZ. If S is wrong, the gate could be wrong. A model with the right T1/T2, or the right asymmetric readout, might predict something far from 0.246, and then P3-2 and P3-3 were testable and should have been scored pass or fail. The predictions might also have been computed after the job, or the circuits changed after the prediction commit. "Untestable" would then be a convenient exit from a fail. The 0.125 and 0.10 floors appear in D3/D4 as "choices the map leaves open". They were not in the map, and they could have been tuned to the expected chip number.

Separately, the headline compares today with 3 November (0.246 vs 0.502). That comparison is not a pre-registered statistic of either bet's pass rule. It is the most quotable sentence in the file, and it rests on two runs that may not be the same experiment. Finally, P(yes) is a coarse summary of nine qubits. A device can "agree" on P(yes) while departing badly qubit by qubit, so "the gap was gone" may be a statistic that cannot see the gap.

The checklist below rejects the attack on the gate and on the order of events. It confirms the attack on the day-to-day reading and on the coarseness of P(yes).

## What would break it next

The verdicts cannot be broken from this record. The gate is 8–10 SE from its thresholds under every simulator variant tried. The open question is the day-to-day reading, and the cheapest test of it is one small job, which becomes a new bet with its own pre-registration (≈ 2.5 QPU s):

- arm A as submitted today, and arm A *with* the pre-measurement barrier restored;
- each as one 1,000-shot block and as 10 × 100 interleaved;
- R0/R1 on the nine qubits;
- run ≥ 3 h from any other run.

This separates "the chip drifted" from "the barrier or job grouping changed", which today's job cannot do. If the barrier arm alone returns to ~0.5, the 3 November surprise was the circuit, not the afternoon. A second repeat on another day costs ~1 s and starts the drift series that P3-3's unrun repeats were for.
