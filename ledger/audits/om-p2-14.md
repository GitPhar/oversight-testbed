# Audit of om-p2-14: with more repeats and a margin fixed in advance, does the model predict its own quirks any better than an observer's?

## In plain English

The verdict stands. The bet was that Haiku would predict its own planted-answer pull equally well under the self label and the "Sam" label, within ±0.05. It did not, and the fail is not close: the point estimate is −0.185 against a margin of 0.05. The direction (self label better) also holds:
- the pre-registered BCa interval is [−0.282, −0.087], and stays below −0.08 on 40 other bootstrap seeds;
- a within-vignette label-swap permutation test gives p ≈ 0.0002;
- no single vignette left out changes the reading.

Five sentences in the write-up have to change:
- The write-up calls Opus's undetermined reading a replication and "a second finding".
- It says the self arm was "within 0.04" when one class is off by 0.042.
- It describes the statistic as error in "how far its own planted answer pulls its re-rating". It measures the average pull per distance class. Cell by cell, the two labels' errors in uptake units cannot be told apart (−0.017, [−0.102, +0.081]). In rating points the self label is about 2 points closer.
- Its bold headline says Haiku "reads itself" better, which is a cause the label-only design cannot separate from a stereotype about "an AI".
- Its header says addendum 1 confirmed D20a, but addendum 1 said "Not confirmed".

The bet is also not registered in `reproduce.py`. The thing most likely to break the reading (the fail itself is safe) is a third label: a different named AI, or "a person". That test would show whether the Sam arm's extra pull belongs to "another AI" in general, which would make this a label effect, not self-knowledge.

## The case that this result is false

The headline is "Haiku reads itself detectably better than it reads Sam". Five things could make that false.

**The statistic is built to favour whichever arm has no average bias.**
- The block statistic averages u within each of three distance classes before taking absolute errors. Any cell-level noise that cancels in the class mean disappears. An arm that is noisy cell by cell but unbiased on average gets a block error near zero.
- So the self arm's 0.024 could hide predictions that track individual conversations no better than Sam's do.

**The interval is fragile.**
- The bootstrap of an absolute value near zero is biased away from zero, so the resampled self errors are inflated.
- The BCa interval then has to make a large bias correction (z0 far from 0) to stay below 0. The percentile interval printed beside it, [−0.244, −0.003], almost touches 0.
- If the bias correction is doing the work, "wholly below 0" is a fact about the BCa formula, not the data.

**Pooling may hide that only one direction carries the effect.** The classes pool plants above and below the model's base (±10, ±30, ±60). If the self advantage lives only in upward plants, the claim is narrower than written.

**The truth for Haiku is old.** It is program 1's re-ratings from 2026-11-03/04, while both arms' predictions are from today. Any drift moves the truth away from both arms, but not necessarily equally.

**"Reads itself" is a mechanism the design cannot see.**
- The arms differ only in "you" against "an AI assistant called Sam". A model that believes "other AIs" stick to their stated answers more than it would gives the same table without any self-access.
- Humans show this pattern: the bias blind spot (Pronin, Lin and Ross 2002).

**The secondary rows go further than the data.** The Opus "replication" rests on an undetermined interval on 8 vignettes with one prediction per cell.

The checklist below finds:
- the first point right at cell level, with no effect on the pre-registered reading;
- the second point wrong: the permutation test and other seeds back the BCa;
- the third point partly right: the effect is concentrated in upward plants, though both directions point the same way;
- the fifth point right about the wording, and right about Opus.
