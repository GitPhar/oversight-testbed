# Audit of om-p2-15: do Haiku's predictions of its own re-ratings beat a guess of "no effect"?

## In plain English

The result stands. The pre-registered test failed: over 324 cells, Haiku's predictions of its own re-ratings missed by 9.17 points against 8.85 for "no effect". The tables regenerate byte for byte, from the old checkout and from the clone's own copy of program 1, and no re-analysis I ran brings the pass rule near passing. Five sentences of the plain-English summary have to change. One says Haiku predicted it would move *toward* another person's number more than it did, but the predicted net pull was right (+2.2 against +2.2 points). The predictions instead scattered twice as far from its usual rating, and at 30 and 60 points their average uptake was *away* from the number. One calls the pooled result "no better than the guess", which the pre-registration forbids. One sums up as "good on average, poor cell by cell", but the own-answer cells are good cell by cell too. One says Haiku predicted "another assistant's re-ratings" more accurately, but no Sam re-ratings exist, and that gap has a jackknife-t interval that includes 0. One says Sean's card was framed by effect-level numbers, but the card shows none, and its event read cell by cell in its own unit also fails. What would break it next: re-asking the self arm on the 162 other-person cells, with a base-rating question added, for about $0.06.

## The case that this result is false

*Written after reproducing the tables and the first re-analysis pass, before working the checklist lines.*

The claim is a negative: "this test did not show that its self-predictions beat assuming no effect." The strongest case against it is that the fail is made by the design, not by the predictions. First, pooling. The primary averages two halves of the data that point in opposite directions. On the 162 own-answer cells, self beats no uptake by 3.9 points with a BCa interval well clear of 0. On the 162 other-person cells it loses by 4.5. A pooled zero made from +4.5 and −3.9 is not "no information". Second, the metric. MAE in rating points rewards a predictor that never moves and punishes one that moves the wrong way in a few cells. By median absolute error, self wins (4.0 against 4.7), and self is closer than no uptake in 172 cells against 147 (26 ties). Third, the comparator is not a know-nothing guess. "No uptake" is Haiku's *measured* usual rating, the mean of separate archived calls. The self arm has to predict that rating from the transcript as well as the shift, so any error in guessing its own usual rating counts against "self" and never against "no effect". If the other-person losses come from that, then the self-predictions may carry real information about the quirk that this comparison cannot see. Fourth, power. The design review's calibrated worlds gave P(pass) of about 0 for Gaussian errors, and 0.20 to 0.56 for heavy-tailed ones, at the cell-level r = 0.87 om-p2-4 had already published. A fail was the expected outcome even for a predictor with some skill.

What this case does not do is overturn the *label*. The pre-registered event is a pooled detection, and the write-up already says "did not show", not "no better than". What survives the case, and what the checklist confirms, is narrower: the write-up's own-versus-other story and its account of *how* the other-person predictions go wrong are partly false. The fail itself is a property of the pooled pre-registered question, not proof that Haiku lacks self-knowledge.
