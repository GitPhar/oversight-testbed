# Audit of ng-p3-15 (round 3): what made 3 November different, the barrier or the shot grouping?

## In plain English

The fail stands and the outcome stays 0. Every number regenerates from the raw counts with my own readout code and with the author's script run offline; the beacon seeds recompute; the PUB labels match the predictions files; the QPU ledger carries exactly two P3-15 jobs at 4 s each, and the program's running total is 69 s of 420 after P3-16. This bet is the opposite of ng-p3-1 and ng-p3-5: it could have passed. At 3 November's effect size the pre-registered rule has a z of about 12, and the 7% odds were a real forecast that turned out right. Two things need fixing in the file. Its header still says "unaudited" although it has been audited and cut (D27). And the one observation every arm in three jobs agrees on, that the chip reads about 0.03 above the live-calibration simulator (pooled z 4.5 and 4.1 in the two runs, and again in P3-16), is stated only in words; it should carry its numbers, still as a lead, because it is the strongest signal program 3 has produced and nothing pre-registered covers it.

## The case that this result is false

From the other side: the bet asked whether 3 November's packaging caused its 0.50, and the design never reproduced that packaging. BB shared a job and an execution span with NB in both runs; the server ignored the seeded order and ran the two blocks first together. So "the surprise was not the barrier" is a claim about a bundle 3 November never used. The null sentence rests on a pooled contrast, and unpooled the barrier raises P(yes) in the interleaved pieces in both runs (+0.022, +0.027) while lowering it in the blocks: an interaction of −0.03 and −0.056 that a lone-job barrier could turn into something. The prediction the arms are compared against was computed once and copied: IBM had not re-measured these qubits between runs, so "three hours later the chip agreed" compares two draws against one number. And every arm sits above that number, which the file waves through as a lead while the pre-mortem predicted 0.25.

What survives: the fail needs only BB < 0.40, which holds by 6 SE in both runs. The packaging gap was closed by ng-p3-16 (the circuit alone in its own job: 0.306). The crossover is reported, post hoc, uncorrected and not significant. The above-prediction offset is real and replicated across three jobs, but it is a fact about the simulator's calibration inputs, not about the barrier, and it was not pre-registered; it belongs in the file with its numbers, which is cut 2.
