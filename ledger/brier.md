## In plain English

Before each test, the engine wrote down how likely it thought a real, rule-passing result was. 8 bets have both a stated probability and an outcome decided by their pre-registered rule. The engine expected 2.90 hits and got 3.

- **The engine's Brier score is 0.263.** Lower is better: 0 is perfect, 0.25 is what always saying 50% scores, and 0.234 is what always saying the observed hit rate (3/8) would have scored. On this tiny sample the engine scored about the same as a coin, slightly worse.
- **The errors lean one way.** Every scored bet was given 15–30%, and both hits were bets given 25%. If anything the odds were too low. With five bets, that lean is not reliable.
- **On net, the full-read revisions made the odds worse.** Scored on the odds first written down (before the overnight literature pass), the Brier score is 0.241. The pass lowered direction 3 from 0.45 to 0.25, and direction 3 hit; it also lowered directions 1 and 4, which missed.
- **Five bets cannot establish calibration.** Every filled bucket's 95% interval includes its predicted value. The table is published so the number accrues honestly, not because it says much yet.
- **The human column is empty, honestly.** Sean's odds were not recorded in the founding week, so there is nothing to score. From program 2 onward he records them before data with `map/human-odds-template.md`.
