# Design review of ng-p3-16

## In plain English

As built, this test can answer its question: does the 3 November result come back when the circuit runs alone in its own job? If running alone brings back 0.50, the bet passes every time in simulation. If nothing has changed since P3-15, it fails every time. The code builds the jobs as described, refuses to submit without each approval, and turns a short or failed job into "untestable". Five things must change first, and none of them moves the bar. First, several of the pre-written sentences are false in plausible worlds. One example: the lone no-barrier job comes back while the lone barrier job does not, and the text would still say that "none" of the variations brought it back. Second, "within ±0.05" can be read two ways, and the two readings pick different sentences 41% of the time in the expected world. Third, a pass rests on one job, A. The design has a second lone job, B, but gives it no role in the reading. Fourth, the claim that A and CB "differ only in" job sharing leaves out that they are separate jobs, in a fixed order. Fifth, the new budget gate would stop the run before job A, the primary arm, if the engine approved only the 7.1 s estimate. The fix is to approve the pre-registered 10 s. Verdict: **fix first**.

### D1. The rule can be applied by a stranger: **flaw** (F2)

The scored rule (§5.1–5.3) is unambiguous for completed jobs. I applied `analyse()` to synthetic raw files from the stubbed `submit()`, 200 draws per world (`dry6_worlds.py`). Each world got exactly one verdict (table under D8). §5.3 runs in code: a job error, a PUB with 990 shots, and a missing job each give `untestable` (`dry3_gates.py`).

There are two gaps.
- **"A − CB within ±0.05" is undefined for the primary.** §4 defines equivalence (the whole 90% CI inside ±0.05) only for the secondary barrier effects. The code (`_class` → `within_equiv`) uses the CI for A − CB too. A stranger could equally read the §5 fail sentence by its point estimate. In the pre-mortem world (all arms 0.30), the CI rule selects the "no difference larger than 0.05" sentence 57.5% of the time, and the point-estimate rule P(|Δ̂| < 0.05) ≈ 98.5%. So the two readings choose different sentences in about 41% of expected outcomes.
- **A budget-gate stop has no verdict in the text.** D29's mid-run gate can stop before a job (D10). Then not all three jobs completed, so the result is not a fail. It is not a pass either. And §5.3 lists only "any job errors" or a wrong shot count. The code returns `untestable` ("job A did not run"), but the pre-registration gives no verdict for this case.

### D2. Every manipulation takes effect: **holds**

- **Circuits** (`dry1_check.py`, `dry4_localsampler.py`). B = the archived gate list, rebuilt (`sx 219, rz 153, cz 117, x 5, measure 9`). A and CB are the same plus one barrier, at index 494 of 504, on all nine qubits, just before the first measure. `p3_15_repeat.base_isa()` equals B. CB is the same circuit as A and CN the same as B (`==`). The QPY round trip preserves all four circuits, barrier included. Nothing re-transpiles them: `run_hardware` passes them to `SamplerV2.run` as is. That call (Batch + SamplerV2, PUBs, shots) is unchanged since the code that ran 3 November's job (`afe1c1b` → `98d6ac6` changed only QPU-seconds parsing). - **Job structure, real seed** (`seed32_order` 1288729591). The order is **C [CB, CN], then B, then A**. Through a real local-mode `Batch(FakeFez)` + `SamplerV2`, job C returned 2 PUBs of [1000, 1000] shots, and B and A returned 1 PUB of [1000] each. So the "alone" manipulation holds by construction for A and B: a one-PUB job. Whether C's two PUBs share one execution span is server-side and cannot be checked offline. The code saves spans and bitstrings (free read) and reports `C_same_span`, as §2 says. In both P3-15 runs, the two 1,000-shot PUBs shared one span.
- **Barrier, simulated** (`dry7_predict.py`, `predict()` run against FakeFez in place of the live target, 20,000 shots). barrier_ALAP 0.2713, nobarrier_ALAP 0.2713, barrier_ASAP 0.2776, nobarrier_ASAP 0.2703. The simulator has no notion of "alone", so it predicts P_A = P_CB. That is the pre-mortem's world.
- **Parameters actually used.** SHOTS 1000, SIM_SHOTS 100,000. Bars: HIGH 0.40, BIG 0.10, ZMIN 3, EQUIV 0.05, CONTROL_TOL 0.05. P315_BB 0.2965. Caps: 4 s per job, 10 s total, reserve 300 s. All match §2–§5. The 3 November 0.502 reproduces exactly from `entangled-mind/results/stage1-run-1.json` with `p3sim.p_yes`. Its ideal is 0.294 and its noisy simulation 0.251.

### D3. Every control can fail: **holds**

The CB control (within 0.05 of 0.2965) fires in every world where the chip has moved: all arms at 0.50 (W4: 100%), and C back but not the lone jobs (W7: 100%). With no drift, a false alarm needs |Z| > 0.05/0.0145 ≈ 3.4, so P ≈ 0.0006. The same limitation as ng-p3-15 applies, and it is not a flaw. The band (0.2465–0.3465) also contains the noiseless ideal (0.294) and the simulator (0.27). So "control passed" means only that the chip has not moved by more than 0.05 since P3-15. §5 uses it only that way, to gate the reading and not the verdict.

### D4. Rivals are matched: **holds**

No fitted models compete. "Alone" and "shared" are measured as equal 1,000-shot PUBs of identical circuits. Neither side is built to lose.

### D5. A dumb baseline is beaten, and the design can see the effect: **holds**

- **Dumb predictors.** "The chip equals its simulator" (all arms about 0.27–0.30) gives P(pass) = 0 (W0). "Constant 0.50 everywhere" gives P(pass) = 0 (W4). The rule needs a difference between A and CB, so no constant world passes.
- **Power.** SE(Δ) ≈ 0.0205. At 3 November's gap (A 0.50, CB 0.30), P(pass) = 1.00. At half the gap (A 0.40, CB 0.30), P(pass) = 0.36. The binding condition is Δ ≥ 0.10, as §4 says.
- **"No difference" reading.** The margin is 0.05, and §4 states its weak power honestly. The CI half-width is 0.034. With a true difference of zero, the CI falls inside the margin 57.5% of the time (simulated; §4 says 56%). The minimum detectable effect is below the margin only for an equivalence claim at about 57% power. The design does not lean on that claim: "not inside" is read as no evidence. Acceptable for a secondary reading.

### D6. Foreseeable execution risks are piloted: **holds**

The pre-mortem is of kind `world`, and there is no LLM content to be refused. Both job shapes have already run on this account through the same `run_hardware` path: a lone 1,000-shot single-PUB job (3 November, `dbl423dgg49c73dk0db0`, billed 2 s), and two 1,000-shot PUBs sharing a span (P3-15, twice). Rules for the failure modes are in code: a job error, a wrong shot count, and a resubmission guard on both the raw file and the QPU-ledger purpose (`dry3_gates.py`). No pilot is needed.

### D9. One item cannot decide it: **flaw** (F4)

§4 handles P10 as "one circuit, one evening, … pass, unreplicated". But the scored contrast rests on a single job, A, against a single PUB, CB. The design's own pre-mortem names "a passing state of the chip", and such a state can land in one job and not another. If it coincided with job A, the bet would pass (W2), and the pass sentence would credit "running alone". The design already has the one in-evening replicate of "alone": job B, a second lone job at a different time. Barrier and no-barrier read alike in P3-15 and in simulation. But B − CN is "secondary, reported, not scored", with no sentence tying it to a pass. W1 (both lone jobs up) and W2 (only A up) produce the same pass sentence. Nothing pre-registers what happens when the leave-one-out replicate disagrees.

### D11. The bet can both pass and fail: **holds**

These are the design's own named worlds (§1, pre-mortem), simulated through `submit()` and `analyse()`:
- In the world where the hypothesis holds (running alone brings back 0.50, W1), P(pass) = 1.00.
- In the pre-mortem world (lone barrier job ≈ 0.30, W0), P(fail) = 1.00 and P(pass) = 0.00.
- Intermediate worlds land in between: half return, P(pass) 0.36; A 0.38 v CB 0.26, 0.12.

So the result is not known before the run. The engine's 5% is a prior over which world holds, not a ceiling the design imposes. Both P(pass) and P(fail) clear 10% in the worlds that bear on them.
