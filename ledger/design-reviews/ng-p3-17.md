Reviewer and author are the same model (claude-opus-5-5), so this is not an independent-family read. Seed commit 323018a. ## In plain English

The question is well chosen and the machinery is mostly sound. The models are nested, the knobs take effect, the gates refuse uncommitted inputs, the seed recomputes, and nothing can cost money. But the check meant to show the test can fail does not do its job. In the world with no blur at all, h221 and h268 were each called "blur still needed" in 2 of 8 draws, against the 1% the test claims. The headline "P(fail | W_readout) = 1.0" hides this, because it multiplies over pairs and h088 alone (0 of 8) drives it to 1. Because the rule needs all three pairs, a test that is too lenient on h221 and h268 is exactly the pass the bet scores. There are three smaller problems. The observed no-blur fit is started less well than the null's fits. The one-setting robustness sentence is written in §4 but not in the code. And two §5 sentences use nouns the design cannot carry. All four can be fixed without changing what is tested.

## Checks

**D1. A stranger can apply the rule: holds.** I ran `--check` (synthetic only; 3 s). `verdict()` returned exactly one verdict on each table: all p = 0.001 → pass; h221 at 0.2 → fail; h221 at exactly 0.01 → fail (strict <); h221 at 0.0099 with h268 at 0.5 → fail; non-finite h221 → untestable. The p formula, B = 100 → 1,000 extension at p ≤ 0.05, and `B_complete` (B ∈ {100, 1000}) match §4–§5. At B = 100 the floor 1/101 = 0.0099 < 0.01, but any p ≤ 0.05 is extended, so a pass is always decided at B = 1,000 (≤ 9 null draws at or above LR_b).

**D2. Every manipulation takes effect: holds.** `--check` on GENERIC_A: at the upper bound A′ moves the rates 1%/2% → 2.94%/5.88%, and at the lower bound to 0.03%/0.06%. The cohR fit recovers synthetic knobs (0.018, 0.040, 0.019, 0.040 for 0.02, 0.04, 0.02, 0.04), and logL moves −45,433 → −45,010 between zero and true knobs. On the archived matrices (columns sum to 1, so prepared states are columns, as `rates()` assumes), the primary bounds put the upper-bound rates at 3r to within 2% on every knob (h221 [0.0074, 0.036, 0.0101, 0.0164] against 3r [0.0075, 0.0364, 0.0101, 0.0165]; h088 and h268 the same). The bounds in the recovery JSON are [−r, +2r] as §2 says. The bootstrap null uses `readout(A, θ_coh[7:11])`, and every refit starts again from the base A, which is correct. Small note, not a flaw: at the lower bound A′ has small negative entries (h221 −0.0009, h088 −0.0007, h268 −0.0016). The likelihood clips them, so fits will not go there, but the lower edge is not "readout error 0" for every column.

**D3. Every control can fail: flaw (F1).** W_readout is the no-blur control: truth is P3-5c's coh state through A′ with the knobs at their upper bound, so cohR is exactly true. Per pair, the test should call it "still needed" in about 1% of draws. The committed recovery gives 2/8 on h221 (LR 14.74 and 10.29 against q99 6.37, null maximum 8.4), 0/8 on h088 and 2/8 on h268 (9.73 and 12.35 against 7.50). If the true rate were 1%, two or more of eight would have probability about 0.003, and it happened on two pairs. The summary P(fail | W_readout) = 1 − Π(needed/8) = 1 − (2/8 · 0 · 2/8) = 1.0 is fixed at 1 by h088 alone. As built, the control's summary cannot register a miscalibrated pair.
- *Replay (S4):* I re-ran the eight W_readout draws on h221 and h268 with the recovery's own seeds and calls. Every LR_b reproduced to 0.01. Refitting cohR with extra starts (the true generator, the upper-bound corner, full16R's optimum with blur removed) left the four flagged draws flagged (14.74, 10.29, 9.74, 12.14), so they are not under-fitted cohR.
- *Where it comes from (S1, h221, well-fitted, n = 10 starts):* with the truth on the bound, LR_b exceeded the recovery's null q99 of 6.37 in 5 of 16 fresh draws (7.0, 7.9, 9.5, 10.0, 10.1). The recovery's null was generated at draw 0's *fitted* knobs, and e0_i was fitted at −0.0022 against a true +0.005. A null generated off the bound is narrower than one generated on it. In S3 (bootstrap-draw fits, cold starts), the mean LR_b was 4.1 generated at the fitted knobs and 7.8 at the bound. This is the known failure of a parametric bootstrap when the true parameter sits on a boundary of a composite null. The run builds its null the same way, from the observed cohR fit, and the pre-mortem itself expects the knobs at their bounds. On h268 the effect is smaller in my draws (0 of 16 above 7.50; 2 of 24 counting the recovery's).
- *Consequence:* consider the realistic fail world the audit trail points to, where h088 has blur and h221 and h268 are readout only. Its pass probability is about the product of the two per-pair false-positive rates, perhaps several per cent where it should be about 10⁻⁴. A scored pass could come from this miscalibration.

**D4. Rivals are matched: flaw (F2).** cohR (11) and full16R (20) are nested, with the same knobs and bounds. The embedding start [cohR, 0 blur] guarantees LR_b ≥ 0, so neither side wins by construction. But the observed statistic and its null are not fitted with equal care, and on the side that matters the observed one gets less:
- The observed cohR gets 11 starts (zero plus 10 random), every one with all four knobs at 0.
- Each null-draw cohR also gets a warm start at the observed cohR optimum, which is that draw's generating point.

In S1 (h221, W_readout truth, the same 16 count sets fitted both ways), the run's observed procedure fell short of the cohR optimum in 2 of 16 draws, by 1.32 and 2.73 logL. That inflates LR_b by 2.75 and 5.53, against null 99th percentiles of 6–14. The null-draw procedure found the optimum on the same counts. On full16R the null-draw fits were short by only 0.01–0.05 on average (they lack the observed fit's `plain` and inc9R starts, but get the observed full16R optimum). On h268 the largest gap was 1.05. Both effects push toward "still needed", which is toward the pass. With cold, knob-zero starts the cohR landscape stalls often: in S3, 3-start cold fits inflated the mean LR_b on h221 from about 5 to 7.8. The `starts()` function never draws random knob values.

**D5. Dumb baseline and visibility: holds.** This is not a "no difference" rule, so no equivalence margin is needed. The dumbest set-up that must not pass is no blur with readout inside the bounds (W_readout). The design names it and runs it, and it does not pass overall (its per-pair calibration is F1). Carry-over (0.95) and the base rate (0.35) are scored beside the odds. Power: W_blur gives LR_b 19–146 against q99 13–14 on every pair, 8 of 8.

**D6. Execution risks: holds.** The pre-mortem is of kind `world`. There is no model and no refusal surface, so no pilot is needed. Run time: a bootstrap-draw fit took 4–10 s under the current load (load average 34 on 12 cores) and an observed-style fit 16–30 s. Even 1,000 draws × 3 pairs on 11 workers fits the stated ≤ 3 h.

**D7. Comparisons match: holds.** cohR and full16R see the same counts, the same A, the same knob bounds and the same reference state (P3-5c's T1/T2-asserted `reference_state`). Against P3-5c, the named factor (four knobs in every model) is the main difference. The others are disclosed or immaterial: the null generator is now cohR with knobs, inc9R is not fitted in null draws, and deviance is on 7 df rather than 11. The F2 start asymmetry is a fitting flaw, not a difference between compared conditions.

**D8. The verdict sentences are written: flaw (F3).** All five sentences exist and `verdict()` emits them verbatim. Three problems:
- (a) The fail sentence and §1 say "P3-5c's 'blur on every pair'". That is not P3-5c's claim. Its title and sentences were cut to "on all three archived pairs (one job, 3 November), the 16-knob model needs … a blur-type term" (audit round 2, cut 1; D30). §6 of this file also says "every pair" will not appear in the write-up.
- (b) "the archive cannot tell blur from misreading" and "readout error that large can/cannot stand in for it" use mechanism nouns and an unqualified "readout error". The design can carry only "the blur-type term" against "readout knobs of this form": per qubit, independent of the partner qubit, fixed over the job, applied after A. Correlated and time-varying readout error lie outside it (§6), but the sentences cover them.
- (c) h088's full model already misfits (deviance 63.3 on 11 df in P3-5c), so its null is drawn from a model the counts reject. Round 3 recorded the lack of a misfit-aware test as this review family's D5 miss. Here the overdispersion-scaled LR_b is a secondary with no sentence, so the pass sentence can say the term "survives" on h088 even if the scaled LR_b falls below its null's q99.

**D9. One item cannot decide it: flaw (F4).** The unit is the pair, and the all-three rule (one pair decides a fail, and the result names it) is pre-registered. But:
- (a) §4's robustness clause ("if one setting carries more than half of a 'still needed' LR_b, the sentence for that pair adds 'This rests mostly on setting <S>'") is not in `verdict()`. The run computes `top_share`, but nothing passes it to the sentences, although §5 says the sentences are "used verbatim (`verdict()`)".
- (b) The clause is again a one-setting rule. The ng-p3-5c audit (round 2) recorded exactly this as a design-review miss: two settings (XZ and XY) carried 69% of h221's rotation LR and passed a one-setting rule unseen.

**D11. **F1 (D3): fixed. Bar (ii) is not met as recorded, and I confirm on a stricter check.**
- *(i) Two-generator null:* `bootstrap()` builds a null at the fitted cohR point and one at the cohR fit with all knobs held at their upper bound (`corner_null_theta`). Each null follows its own B = 100 → 1,000 rule. Then p = max of the two, and `B_complete` requires both. This is in place.

- *(ii) Per-pair no-blur rates:* 2/24 on h221, 1/24 on h088 and 3/24 on h268. Two of the three are above my ≤ 0.05 bar, so I had to decide. The recovery classifies all 24 draws against one threshold: the larger of two 99th percentiles, each estimated from a single B = 100 null on the first draw. A B = 100 quantile is noisy, and it is looser than the run's rule, which extends to B = 1,000 whenever p ≤ 0.05. On h268 that one threshold came out at 8.71.
- *Direct check, h268:* I drew 100 independent data sets from the W_readout truth (P3-5c's coh state, knobs at the upper corner, which is also where the run's corner null is generated). I fitted each with the D43 observed-draw procedure (no warm start), and fitted another 100 with the D43 null-draw procedure (warm start from a first draw's optima).

  - 4% of world draws and 4% of null draws exceed 8.71. So the recovery's 3/24 reflects that low threshold, not a null narrower than the truth.
  - World draws above the null-draw q99: 2 of 100.
- *Direct check, h221:* 80 + 80 draws, the same way. KS p = 0.24. World draws above the null-draw q99: 2 of 80. Above 11.10: 1 of 80, the same as the null draws.
- *Conclusion:* with the generator on the bound, the null reproduces the LR_b distribution of the no-blur truth on both pairs. The run's size at its actual B is near the nominal 1%; my estimates are 1–2.5% at these n. The original failure was a null generated off the bound, and the corner generator removes it. The recovery's rates above 0.05 come from its B = 100 classifier, not from the run's rule.
