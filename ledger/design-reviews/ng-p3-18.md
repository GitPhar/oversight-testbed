# Design review of ng-p3-18

## In plain English

As built, this test can answer its question: do both error terms come back when the same 39 circuits run again in a fresh job? The circuits, the reference-state construction, the assignment matrix and the fits are exactly P3-5c's. I checked this bit for bit on 3 November's inputs. The pre-run worlds are reproducible and can go either way. At 3 November's term sizes the bet passes every time, and in the world with no extra error it fails every time. I added the job's real readout noise (a matrix estimated from 1,000 shots per state), and that does not make the no-error world pass. Five things must change first. None of them moves the bar.
1. The reference state is built from IBM's calibration read before submission. 3 November's was read at the time the job ran, so a recalibration while the job waits in the queue would add a difference nobody named.
2. The submission path can be resubmitted after a crash while the job is queued. It can also lose finished counts if a client call fails after the job has run.
3. The fail sentence repeats the "every pair" that P3-5c's audit cut. It offers "shot count" as an explanation, which the design's own worlds rule out at 3 November's sizes. And it drops P3-5c's misfit sentence.
4. The setting-share and pair-flip sentences that §4 promises are not in the code. The one-setting rule repeats the gap P3-5c's audit found.
5. There is no verdict for a refusal before submission.

Verdict: **fix first**.

### D1. The rule can be applied by a stranger: **flaw** (F5)

`verdict()` applied to all 64 label tables (3 pairs × {rotation, blur}) returns exactly one verdict each time: 27 fail with 0 pairs both, 27 fail with 1, 9 pass with 2, 1 pass with 3 (`dry7_verdict.py`). §5.3 works in code: 38 results for 39 circuits, a PUB short by 300 shots and a recorded job error each give `untestable` (`dry3_submit.py`). The adaptive bootstrap rule is unambiguous: p = (1 + #null ≥ LR)/(B + 1), extended to B = 1,000 when p ≤ 0.05, and present iff p < 0.01. `--analyse` runs end to end on local-mode counts and writes the per-pair and overall sentences with the run date filled in (`dry8_analyse.py`, B cut to 4/8 for speed).

The gap: there is no verdict for a stop before submission. `--prepare` raises if an archived circuit is no longer ISA on the live target (a retired qubit or CZ pair) or if a property is missing. `--submit` re-checks ISA on the live target, and the reserve rule or the hard stop can refuse. §5.3 covers only a job that errors, short PUBs and a count mismatch. So a stranger cannot tell whether such a stop is untestable, withdrawn or waiting.

### D2. Every manipulation takes effect: **holds**

- **Circuits** (`dry1_check.py`): 39 circuits, 13 per pair in the archived order (9 settings, then calibrations 00/01/10/11). All are ISA on FakeFez, and gate counts equal the archive for all 39. `load_isa` rebuilds each circuit gate by gate from the archived list.
- **Shots:** 66,000 in all, giving an estimate of 20.48 s and a cap of 24 s. Through the real `run_hardware` path (Batch + SamplerV2, FakeFez local mode, `dry3_submit.py`), the job returned 2,000 shots on each of the 27 tomography PUBs and 1,000 on each of the 12 calibration PUBs. Keys were two-bit `00…11`, so the per-PUB shots in the `(circuit, None, shots)` tuples override `shots=2000`, as `SamplerPub.coerce` also shows.
- **Reference state, the knob this bet turns** (`dry2_ref.py`): `reference_states()` given 3 November's snapshot reproduces `C.reference_state` exactly (max |Δ| 0, 1e-16 and 2e-16 on the three pairs). With a perturbed snapshot (T2 × 0.5, T1 × 0.7, CZ error × 2), it moves by trace distance 0.006, 0.057 and 0.061, so the fresh calibration does reach ρ_ref. The live snapshot would list the same 14 qubits and 12 CZ pairs as 3 November's.
- **Assignment matrix:** built the P3-18 way from 3 November's calibration counts, it equals program 1's stored matrix exactly on all three pairs (Qiskit order verified).
- **Parameters used:** SHOTS 2000/1000, B0 100, B1 1000, extension at 0.05, α 0.01, the primary set coh/inc9/full16, LR_rotation = full16 − inc9 with null inc9, LR_blur = full16 − coh with null coh, the bootstrap at the job's own n, deviance df 11, seed32_boot. All match §2 and §4.

### D3. Every control can fail: **holds**

The control world is W_neither (the simulator's state is the whole story). It must be rejected, and it is: 0 of 6 draws per pair show both terms, so P(pass) = 0. I re-derived the first W_both and W_neither draws on h221 from the committed RNG path: 36.8218 / 56.7895 and 7.1423 / 1.1362, identical to the committed file (`dry4_worlds_repro.py`). The worlds file uses 3 November's exact assignment matrix, but this job estimates its matrix from 1,000 shots per state, so I rebuilt W_neither with the real input (`dry5_anoise.py`). The data come from the true matrix and the fit uses a matrix sampled at 1,000 shots per column (median max |Â − A| 0.0066), with 40 draws per pair at 2,000 shots against the worlds file's null 99th percentiles. Results: rotation exceeded the line 0 of 120 times, blur 1 of 120 (h268), and both 0 of 120. At 3 November's 4,000/4,000 shots it was 0 of 30 for both. A quarter of the calibration shots does not let the no-error world pass.

### D4. Rivals are matched: **holds**

These are P3-5c's nested families, unchanged. P3-5c's D26 recovery on the real reference states labelled neither, blur-only, rotation-only and both correctly. The known out-of-family rival, readout drift beyond A (P3-5c audit, round 2), is named in §6 and is P3-17's subject. The sampling part of readout error is covered under D3.

### D5. A dumb baseline is beaten, and the design can see the effect: **holds**

The dumbest world (no error beyond the simulator, with the real 1,000-shot readout matrix) fails 120 of 120 times. At 3 November's term sizes, 2,000 shots detect both terms on h088 and h268 in 6 of 6 draws and on h221 in 4 of 6, so P(pass) = 1.0. No "no difference" claim is made: a fail is "both on ≤ 1 pair", not equivalence.

### D6. Foreseeable execution risks are piloted: **holds**

The pre-mortem is of kind `design`, and no model content can be refused. 3 November's job had the same shape: one 39-PUB job through `run_hardware`. I verified per-PUB shots offline. ### D8. The verdict sentences are written: **flaw** (F2)

The pair sentences hold: "terms in a 16-knob model, not identified mechanisms". Three problems:
- **The fail sentence quotes "P3-5c's 'both terms on every pair'".** The P3-5c audit (cut 1, D30) removed "every pair" as a scope word. P3-5c's record now reads "on all three archived pairs (one job, 3 November)".
- **The fail sentence offers "(or to that job's shot count)" and leaves out the fresh reference.** The design's own worlds show that at 3 November's term sizes, 2,000 shots pass with P = 1.0, and P(fail | W_both) = 0. So shot count alone is not a reading this design can carry. A fail means the terms were smaller or absent in this job, measured against its own reference state, which was rebuilt from that day's calibration. "Only 0 of 3" also needs a 0 form.
- **P3-5c's misfit sentence is gone.** P3-5c's per-pair text carried "Even both terms together leave a misfit the model cannot explain (deviance … on 11 df)". Round 3 (cut 3) added that on a misfitting pair the nulls are not calibrated, with the overdispersion-scaled LRs given. P3-18 computes `misfit`, `overdispersion` and the scaled LRs, but no sentence uses them. On a pair like h088 (deviance 63.3 on 3 November), the "need both" sentence would go out without the caveat P3-5c's record carries. The local-mode dry run produced exactly this case: h088 misfit True, and no sentence.

### D9. One item cannot decide it: **flaw** (F3)

§4 commits two things. First, a named pair list and "whether one pair's label flip would change the verdict". Second, "This rests mostly on setting <S>" whenever one setting carries more than half of a present LR. §5 says the sentences are "used verbatim (`pair_sentence()`, `verdict()`)". Neither function, and nothing in `analyse()`, produces either line. `setting_shares` is computed but never read. The one-setting rule also repeats the design-review miss recorded by the P3-5c audit (`design_review_missed`, D9). On h221, two settings (XZ, XY) carried 69% of the rotation LR, and dropping both left 13.8 against a line of 13.0. That is the label most likely to decide a 2-of-3 pass. The pre-mortem names it, and in the worlds it is the one pair at 4 of 6.

### D11. The bet can both pass and fail: **holds**

The design's named worlds at the job's n: P(pass | W_both) = 1.0 (h221 4 of 6, h088 6 of 6, h268 6 of 6) and P(fail | W_neither) = 1.0. That still holds with the real 1,000-shot readout matrix (D3), and the committed file regenerates. Each outcome clears 10% in the world that bears on it, so the result is not known before the run. What it turns on is whether this job's terms are as large as 3 November's. Two consequences, recorded for the author and not counted as flaws:
- The pre-mortem ("halved shots push h221 and one other pair below") has P ≈ 0 at 3 November's sizes. A fail now needs the terms themselves to shrink, which is why F2 rewrites the fail sentence.
- The odds may be revised before data, keeping both numbers, if the author reads P(pass | W_both) = 1.0 as bearing on them.

The bootstrap null is fitted as carefully as the observed statistic (`dry6_bootcare.py`, h221, 12 null datasets at 2,000 shots). The bootstrap's fit (2 random starts plus warm starts) gives LRs within 0.04 of a 30-start fit, and the observed statistic's 10-start fit is within 0.10. That is immaterial against lines of 7–17.
