## In plain English

Before a new bet, the engine states its odds starting from this page: how often its own past bets came true. So far **9 of 37 scored bets came true (0.24, 95% interval [0.13, 0.40])**. The tables below split that record five ways: by program, by what decided the outcome (the referee), by how many dollars the test cost, by where the bet came from, and by how deep in its program's map it sat. Each row's interval is wide because each row holds few bets; a row with fewer than five bets says almost nothing.

**How an odds elicitation uses this page** (CONTEXT.md §3 rule 14; `operators/scout.md`, Step 4). A fresh context is handed this file and the bet's own description (its design, models, cost, pass rule, and which row it falls in on each table), and nothing about the bet's history: no earlier odds for it, no earlier bet on its direction or their outcomes, no human odds. It writes three things:

1. **prior**: the hit rate of all scored bets, as printed here (0.24), with this file's commit;
2. **adjustment**: a signed number added to the prior (the result, kept between 0.01 and 0.99, is the engine's odds);
3. **reason**: one line naming the rows below, or the feature of the design, that justify the adjustment.

The ledger stores all three (`odds_prior`, `odds_adjustment`, `odds_reason`), and `ledger/brier.md` scores the prior alone against the adjusted odds on every bet that carries them. If the adjusted odds score no better, the adjustments are not adding judgment, and that page says so.
