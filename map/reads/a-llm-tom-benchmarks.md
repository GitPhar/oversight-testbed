## Cluster summary

**What this literature has settled.** On canonical false-belief vignettes, frontier models from GPT-4 onward answer forced-choice belief questions at or above adult human level. Kosinski's GPT-4 solved 75% of 40 eight-scenario tasks; Strachan et al. found GPT-4 at ceiling and above humans on hinting, irony and strange stories; Street et al.'s GPT-4 matched adults on orders 2–6. It is equally settled that this success is brittle and shallow in a specific way. Small changes to the story (transparent containers, "in" becoming "on", trusted testimony, asking about the mover) flip answers (Ullman; Shapira et al.; Nickel et al. 2026). Templated benchmarks can be solved by lexical rules (Le et al.). Models that state a character's mental state correctly do not reliably *use* it. SimpleToM finds mental-state accuracy above 95% next to behaviour-prediction accuracy of 49.5–67% and judgment accuracy of 10–25% for GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-405B and Claude-3.5-Sonnet. FANToM finds that correct belief answers do not survive a requirement to be consistent across the question types that probe the same belief. Riemer et al. find accurate predictions of an opponent that the model then does not act on. Muchovej et al. (2026) find GPT-4o's inferred mental states inconsistent with its own action predictions. Each of these is a different measurement of a dissociation between an attribution the model can *state* and an attribution that drives its other answers. Two further findings recur. Answers depend heavily on elicitation format: yes/no versus "more likely" (Strachan), explicit "thinks" versus implicit "looks for" (Trott ×2), next-token versus question-answer prompting (Riemer: 96.7% versus 59.6% literal-ToM accuracy for Mixtral), choice versus free response (FANToM). And humans also fail several of the "trivial" perturbations (Strachan SI: transparent and preposition variants, N = 757).

**What it has not looked at, and what our instruments reach cheaply.** No paper I found elicits an *attributed* belief as a graded verbal confidence ("how sure is Sam that the bag holds chocolate, 0–100"). The graded measures that exist are token probabilities or sampling frequencies (Kosinski, Ullman, Trott, Street, Muchovej), a three-level knew/unsure/didn't-know code (Strachan), or are thresholded back to a binary. No one has applied the program-1 battery to attributed beliefs: question-order swaps, repeat-consistency with the earlier answer hidden versus shown, six-format one-latent tests, or planted answers credited to self versus other. The nearest manipulation is SimpleToM's "MS remind". It shows the model its own earlier mental-state answer, and behaviour and judgment accuracy then jump (Claude-3.5-Sonnet judgment 24.9 → 84.1). That is a consistency-as-memory effect in third-person ToM, observed but never decomposed: nobody hid that answer, planted a wrong one, or credited it to someone else. Nor has anyone varied the model's *own* credence in the proposition independently of the character's evidence, so egocentric or curse-of-knowledge leakage is known only qualitatively. Examples are FANToM's omniscient-view answers, SimpleToM's venomous-snake failure, and Ullman's mover who "thinks" the cat is still in the basket. Our five instruments reach all of these gaps with single-turn calls on public item sets. The item sets are SimpleToM (ODC-BY, 1,147 stories), Kosinski's 40 tasks (OSF), Strachan's battery and perturbations (OSF, CC-BY-NC), Trott's 192 passages (OSF), BigToM (MIT), FANToM (MIT, evaluation-only) and ExploreToM (CC-BY-NC). The cheapest high-value design turns each item's belief question into a 0–100 attributed credence and runs it through order, hidden/shown, planted-own/planted-other and six-format conditions. A second design adds an "own-prior" arm, using program-1 claims on which the model already has a stable rating, to measure how far the model's own credence pulls the credence it attributes to a character.

## Read records

### A1. Kosinski 2024, "Evaluating large language models in theory of mind tasks"
- **Citation:** M. Kosinski, *PNAS* 121(45): e2405460121 (2024). DOI 10.1073/pnas.2405460121. arXiv 2302.02083 (v1 4 Feb 2023 … v7 4 Nov 2024; title and id confirmed on arXiv). CC BY-NC-ND.
- **Read depth:** full main text (the PNAS version via the arXiv v7 PDF). SI appendix not read.
- **What was tested:** 20 unexpected-contents and 20 unexpected-transfer tasks, written by a hypothesis-blind assistant. Each task has one false-belief scenario, three true-belief controls (open container / correct label / informed protagonist; present protagonist / informed protagonist / no transfer), and reversed versions of all four: eight scenarios with two prompts each, 16 prompts per task. A task counts as solved only if all 16 are correct. The prompts are sentence completions ("Sam calls a friend to tell them that she has just found a bag full of"). The first word is scored, and the model is reset after each completion. A sentence-by-sentence analysis presents the story in one-sentence increments and estimates P(word) from 1,000 completions at temperature 1, giving 2,000 runs per step.
- **On whom:** 11 LLMs: GPT-1, GPT-2XL, six GPT-3 models, ChatGPT-3.5-turbo (March 2023), ChatGPT-4 (June 2023), BLOOM.
- **Result:** Older models 0%. GPT-3-davinci-002 5% [0, 10]. davinci-003 and GPT-3.5-turbo 20% [11, 29]. GPT-4 75% [66, 84], with 90% on unexpected contents and 60% on unexpected transfer (χ² = 8.07). Correlation between the two task types across models R = 0.98. Adding the true-belief controls cut davinci-003 from 60% to 20% and GPT-4 from 95% to 75%. In the sentence-by-sentence analysis GPT-4's P(protagonist believes "chocolate") rises once the false label is mentioned and goes to about 100% once Sam "doesn't look inside". In the open-container control, Sam's belief came out P(popcorn) = 88.5% versus 11.5% chocolate.
- **Limits (author):** failures may be artefacts of the scoring key ("valuable evidence" scored wrong) or of the model confabulating plot twists. Success may still rest on response strategies not yet identified. **Ours:** all-or-none scoring hides graded structure. The only "confidence" is a sampling frequency at T = 1, not an elicited credence. No human data were collected; the children's baseline is a meta-analysis. There is no own-knowledge manipulation.
- **Borrow:** the 40-task item set with controls and reversals (OSF osf.io/csdhb). The **sentence-by-sentence trajectory** is a ready-made design for graded attributed belief: we can ask for a 0–100 rating of Sam's belief after each increment and compare the curve with sampling frequency. The reversal control (swap label and contents) is a cheap check on content priors.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Author:* more protagonists, to find how many minds can be tracked. Use open-ended rather than Likert or multiple-choice formats, because human-study formats may cue "test-taking" behaviour. "Put words in the models' mouths", for example preambles in which the model self-reports a trait. *Ours:* the author explicitly discourages Likert formats, which is a testable claim. Our six-format test can ask whether a 0–100 attributed credence, a yes/no and a completion probability load on one latent. The "words in the mouth" suggestion is our planted-answer instrument. Planting "I think Sam believes popcorn" as the model's own earlier turn versus another reader's, then re-asking for the rating, gives the own-versus-other uptake for an *attributed* belief.
- **Transfer verdict:** **transfers.** Clean items with built-in controls, and a graded trajectory paradigm that maps directly onto our rating instrument.

### A2. Ullman 2023, "Large language models fail on trivial alterations to theory-of-mind tasks"
- **Citation:** T. D. Ullman, arXiv 2302.08399 (v5, 14 Mar 2023). Confirmed on arXiv.
- **Read depth:** full. The full HTML was processed by the fetch tool, and every completion probability was checked against the PDF text.
- **What was tested:** eight hand-made variants of two of Kosinski's v1 vignettes, scored by completion probabilities, on the GPT-3.5 used in Kosinski's v1 (the text says "the most recently available iteration of GPT-3.5"). Unexpected contents: 1A transparent bag, 1B Sam cannot read, 1C trusted testimony, 1D Sam filled and labelled the bag herself. Unexpected transfer: 2A transparent containers, 2B "in" → "on", 2C trusted communication, 2D ask about Mark, the mover.
- **On whom:** one model, single vignettes, no N or statistics.
- **Result (checked):** every variant fails. 1A first belief prompt P(chocolate) = 95%. 1C (friend says popcorn, Sam believes her) P(popcorn) = 13% versus P(chocolate) = 81%. 1D P(chocolate) = 87% on the first prompt. 2A John looks in the chest 90%. 2B "on the basket" 74%. 2C basket 94%. 2D: **Mark, who moved the cat himself, "thinks the cat is in the basket" at 99%** and "will look in the basket" at 54%. A misplaced double space changed a 1A result in an earlier version.
- **Limits (author):** hand-crafted, not a benchmark. A generator is deliberately withheld so that it cannot be "gobbled up". **Ours:** one model, no repetition, no human baseline (Strachan later showed humans also fail 2A and 2B). The probabilities are next-token, not elicited.
- **Borrow:** the perturbation taxonomy, which Shapira, Strachan and Nickel later systematised. The 2D "mover" case is a ready-made own-knowledge-leakage probe: the character who *knows* is assigned the script's false belief.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Author:* a skeptical zero-hypothesis; weigh outlying failures over average success; integrate computational ToM models with LLMs. *Ours:* the double-space sensitivity and the 1C result, where testimony is ignored, are exactly the "reading of text" signature program 1 found. A graded version would ask for a 0–100 credence for Sam/John/Mark per variant, then plant the model's own earlier rating versus another reader's. If attributed credence behaves like self-credence, planted-own uptake should be high and planted-other near zero. Variant 2D, run against the mover's own first-person line ("Mark: I know the cat is in the box"), tests whether a first-person statement in the transcript is weighted like the model's own planted answer.
- **Transfer verdict:** **partly.** Good probes but too few items. Use them as seeds inside a larger set (Nickel 2026 or Shapira's Adv-CSFB).

### A3. Shapira et al. 2024, "Clever Hans or neural theory of mind? Stress testing social reasoning in large language models"
- **Citation:** N. Shapira, M. Levy, S. H. Alavi, X. Zhou, Y. Choi, Y. Goldberg, M. Sap, V. Shwartz. EACL 2024. arXiv 2305.14763 (confirmed; the text read was v1, 24 May 2023).
- **Read depth:** partial. The full HTML was summarised by the fetch tool, and results passages were spot-checked in the PDF text. Appendices only via the summary.
- **What was tested:** six existing datasets (Triangle COPA 100, SocialIQa sample 400, ToMi sample 400, Epistemic Reasoning 2,000, FauxPas-EAI 176 questions over 44 stories) plus two new ones. **ToMi′** has 30 ToMi stories (180 questions) rewritten as sentence completions. **Adv-CSFB** has 183 questions over 40 stories built from Kosinski's tasks plus Ullman-style adversarial variants: 110 unexpected-contents and 73 unexpected-transfer. Three probing methods were compared: LM-probing (answer log probability), MC-probing and CoT-probing.
- **On whom:** 15 LLMs (Flan-T5 family, Flan-UL2, davinci-002/-003, gpt-3.5-turbo-0301, gpt-4-0314, five Jurassic-2 models). Temperature 0.
- **Result (from the summary; the key ones checked):** probing method shifts scores substantially (SocialIQa davinci-003: LM 55 / MC 60 / CoT 68). On the original Kosinski tasks the models score 87 (davinci-003), 65 (gpt-3.5) and 87 (gpt-4); on ToMi′ "all questions" they score 10, 27 and 20. On Adv-CSFB, GPT-4 does well on plain false belief (97.5%), but "transparent access" and "in→on" fall to 0% for newer models. FauxPas-EAI GPT-4 27%, below the majority baseline. The heuristics named are reporting bias, recency, over-cooperativeness (treating every detail as relevant) and "centering" (the main character is favoured).
- **Limits (authors):** small datasets, ambiguous items, refusals, format non-compliance. **Ours:** all forced choice; no confidence; no human baseline on Adv-CSFB.
- **Borrow:** Adv-CSFB and ToMi′ (github.com/salavi/Clever_Hans_or_N-ToM; public, no data licence named). The **LM / MC / CoT probing contrast** is a format manipulation we can add to our six.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* pair hard and easy questions and score them jointly, or report the minimum across splits; build benchmarks native to LLMs; be cautious that CoT may leak task structure. *Ours:* "over-cooperativeness" is the pragmatic-artefact hypothesis (see Hu et al.). It predicts that the question text itself constructs the attribution, which our not-asked versus asked transcript copies can measure. "Centering", meaning better answers for the protagonist, gives a self-like/other-like axis inside a story. Rate the main character's and a side character's beliefs on the same evidence and test whether order effects and planted-answer uptake differ.
- **Transfer verdict:** **partly.** The item sets and the probing-method contrast transfer; the forced-choice analyses do not.

### A4. Gandhi, Fränken, Gerstenberg & Goodman 2023, BigToM, "Understanding social reasoning in language models with language models"
- **Citation:** NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks. arXiv 2306.15448 (v2, 4 Dec 2023; confirmed).
- **Read depth:** partial. The fetch tool summarised the first 100k of 109k characters (main text and most appendices). I read the results passages on forward belief, forward action and backward belief directly in the PDF text.
- **What was tested:** GPT-4-populated causal templates (context, desire, percept, belief, causal event, action), 200 templates × 25 conditions = 5,000 items. The main analyses cover Forward Belief, Forward Action and Backward Belief (infer belief from an observed action), each in true-belief and false-belief versions, with and without an explicitly stated initial belief. A false-belief item scores only if its true-belief twin is also correct.
- **On whom:** text-davinci-003, gpt-3.5-turbo, gpt-4-0314, claude-v1.3, claude-2, llama-65b. 0-shot, 0-shot CoT, 1-shot, 1-shot CoT. Humans (crowdworkers) for the backward condition and for quality ratings.
- **Result:** GPT-4 0-shot TB∧FB scores: Forward Belief 0.97/0.90, Forward Action 0.79/0.90, Backward Belief 0.40/0.40 (without/with initial belief). Humans on backward belief: TB 82%, FB 72%. Most models are "far below chance" on backward belief, "indicating that they reliably attribute the wrong belief". Stated initial beliefs often *hurt*: models anchor on them. GPT-4 scored 0.861 on GPT-4-generated items and 0.858 on Claude-2-generated items.
- **Limits (authors):** model-written items (about 3% commonsense errors), low syntactic diversity, percept–belief–action only. **Ours:** binary; explicit mentalising verbs ("Noor sees", "does not notice") hand the answer over (SimpleToM's critique).
- **Borrow:** the causal-template generator and the 5,000 items (github.com/cicl-stanford/procedural-evals-tom, `data/bigtom`, MIT). The **with/without stated initial belief** factor is an in-story analogue of a planted answer: a stated belief the model anchors on.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* extend causal templates to other domains; build interactive benchmarks. *Ours:* the anchoring on a stated initial belief is the key handle. Convert Forward Belief items to 0–100 credence and vary who states the initial belief: the narrator, the character in first person ("I think the coffee is hot"), the model's own earlier turn, or another reader's turn. If attributed belief is a reading of text, anchoring should depend on where the sentence sits in the transcript more than on whose mind it reports. The "reliably wrong" backward-belief pattern suggests a systematic leak from the world state into the attributed belief; graded ratings would show whether it is a shift or a flip.
- **Transfer verdict:** **transfers.** Large, permissively licensed, factorial items with a built-in anchoring manipulation.

### A5. Kim et al. 2023, FANToM
- **Citation:** H. Kim, M. Sclar, X. Zhou, R. Le Bras, G. Kim, Y. Choi, M. Sap, "FANToM: A benchmark for stress-testing machine theory of mind in interactions". EMNLP 2023. arXiv 2310.15421 (v3; confirmed).
- **Read depth:** partial. Sections 2–4 (construction, question types, metrics, results text) read directly in the PDF; the rest via the fetch summary. Table 9 (full results) not parsed: figure and table numbers in the PDF text were too garbled to quote beyond those below.
- **What was tested:** 256 multi-party small-talk conversations in which characters leave and rejoin (generated by davinci-003, validated on MTurk). For each piece of information a character missed: FactQ, BeliefQ[Dist.] (free response, scored by whether the answer is closer in SentenceBERT space to the **person-centric** or the **omniscient-view** belief), BeliefQ[Choice], AnswerabilityQ[List / Y/N] and InfoAccessQ[List / Y/N]. Counts: 1,415 belief questions, 703 FactQs, 2,689 Y/N questions. "All*" requires all six types correct for the same information.
- **On whom:** 13 models (GPT-4 0613 and 0314, ChatGPT 0613, davinci-003, curie-001, Flan-T5, Flan-UL2, Falcon, Mistral, Zephyr, Llama-2-70B-chat); a fine-tuned Flan-T5-XL; student volunteers (32 sets).
- **Result:** Human "All" (excluding the free-response type) 87.5. In Table 1 (short context, CoT), GPT-4 0613 (June) + CoT scores 26.6 on "All question types"; fine-tuned Flan-T5-XL scores 53.7. Choice-format belief answers far exceed the list-type answerability and info-access questions that are their prerequisites ("illusory ToM"). Free response is worse than choice. Full conversations are worse than short ones. Models with better FactQ scores can have *worse* BeliefQ[Dist.] scores: "models … reproduce similar responses to FACT Q for BELIEF Q [DIST.]". In other words, the omniscient answer leaks into the character's belief. CoT mainly reduces false positives (including unaware characters).
- **Limits (authors):** small talk among strangers only; LLM-generated conversations. **Ours:** consistency is measured *across question types*, not across repeats or orders; no graded belief.
- **Borrow:** the **omniscient-versus-person-centric distance score** is a ready-made, continuous curse-of-knowledge measure for free-text attributions. The All* idea is a cross-format consistency score. Data: github.com/skywalker023/fantom (MIT; "evaluation only").
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* more scenario and relationship types, multimodal settings, belief graphs. *Ours:* FANToM's six question types are effectively six elicitation formats for one attributed belief, so our one-latent versus format-specific likelihood test applies directly. It asks whether a single "Kailey-knows" latent explains the binary, list and choice answers, or whether each needs its own parameter, as Haiku's self-credences did. The leakage effect can be graded: ask for a 0–100 rating that Kailey believes the full-fact answer, and test whether the rating rises with the model's own FactQ confidence (an own-to-other leakage slope).
- **Transfer verdict:** **transfers.** A multi-format, leakage-sensitive design, though its long contexts make it more expensive than the vignette sets.

### A6. Le, Boureau & Nickel 2019, ToMi, "Revisiting the evaluation of theory of mind through question answering"
- **Citation:** EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019, pp. 5872–5877. ACL Anthology D19-1598 (no arXiv id found).
- **Read depth:** full (6 pages).
- **What was tested:** shows that ToM-bAbI is solved perfectly by a 4-rule lexical algorithm (100% on ToM-easy and ToM). Builds ToMi with randomised distractors, shuffled event order, random queried agent, and **all six questions asked of every story** (reality, memory, first-order A and B, second-order A and B), scored jointly.
- **On whom:** MemNN, EntNet, RelNet; not LLMs.
- **Result:** joint accuracy on ToMi: rules 36.5, MemNN 44.3, EntNet 66.8, RelNet 57.4. On false-belief pairs, first-order accuracy is 12.62 / 10.40 / 54.95 (MemNN/RelNet/EntNet).
- **Limits (authors):** synthetic, simple language. **Ours:** pre-LLM. Known label ambiguities (SymbolicToM documents some).
- **Borrow:** the principle that every story gets every question, scored jointly. Generator code: github.com/facebookresearch/ToMi (CC BY-NC 4.0).
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Ours:* asking reality and belief questions about the same story is where an **order effect** lives. Ask the reality question first (the model states its own view of the world), then the belief question, and compare with the reverse order and with a not-asked copy. That is program 1's order design with the "own view" made explicit.
- **Transfer verdict:** **partly.** A design principle and a generator; the items are stale and contaminated.

### A7. Sclar, Kumar, West, Suhr, Choi & Tsvetkov 2023, SymbolicToM, "Minding language models' (lack of) theory of mind: a plug-and-play multi-character belief tracker"
- **Citation:** ACL 2023. arXiv 2306.00924 (confirmed).
- **Read depth:** partial ("briefly", as briefed). Abstract, method (Algorithms 1–3), results summaries, robustness sets and limitations read directly; per-model tables not parsed.
- **What was tested:** an inference-time method that builds per-character belief graphs (world graph plus local graphs updated only for **witnesses**). It then answers "Where does Bob think the celery is?" by retrieving the sentences in Bob's graph and asking the LLM the **simplified, perspective-free question** ("Where is the celery?") over that filtered story.
- **On whom:** GPT-3 family, GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Flan-T5, LLaMA-7B/13B, Macaw; supervised baselines. ToMi, story-structure variants (D1–D3) and ParaphrasedToMi.
- **Result:** large zero-shot gains: +62 points on first-order false belief for Flan-T5-XL and +78 on second-order false belief for GPT-3.5. Robust to structure changes where supervised models overfit.
- **Limits (authors):** assumes chronological narration; inherits NLI and OpenIE errors. **Ours:** an engineered pipeline, not a test of the bare model.
- **Borrow:** the **perspective-filtering transformation** is a transcript edit. It turns a third-person belief question into a first-person or world question over the character's own transcript.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Ours:* this is the most direct handle on the seed question. If belief attribution is "the same operation run on a transcript", then the model's 0–100 credence that *Bob believes P* given the full story should match its credence that *P* given only Bob's filtered transcript, item by item. Both should then show the same order and format signatures. A mismatch localises a separate third-person operation. The comparison costs two calls per item.
- **Transfer verdict:** **transfers** as a manipulation; the method itself is not needed.

### A8. Strachan et al. 2024, "Testing theory of mind in large language models and humans"
- **Citation:** J. W. A. Strachan, D. Albergo, G. Borghini, O. Pansardi, E. Scaliti, S. Gupta, K. Saxena, A. Rufo, S. Panzeri, G. Manzi, M. S. A. Graziano, C. Becchio. *Nature Human Behaviour* 8, 1285–1295 (2024). DOI 10.1038/s41562-024-01882-z. Preprint arXiv 2305.15068.
- **Read depth:** full main text and methods (open-access PDF). Supplementary sections 3 (item position) and 4 (false-belief perturbations) read directly; the other SI sections were skimmed.
- **What was tested:** a battery (false belief, irony, faux pas, hinting, strange stories) with original and novel items. Each test was delivered in one chat session, with 15 sessions per model. Follow-ups: (i) a **faux-pas likelihood** reframing ("Is it more likely that X knew or didn't know…?"); (ii) a **belief-likelihood test** with faux-pas / neutral / knowledge-implied variants of 6 stories, coded −1 / 0 / +1, one item per chat; (iii) item-position analysis and a randomised-order replication (SI 3); (iv) Ullman-style false-belief perturbations in humans and LLMs (SI 4).
- **On whom:** GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 (ChatGPT web interface, April–May 2023 and Oct–Nov 2023), LLaMA2-70B-Chat (plus 7B and 13B in the SI). Humans N = 1,907 in total (250 battery, 900 belief-likelihood, 757 perturbations).
- **Result:** GPT-4 was above humans on irony, hinting and strange stories and at ceiling on false belief, but below humans on faux pas. Its faux-pas errors were almost all "not enough information" (only 2 of 349 said "knew"). Under the likelihood framing GPT-4 is perfect. The authors call this "hyperconservatism", not failed inference. Belief-likelihood test: GPT-4 discriminates the variants like humans (faux pas vs neutral χ² = 109; knowledge-implied vs neutral χ² = 18.1) and reports "unsure" on 42 of 90 neutral responses. LLaMA2-70B does not separate neutral from knowledge-implied (χ² = 1.80, P = 0.18), so its faux-pas "win" is an ignorance bias. **Order:** GPT-3.5 shows item-position effects within sessions; in the randomised replication the effect remains only for irony (more errors early). GPT-4 and LLaMA2 show no position effects. **Perturbations:** GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 fail transparent, preposition and testimony variants. **Humans also fail transparent and preposition** (they say the character looks where she left the object). LLaMA2 passes the mover variant.
- **Limits (authors):** closed, evolving models; perturbations confound ToM with physical reasoning. **Ours:** the knew/unsure/didn't-know code is the closest thing to a graded attributed belief in the literature, but it is three-level and dominated by refusal-like "unsure" answers. There is no repeat-consistency or planted-answer test.
- **Borrow:** all items, responses and codes (OSF osf.io/fwj6v, CC-BY-NC 4.0). The **yes/no versus "more likely"** contrast is one of our formats with a known large effect. The belief-likelihood triplets are an ideal graded-attribution item set: same story, utterance varies the evidence that the speaker knew. The authors' own framing of the false-belief challenge is a hypothesis to test. They say that in humans success "requires inhibiting one's own belief about reality", and that if a machine "does not have its own self-perspective … tracking the belief of a character in a story does not pose the same challenge".
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* examine non-human decision behaviour in real-time second-person interaction; use open models for targeted probing. *Ours:* (1) Run the belief-likelihood triplets as 0–100 "how likely is it that Amanda knew …" across our six formats. GPT-4's "unsure" mass predicts strong format dependence, and the one-latent test would quantify it. (2) Their claim that the model has no own perspective to inhibit is directly testable. Give the model a perspective by planting its own earlier rating of the world fact (in program-1 fashion) and see whether attributed knowledge shifts with it. If there is no self to inhibit, planted-own and planted-other should behave alike. (3) SI 3 shows within-session position effects even for these tasks. Our hidden/shown earlier-answer edits would separate position effects from memory effects.
- **Transfer verdict:** **transfers.** It has human baselines, open items, a format manipulation with a known effect, and an explicit hypothesis about the absence of a self-perspective.
- **Successors or replies:** I found no matters-arising or reply by the same group (2024–2026). Hu, Sosa & Ullman (A12) take up the human-perturbation result. Riemer et al. (A11) and many 2025–2026 benchmark papers cite it. Search: Nature Human Behaviour site plus arXiv author queries, 2026-11-04.

### A9. Street et al. 2024, "LLMs achieve adult human performance on higher-order theory of mind tasks"
- **Citation:** W. Street, J. O. Siy, G. Keeling, A. Baranes, B. Barnett, M. McKibben, T. Kanyere, A. Lentz, B. Agüera y Arcas, R. I. M. Dunbar. arXiv 2405.18870 (v2, 31 May 2024; confirmed).
- **Read depth:** full main text; appendix not read.
- **What was tested:** MoToMQA: 7 handwritten stories of about 200 words, each with 20 true/false statements (10 ToM at orders 2–6, true and false; 10 factual at matched "levels"). Two prompt conditions and **two response-order conditions ("true or false?" versus "false or true?")** to test anchoring. LLMs were scored by **log probabilities** of {True, true, TRUE} versus {False, false, FALSE}, normalised to P(true) and then thresholded at 0.5.
- **On whom:** LaMDA, PaLM, Flan-PaLM, GPT-3.5 Turbo Instruct, GPT-4. Humans: 29,259 UK adults, one statement each.

- **Limits (authors):** 140 statements; English only; cognitive and affective states not separated. **Ours:** a graded P(true) was computed and then discarded by thresholding. The benchmark is "available upon request" only.
- **Borrow:** the response-option-order manipulation and the logprob-to-P(true) pipeline (for the open-weight probe arm). Under the IMT naming convention **"1st order" is the subject's own mental state**, so the order ladder already contains a self rung that no one has tested alongside the others.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* cultural and language breadth, beyond order 6, multimodal. *Ours:* the anchoring null for GPT-4 is on *response-option* order, not *question* order, and on thresholded answers. Our graded design asks whether the attributed credence for an order-n statement shifts when the order-(n−1) statement is rated first (question-order effect), and whether that shift survives hiding the earlier answer. Adding an explicit order-1 rung ("How confident are *you* that …") alongside orders 2–6 on the same story gives a within-item self-versus-other comparison.
- **Transfer verdict:** **partly.** The design ideas transfer; the items are gated.

## Cross-cutting answers to the brief's questions

1. **Were attributed beliefs ever elicited as graded confidences?** Not as verbal or structured confidences. The graded measures in this literature are: sampling frequency over 1,000 completions (Kosinski); single-token completion probabilities (Ullman); log-odds over two locations (Trott ×2); logprob P(true), thresholded (Street); next-token distributions over actions and beliefs (Muchovej); a three-level knew/unsure/didn't-know code (Strachan belief-likelihood test); and the "more likely" reframing (Strachan). A 0–100 rating of a character's belief, with structured output, appears to be unstudied.
2. **Order effects, format dependence, consistency-as-memory on attributed beliefs?**
   - *Order:* within-session item position (Strachan SI: GPT-3.5 irony only, after randomisation); response-option order (Street: PaLM 73.2% vs 47.1% "true", GPT-3.5 43.9% vs 22.9%, GPT-4 null); story-internal first mention (Trott β = 1.32); sentence-by-sentence trajectories (Kosinski, Nickel). *No one swapped the order of the belief question and the reality question with graded ratings.*
   - *Format:* large effects everywhere. Strachan yes/no versus likelihood (GPT-4 faux pas from failing to perfect); Riemer LM/QA/CoT (literal ToM 96.7 / 59.6 / 33.5); Trott explicit/implicit cue; FANToM choice/free/list; Shapira LM/MC/CoT. *No one has tested whether formats load on one latent attributed belief.*
   - *Consistency as memory:* SimpleToM's MS remind (shown own answer boosts applied ToM by up to 59 points) and Riemer's Social prompting (shown own prediction does *not* fix action) are the only manipulations of a remembered attribution, and they point in opposite directions. Cross-question consistency is low (FANToM All*, SimpleToM All Correct, Muchovej forward/inverse). *No one hid, falsified or reassigned the earlier answer.*
3. **Perturbations that dissociate the model's own knowledge from a character's.** Qualitative or binary only: Ullman 2D (the knowing mover is assigned the false belief at 99%); FANToM BeliefQ[Dist.] (free-text answers drift toward the omniscient view, worse in models with better FactQ); SimpleToM behaviour and judgment (the model reasons from the hidden fact, for example the snake); BigToM backward belief (reliably wrong); Strachan's hypothesis that LLMs have "no self-perspective" to inhibit. *No study varied the model's own prior credence in the proposition independently of the character's evidence*, the egocentric-anchoring design of the human curse-of-knowledge literature.

## Bearing on whether first- and third-person attributions share a mechanism

No paper tests this directly for beliefs. Five findings bear on it:

- SymbolicToM's gains come from turning "where does Bob think X is?" into "where is X?" over Bob's filtered transcript. A third-person question can be replaced by a first-person or world question on an edited transcript.
- ExploreToM's shared-belief questions are *harder* than divergent-belief ones. The difficulty is reading the text's state, not perspective-taking.
- SimpleToM's MS remind shows that a shown earlier attribution drives later answers, the program-1 consistency-as-memory signature, though in the third person and never decomposed.
- Kim et al. 2026 find self-attributions of *mentality* move like attributions to similar others (chatbots) and dissociate from ToM accuracy.
- Strachan et al. hypothesise the model has no own perspective that competes with the character's.

Together these are consistent with attribution to self and to others being one transcript-reading operation. None rules out a separate one. The decisive cheap tests are A7's filtered-transcript equivalence and A10's full transcript-editing set on graded attributed credences.
