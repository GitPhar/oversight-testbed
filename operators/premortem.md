# The pre-mortem: standing instructions

Before each run, the session that pre-registers the bet writes one sentence: **"If this fails, the most likely reason is …"**. After the run, the auditor scores it. Over many bets, the ledger can then say whether the engine knows *how* its tests go wrong, not only *how often*. This file is the whole procedure. It follows ARCHITECTURE.md (the `Bet.premortem` field and steps 3 and 5 of the loop) and proposal.md Q1.

## In plain English

The odds say how likely a test is to work. They say nothing about why it might not. A researcher with good judgment can usually name the weak point before starting: too few usable items, the model refusing the format, a ceiling effect, a rival explanation the design cannot rule out. If the engine's one-sentence guesses keep naming the real cause of failure, its judgment about designs is worth something. If they never do, its designs fail for reasons it cannot see, and that is worth knowing before anyone trusts it to run unattended.

The pre-mortem costs one sentence and no money.

## Writing it (in the pre-registration, before the run)

- **One sentence**, at most 35 words, beginning exactly "If this fails, the most likely reason is". It goes in the pre-registration's §0 table (`operators/prereg-template.md`) and is committed with it. It is never revised after that commit. A deviation row cannot change it.
- **Name something checkable afterwards.** "Something unexpected" and "noise" are not pre-mortems. Name the mechanism: a floor or ceiling, a parse-failure rate, a refusal, too few items passing a filter, an artefact class from the auditor's checklist (B5–B12), a confound, or the effect simply not being there.
- **Tag its kind**, so that the lazy answer is visible:
  - `world`: the effect is not there. The hypothesis is false and the design is fine.
  - `design`: the design cannot see the effect even if it is there (power, floor, ceiling, a weak manipulation, the wrong statistic).
  - `execution`: the run will not complete as pre-registered (refusals, parse failures, the budget cap, model or format behaviour).
  - `artefact`: the test will pass, or fail, for a reason other than the hypothesis (pooling, ties, leakage, a few items carrying it, a confound).
- **Saying `world` is allowed** when it is honestly the most likely reason. It is scored like any other kind but reported separately, because a pre-mortem that always says `world` names the obvious and shows nothing about the design.
- **Sean may add his own, optionally,** on the human-odds form (`map/human-odds-template.md`), written before he sees the engine's. It is scored the same way, in its own column.

## Scoring it (by the auditor, after the run)

The auditor fills three header fields in `ledger/audits/<bet-id>.md` (see `operators/auditor.md`), after the checklist, so that the checklist evidence decides it:

- `failed`: `true` if the post-audit verdict is anything other than `pass` or `pass-unreplicated`, **or** the run did not complete as pre-registered (cap reached, refusal, `untestable`). Otherwise `false`.
- `failure_reason`: one line in the auditor's words, with its kind (`world`, `design`, `execution` or `artefact`), stated before the auditor looks at the pre-mortem. Write it first, then read the pre-mortem.
- `premortem_score`:
  - `named`: the bet failed, and the pre-mortem names the main reason the auditor found (the same mechanism, not merely the same kind).
  - `kind_only`: the bet failed for a reason of the same kind as the pre-mortem, but by a different mechanism.
  - `other`: the bet failed for a reason the pre-mortem does not name.
  - `not_failed`: the bet passed. The auditor also notes `risk_seen: true|false`, meaning the named risk showed up anyway without sinking the test.
  - `unclear`: the record cannot say why it failed. That is itself a finding about the write-up.

The ledger session copies these into the bet's line in `ledger/bets.jsonl`. Founding-week bets have `premortem: null` with a note, because no pre-mortems were written then. That is the honest entry, not a gap to fill from memory.

## What is published

`ledger/scores.md` (generated by `python3 ledger/scores.py`) reports the following.

- **Pre-mortem hit rate:** `named` ÷ failed bets with a pre-mortem, with a Wilson interval. Also reported: `named` + `kind_only`.
- **By kind**, the stated kind against the kind of the actual failure, as a small table.
- **The lazy baseline:** the share of failures whose actual reason was `world`. A pre-mortem that always said "the effect isn't there" would score this hit rate. The engine's pre-mortems add something only if their hit rate beats it.
- **The human column**, where Sean wrote one.

This is a secondary measure under Q1, not a pre-registered hypothesis. It enters the paper as descriptive until it has at least 15 failed bets with pre-mortems.
