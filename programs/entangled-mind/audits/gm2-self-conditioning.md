## Plain-English summary of the audit
The headline holds. I recomputed it independently and got R = 1.91 [0.92, 4.90]. It stays above the 0.5 bar whichever single item is dropped (lowest lower bound 0.86) and under other bootstrap seeds, so the pre-registered "question-driven" verdict stands. Two readings go further than the data. First, the order effect that survives redaction is mostly a *different* order effect. Shown and redacted order profiles each reproduce well across split halves, but they correlate only 0.41 with each other (0.44 after correcting for noise). So "the question does the work" holds only in the sense that some order dependence survives, not in the sense that the shown effect is the question's. Second, "the consistency was memory, not a stable judgment" is not supported. Fresh first ratings in independent conversations agree within 5 points 74% of the time. The low redacted agreement (23%) comes from the redacted chain moving the final rating by a systematic per-item shift, not from an unstable judgment. "Follows it ... and then some" (b_MF 1.27) rests on one item, h238.

## Holes found
1. **The redacted order effect is mostly a different effect, not the shown effect with the record removed.** Per item, the four order means are highly reproducible within each condition (split-half r: shown 0.80, redacted 0.93 on 10 items). Shown and redacted profiles correlate only 0.41 (disattenuated 0.44 [0.07, 0.75]). The placeholder also changes what the model writes. Final M = 15 occurs in 26% of redacted O-chain finals against 8% shown, and 5% of redacted finals sit more than 50 points from the item median against 1.3% shown (for example h191 O1 and h112 O2, where 9 of 10 finals are 0). So R ≥ 0.5 shows that order dependence survives redaction. It does not show that the shown order effect "is" the question's. Consequence: the "question-driven" label is true by the rule, but any reading that the question carries the observed (shown) order effect is unsupported. This hole is more serious at 40 items (see `audits/gm2b-self-conditioning-40.md`).
2. **The "memory, not stability" reading ignores a third explanation.** Pre-registered rule 5 treats low redacted agreement as memory rather than a stable judgment. But fresh first ratings agree 74% across independent conversations. The redacted M₂ is displaced systematically: per item, mean \|shift\| is 23 points against a within-item SD of 20. So the drop from 81% to 23% mixes copying the visible answer with the redacted chain moving the verdict, which is the very effect claim 1 measures. Consequence: "the record raises repeat agreement" holds. "The consistency was not a stable judgment" does not.
3. **b_MF is a ratio with small, clipped denominators, and one item dominates it.** The planted M gaps range from 16 to 30. h238 contributes 4.56 (finals 19 → 97 for a 17-point plant; the model's own S4 rating collapses to 3–6 under planted-high). The median per item is 1.00. Without h238 the value is 0.90 [0.57, 1.22], so "and then some" disappears.
4. **"Next-rating pull" double-counts the final rating.** Its positions include the final M of every chain, which the final-M shift (10.4) reports separately. On sub-question ratings alone the pull is 2.9 [1.5, 4.3].
5. **The planted values are not always ±15 around the model's own rating.** They were fixed from experiment-5 archives (a different context), and clipping applies. This is minor for the conclusions, but the plain-English description is loose.
