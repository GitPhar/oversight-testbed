## Plain-English summary of the audit
The numbers reproduce exactly. The primary verdict ("question-driven" on all 40 items, R = 1.23 [0.75, 2.02]) is robust: no single dropped item pushes the CI below 0.5. The only independent replication, the 30 new items, passes the frozen rule by a hair (lower bound 0.512 against 0.5). Dropping any one of 7 of those 30 items turns it into "leaning". The headline sentence "the question carries roughly all of the order effect, and the record adds little to it" is wrong. Shown and redacted produce order effects of similar *size* but largely different *pattern*: each reproduces within its own condition (split-half r ≈ 0.9), yet they correlate only 0.32 with each other. So the record strongly changes which orders move the verdict. The placeholder check (equal within-order SD) shows only that the placeholder adds no noise. It does not show that the redacted order effect is free of placeholder artefacts: the placeholder visibly shifts the model's ratings toward stereotyped values. The "memory, not stability" reading is overstated, as in D37: fresh first ratings agree 77% of the time.

## Holes found
1. **Equal size is not the same effect.** The text infers from R ≈ 1 that the question "carries roughly all" of the order effect. The per-item order-mean profiles are reliable in both conditions (split-half r 0.90 and 0.91 on 40 items; 0.93 and 0.90 on the 30 new). If the question alone produced the shown order effect, the redacted profile would match the shown one. It does not: r = 0.32, disattenuated 0.35 [0.09, 0.59]. Hiding the answers leaves an order effect of the same size but changes which orders move the verdict and in which direction. Consequence: R measures only magnitudes. The decomposition claim "question ≈ all, record ≈ nothing" is unsupported, and the data point the other way, because the record shapes the effect.
2. **The only independent replication is marginal.** The 30 new items give a lower bound of 0.512 against a bar of 0.5. 7 of 30 single-item drops flip the label to "leaning" (minimum 0.45, dropping h048). The primary pooled set passes robustly, but it contains the 10 items that motivated the run, h191 included. Reports such as "It holds on the 30 new items alone" should add "only just".
3. **The placeholder check cannot clear the placeholder.** Within-order SD tests only for added noise. The placeholder changes the model's outputs systematically: stereotyped 15/85 ratings, more 0s, and idiosyncratic order-specific collapses (for example h112 O2, where 9 of 10 finals are 0 under redaction against 25–55 shown; h191 O1, 85–97 against 3). Those are exactly the order-specific shifts that would raise OE_redacted. Consequence: "not an artefact of it" and "Not the placeholder" are unsupported.
4. **"Memory, not stability" again confounds memory with displacement.** The case is the same as D37 (see `audits/gm2-self-conditioning.md`, hole 2). Fresh first ratings agree 77%. Redacted M₂ is moved systematically by the redacted chain.
5. **"Spread across items" is weaker than stated.** The 82% includes 13 items whose shown OE is below 2 points. The OE (a root-mean variance component) is dominated by a few items: the top 3 carry 60% of the summed redacted variance component.
