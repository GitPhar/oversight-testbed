## Plain-English summary of the audit
The headline survives everything I tried. An independent refit gives mean ΔLL +19.6 [+16.1, +22.9] with 87% of claims positive. A version that gives every format a fully flexible monotone link still has the format-specific model winning (+17.7 [+15.5, +19.8], 91%). In simulation, data generated from one credence do *not* make the format-specific model win (ΔLL ≈ −5), even with small claim × format deviations. So the result is not a trivial win for the model with more parameters, and "no single credence explains all six formats" stands. The problems are descriptive. The "20–34%" (and "21–35%") figures are (1 − Kendall τ_b)/2, not the share of statement pairs put in opposite orders: the actual share is 12–25% of pairs (20–30% of the pairs both formats separate). Kendall's W is slightly mis-computed because ties are broken arbitrarily. The by-format ΔLL and slope table reflects an under-converged fit. "Disagreement grows with the formats compared" has no analysis behind it. Mechanism wording ("put together by the question format", "not reading a stored number", "need six separate credences") outruns a behavioural non-invariance test, which the follow-up (D46) shows still leaves a shared component of about 40%.

## Holes found
1. **The pair-reversal figures are a tie-adjusted index, reported as a share of pairs.** `discordant_vs_F1 = (1 − τ_b)/2` (code comment: "ignoring ties' correction"). With binary formats tied on 26–37% of pairs, this index is not "the share of statement pairs put in opposite orders". The real share is 12–25% of all pairs (20–30% of pairs both formats separate). Both "20–34%" (full data, record, v3) and "21–35%" (F1-even vs Fx-odd, README, morning brief, NC) are the same index on different data. Neither is a count of reversed pairs. The cross-versus-noise contrast survives, and becomes stronger, on the proper count.
2. **The archived L1 fit is not at its optimum, so the per-format table is optimiser-dependent.** The joint optimum has a training objective at least 9.5 log-likelihood units higher. F6's slope is 4.81, not 3.23, and F6's share of ΔLL falls from +3.5 to +0.06 while F1's rises from +3.7 to +7.0. The headline ΔLL moves only from 19.36 to 19.63. `reproduce.py` reproduces the archived (under-converged) numbers byte-for-byte, so it cannot catch this.
3. **Kendall's W is computed with arbitrary tie-breaking.** argsort(argsort(...)) gives tied claims distinct ranks in index order. Midrank W is 0.57 and tie-corrected W is 0.62, not 0.56. The cross-model comparison in the NC paper (W 0.54 / 0.71 / 0.86) presumably uses the same code, so the trend may survive, but the values are biased low where ties are common (binary formats, weaker models).
4. **Mechanism wording outruns the test.** L6 beating L1 on held-out repeats shows that one latent plus monotone links does not reproduce every format to within repeat noise. It does not show that the format "constructs" the answer or that nothing is stored: about 40% of each format's reproducible variance is predictable from the others (D46).
5. **"Disagreement grows with the formats compared"** is unsupported. No analysis varies the number of formats.
6. **The parameter-count question raised by the lead auditor is answered in the authors' favour.** Held-out repeats do penalise L6's 960 latents. Simulated one-credence data give ΔLL ≈ −5, and a fully flexible monotone link does not close the gap. "Any model with a per-format fixed response mapping" would *not* win as L6 does. A per-claim × format deviation is what wins. The caveat at results:37 ("any reproducible ... deviation favours the separate-belief model") understates the test.

## Does the verdict stand?
Pre-registered verdict: **no single credence** (more than half of claims positive and the CI above 0). **STANDS.** It holds under an independent refit (+19.6 [+16.1, +22.9], 87%), under fully flexible monotone links (+17.7 [+15.5, +19.8], 91%), and LOO-item. It is not produced by parameter count, since simulated one-credence data favour L1. The descriptive statistics (pair reversals, W, the by-format table) and the mechanism wording need correcting. The scope is Haiku, one context per claim.
