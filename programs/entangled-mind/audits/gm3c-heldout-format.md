## Plain-English summary of the audit
The numbers are about right. An independent refit gives mean Q = 0.398 and mean cross-format R² 0.371 [0.284, 0.470]. Two link-free checks, a cross-validated linear prediction from the other five formats and a squared-rank ratio, also give about 0.44, so "one credence recovers well under half of each format's reproducible answer" is solid. The pre-registered *label*, "little format-free belief", is not. It rests on the CI's upper end falling under 0.5 by 0.0012 (0.4988). My refit at the same seeds gives 0.501, which is "partial". Other bootstrap seeds give 0.494–0.504, and two of three alternative fold seeds give 0.510 and 0.522. The label is decided by Monte Carlo and optimiser noise. The comparison with Smolin & Wilder (0.60–0.90) is called "like-for-like", but it is not. Their numbers are second-hand (reported by the literature session) and their framings, items and statistic differ.

## Holes found
1. **The verdict label sits inside the noise.** The archived upper end is 0.4988 against a bar of 0.5. Re-running the same analysis with a better-converged L1 fit (the archived fitter is under-converged; see `audits/gm3b-format-invariance.md`, hole 2) at the same fold and bootstrap seeds gives 0.501, which is "partial". With 2,000 resamples the 97.5th percentile itself has Monte Carlo error of about ±0.005, and the fold assignment moves it by up to +0.02. Consequence: the honest reading is "borderline between little and partial". The firm conclusions are that Q is well below the 0.75 needed for "a usable single belief", and that the point estimate is about 0.4.
2. **The Smolin & Wilder comparison is not like-for-like.** It is the same kind of question (does one latent predict a held-out output?). But: (a) their numbers come from a second-hand summary, and the held-out unit and R² definition are not verified; (b) 12 of their 16 outputs come in families of 4 near-duplicate framings, which makes a held-out framing far easier to predict than any of our 5 dissimilar formats; (c) their items are standalone cases across 3 domains, ours are 160 contested claims from 40 items; (d) absolute R² depends on item spread, and Q (our ceiling-normalised statistic) has no counterpart in their report. So "Haiku is below their whole range" does not separate model from method. Consequence: the comparison sentences should be softened to "for orientation".
3. **The prereg had little left to protect.** Pairwise ρ, split-half reliability, fitted links and per-claim means were all published from the same data three hours earlier. Q is roughly predictable from them (mean ρ 0.54 between formats, reliabilities about 0.9). The bars (0.75 and 0.5) were set with this in view. Freezing the code still prevents post-hoc changes to the statistic, so this is a limitation rather than misconduct.
4. **The mean Q is pulled down by one format.** The coin comparison (Q 0.18) has a strong response bias (79% choose the statement). Without it, mean Q is 0.44. This is not hidden (results:13), but "little format-free belief" leans on it.
