## Plain-English summary of the audit
The main result holds. Asking an earlier question changes later ratings in 5 of 6 comparison families. I recomputed every number in the table from the raw conversations, and the result survives dropping the three most influential items and a stricter permutation for the family that reuses data. The noise baseline is fair: it is the permutation-null mean |d| for the same cell sizes, which if anything is conservative. Three things are overstated. (1) The midway-choice family (N4: 5 conversations per arm, p = 0.16) is called "not invasive" and "the one non-invasive family" in the papers, but the pre-registration itself warns that N2–N5 can miss small effects. (2) "Macrorealism holds by construction, so every effect is invasiveness" is a theoretical argument that the pre-registration says "does not depend on the data", yet the record files it beside the results as if the test found it. (3) The README says every overnight test was frozen "before its data were opened", which is false for this archival reuse.

## Holes found
1. **The pre-registration protected little beyond the test statistic.** The families were chosen after exp5 run 1 had reported order effects on 60 of 80 QQ pairs, the Kvam null and a 24.7-point median span across chain types, and after run 2 had shown 12 of 20 anchor-(a) pairs with large order effects. The code was committed 89 s after the markdown, together with the results. The verdict of 5 of 6 was therefore largely foreseeable. This does not make it wrong: every recomputation agrees, and the effects are large. But the result should be described as a confirmatory re-analysis of data already known to show order effects, not as a fresh test.
2. **N4's null is underpowered, yet it is promoted to "non-invasive".** N4 has 5 conversations per arm. Its mean |d| exceeds the null by 1.6 points (5.0 against 3.3). The pre-registration (§6) says small effects in N2–N5 "can be missed". `paper/clonable-minds.md:122-123` and `paper/no-credence-to-read.md:196-197` turn p = 0.16 into "not invasive" and "changes the later rating no more than a filler turn does". The NCR's own exploratory KS tally (5 of 40 items reject against 2 expected) points the other way.
3. **The theoretical conclusion is presented next to the data as if it were a result.** "Macrorealism holds by construction" is an argument about the system (pre-registered §5.2 as data-independent). It is reasonable, but no NSIT number supports it or could refute it. Files that put it in a results row (record:48) or a commit headline make an argument look like a finding.
4. **The headline mean |d| is tail-driven.** N1M has median |d| 6.4 against a mean of 12.4, and three items carry half of T. Significance is robust to dropping them (9.5 against 2.1), so this is a description issue only.
5. **Minor: N1S's "consistent direction".** 30 of 80 cells go the other way. The item-bootstrap CI on the mean excludes 0, but "consistent" overstates a net shift.

## Does the verdict stand?
Pre-registered: invasive = Holm p < 0.05. Result: N1M, N1S, N2, N3 and N5 are invasive; N4 is not. **STANDS WITH CAVEATS.** Every family's statistic and p-value recomputes from the raw archive. The result survives a leave-out of the top 3 items and a joint permutation for N2's shared O1 data. The caveats: the families' outcomes were largely known before freezing, N4's "no" is an underpowered null, and the macrorealism conclusion is an argument, not a result of this analysis.
