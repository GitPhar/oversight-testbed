## Plain-English summary of the audit
I rebuilt the one-credence versus six-credence test from scratch, with a different fitting method. On Sonnet I reproduce +9.23 against +9.22, with 92.5% of claims positive and the same interval. The test F′ score also reproduces: Q = 0.687 [0.53, 0.83]. Sonnet's *mixed* reading comes from one claim (h058 claim 8, ΔLL −308). On that claim Sonnet's own answers within a single format jump between extremes. Remove that one claim and Sonnet reads *no single credence* (+13.2 [+9.8, +17.0]). The Opus arm supports no verdict. It covers 26 claims from 7 hand-ordered items, and Opus answers almost deterministically. The one-credence model's fitted slopes run off to about 20,000–56,000, so ΔLL depends on where the optimiser stops: 0.16 to 0.42, with 14 to 16 of 26 claims positive. One claim fewer above zero would flip the pre-registered label from *mixed* to *one credence suffices*. The pre-registered "mixed" label is correctly applied, but the paper's and brief's sentences saying the format finding "does not hold on Opus", that one credence "fits as well as" six there, or that "the evidence points the other way" go beyond what the data show. Kendall's W does rise from Haiku to Sonnet to Opus, and the rise survives an item-matched check on Opus's 26 claims (0.50, 0.76, 0.84).

## Holes found
1. **Sonnet's *mixed* reading rests on one claim, and that claim is a fitting artefact rather than evidence for a single credence.** h058 claim 8 has ΔLL −308. Sonnet's within-format answers on it are bimodal: F5 gives 5, 90, 5, 8, 5, 90, 5, 90, 85, 90; F6 swings between "very unlikely" and "very likely"; F1 sits around 8–70. L6 shares one Gaussian σ per format across claims (fitted σ_F1 0.33–0.40, σ_F5 0.40–0.50), so a claim whose even and odd repeats differ gets a catastrophic held-out likelihood under L6. Removing this claim gives +13.24 [+9.79, +16.97], 93.7% positive, *no single credence*. Removing item h058 gives +13.6 [+10.2, +17.6], also *no single credence*. Every other leave-one-item-out keeps *mixed*. This is the answer to the Haiku-against-Sonnet puzzle (86% positive with a CI above 0, against 92% positive with a CI across 0). One −308 claim, resampled with its item in the cluster bootstrap, drags resampled means below 0. Sonnet's per-claim evidence against one credence is as broad as Haiku's. Consequence: "weakens sharply" is not supported on the primary statistic. The weakening on Sonnet shows in F′ (Q 0.29 → 0.69) and in W, not in the share of claims.
2. **The Opus arm cannot support any verdict.**
   - (a) It holds 26 of 80 claims from 7 of 20 items. These are the first items in list order, all Haiku-wrong, so the subset is not random.
   - (b) Opus is near-deterministic: in 57% of claim × format cells all 10 repeats are identical. The prereg's own §5 warned that ΔLL then shrinks mechanically.
   - (c) L1's link slopes are not identified under separation. The archived Opus fit has b_F4 = 19,664 and 56,056, so the penalised likelihood has no finite maximum. ΔLL then depends on where the optimiser stops: archived +0.42 with 15/26 positive; my fits +0.16 to +0.28 with 14–16/26.
   - (d) The label is a knife-edge. At 13/26 positive with a CI containing 0, the pre-registered rule says *one credence suffices*.

   Consequence: "mixed" is the correct mechanical label, but the Opus row is undetermined. It is not "fits as well as six" and not "points the other way".
3. **The D38 statistic is optimiser-dependent whenever a format is perfectly separable.** This applies to the Haiku reference too: unbounded joint optimisation drives Haiku's L1 F2 slope to the bound, and ΔLL to 247. Block coordinate ascent with 40 rounds stops earlier, at a finite slope. The reading survives every variant I tried, but the reported magnitudes (+19.42; +0.42) are properties of the fitting procedure as much as of the data. This should be stated for D38 as well.
4. **The analysis changed after Opus data existed.** `f406a9c` (07:53:12) changed `mm_gm3b.analyse_one` to use only complete claims and to skip F′ for incomplete arms. That was 7 minutes after Opus's calls and before results. It is announced in DM4a (07:49:52) and is a natural rule, but it was not frozen with DM2. Its effect is small: it drops 40 calls of a 27th claim.
5. **The chunking fix was applied asymmetrically.** DM2a resumed Sonnet in 400-call chunks to get past the 1.25× projection factor, but Opus stayed stopped. A 400-call Opus chunk projects to $3.10 + 400 × $0.00194 × 1.25 = $4.07, under its $4.30 cap, so about 6–7 more Opus claims were affordable under the frozen cap. This is minor, but it is part of why Opus's arm is so small.
6. **The W trend is real but secondary.** W measures rank agreement of per-claim means, and near-deterministic answers at the extremes push it up. It is not the pre-registered belief test. The argsort tie-breaking inflates W by about 0.02 on Sonnet and Opus. The item-matched trend (0.50 → 0.76 → 0.84) holds, so the paper's caveat that "the last step is not item-matched" can be answered in its favour (exploratory).
7. **The DEVIATIONS time stamps are wrong.** DM2 says 07:50, but it was committed at 07:44:03 and the first call came at 07:45:58. DM2a says 08:50 but was committed at 07:59:51. If anyone relied on the stated times, the "before any call" claims would read as false.
