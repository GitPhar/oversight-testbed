## Plain-English summary of the audit
Every number in the table reproduces exactly from the archive. I rescored A2, F2, L, H and G from the raw pool records with my own circuit and AUROC code, and the per-item scores match to 1e-15. The pre-registered verdict ("not met: ties or loses") is correct, but the test could hardly have passed. The non-overlapping-intervals rule needed A2 at about 0.93 or more against F2's 0.84, and the paired interval (±0.07) rules out only differences larger than about 0.07. Item type alone scores 0.71 on the locked test, and mean claim confidence alone 0.77. Most of the 0.83 is not deliberation structure. Cut or reword the coupling sentences and the "G beats L and H on both sets" / "G outperforms every structural analysis" sentences.

## Holes found
1. **The coupling argument is wrong, though the conclusion survives.** The "flat in κ" evidence comes from candidates where κ is unidentifiable: |α| > 1.3 (8 of 10 candidates), or α = π/2 with N = 0, where the readout is the Z axis and neither the phases nor the diagonal RZZ change the probabilities. For the selected model the train objective is sharply curved in κ: 0.819 at the fit, 0.611 at v1's (1, 1) and 0.534 at (1.5, 1.5) (`scratchpad/g2/profile.py`). The fitted value is informative: it rules out strong couplings. What supports "couplings add nothing" is the κ = 0 ablation (train 0.806 vs 0.819; locked test −0.009 [−0.029, +0.009]), not the candidate spread. The near-Z candidates are effectively confidence-only scorers, and exploratorily they score 0.83–0.85 on the locked test. That is further evidence that the relation web adds nothing, but it is not what the text says.
2. **The primary test was almost unpassable, and "tie" has no margin.** F2's upper bound is 0.89, and A2's interval half-width is about 0.07, so A2 needed an AUROC around 0.93 or more (a gain of about 0.10 or more). The paired difference has SD 0.037, so the 80%-power detectable difference is about 0.10, and the 95% interval excludes only |Δ| > about 0.07. "Not met" is correct. "Ties" should be read as "no difference larger than about 0.07 in either direction".
3. **Item type and plain confidence carry most of the signal.** After the D9b enrichment, the 49 claim and puzzle test items contain 0 wrong. A type-only score (simpleqa > science > claim/puzzle) gets AUROC 0.71 [0.64, 0.78] on the locked test, and negative mean claim confidence alone gets 0.77 [0.69, 0.85]. Within type, A2 is 0.79 (science) and 0.84 (simpleqa). The by-type table is in the file, but no sentence warns that 0.83 is not a measure of deliberation structure.
4. **The run-2 "held-out" set had been heavily used before v2 scored it.** Stage 2 runs 2 and 2b, D15 and window note W4 all touched these 80 items. W4 (17:41) computed hedge count AUROC 0.78 on exactly these 80 items before H was pre-registered, so H's held-out 0.77/0.78 here is a recomputation, not a test. The held-out A2 − F2 edge also sits on the same questions as the Sonnet edge (see audits/sonnet-replication.md).
5. **D9b selection after interim error rates.** It is disclosed and category-level, and it came before the split, so it does not leak, but it is the reason for hole 3.
6. **Minor.** Pool 5 contains 5 duplicate questions. Two straddle train and validation (t071/t478, t232/t426; all right answers), and none touch the test set. The paired "point estimates" in the JSON are bootstrap means, not observed differences (+0.108 vs observed +0.106).

## Does the verdict stand?
Pre-registered verdict: NOT MET ("the learned quantum encoding ties or loses to its classical twin"). **STANDS WITH CAVEATS.** The rule was applied correctly to correct numbers. But the rule had almost no power against any plausible advantage, "tie" is a non-detection within ±0.07, and the stated reason the couplings don't matter is wrong (the ablation, not the candidate spread, supports it).
