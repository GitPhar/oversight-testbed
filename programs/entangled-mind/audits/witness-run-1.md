## Plain-English summary of the audit
The witness result is solid. I rebuilt each pair's two-qubit state from the archived hardware counts with my own code: linear inversion and readout mitigation, cross-checked with a maximum-likelihood fit. The negativities come out at 0.147, 0.063 and 0.067, exactly as reported. Their lower bounds (about 0.05 or higher) stay well above zero when the bootstrap also resamples the calibration circuits, and also without any readout mitigation (lower bounds 0.128, 0.039, 0.047). So "certified 3 of 3" and "clears zero without mitigation" both hold. The three items were the top 3 of the 80 test items by ideal negativity, as the rule says. What overreaches is the summary "the encoded belief states are certified entangled". The witness certifies two-qubit entanglement in the three most entangled of 80 states (ideal pair negativity 0.16–0.18, against a median of 0.058). In one of the three (h221) the measured pair is decoupled from the rest of the state, so that test is a two-qubit entangling test. And arm A, the readout actually used for judgment, was never run on hardware.

## Holes found
1. **The summary sentences generalise past the evidence.** The witness rule picks the three *most* entangled of the 80 test states (ideal pair negativity 0.16–0.18; median item 0.058; 15% of items below 0.01). Given that the device kept roughly half the predicted negativity on the two mixed-pair items, a typical item's best pair would sit near 0.03 on hardware, and some items would show none. "The encoded belief states are certified entangled" (README:17, 188; v3:55, 831; paper.md:22, 146; record.md:72) should say *three* states, *selected as most entangled*, and *pairwise*.
2. **One of three is a two-qubit test.** h221's pair is decoupled (pure reduced state), so its certificate says nothing about the belief web's structure. Only 2 of 3 certificates concern a pair entangled with the rest of its belief state. The result file and README say this; the abstract-level sentences do not.
3. **D8 and the submitting code were committed after the job ran.** The IBM record shows the change was real and made before submission, so this is a record-keeping gap, not a forking path. But README:221's blanket "logged before the data they affect" is false for D8, as it is for D10 (logged after Stage 1 returned) and D15 (exploratory on existing data).
4. **The bootstrap ignores calibration-matrix uncertainty.** Including it widens the intervals by at most 0.002 (my check), so this is immaterial.
