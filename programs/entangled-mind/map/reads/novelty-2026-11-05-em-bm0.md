## Novelty line
The source part is in print: Kumaran et al. 2025 (arXiv 2507.03120) find Gemma 3, GPT-4o and o1-preview change their mind far less when their own initial answer is visible (13.1% against 32.5% hidden) and that the effect disappears when the same answer is attributed to another LLM (33.2% against 31.3%). Their design is two turns at one fixed foil separation, so neither the fall in uptake with distance (0.71, 0.44, 0.21 at 10, 30, 60 points) nor the survival of the pull across three filler turns is stated there or anywhere else found: partly new. Search: WebSearch for "LLM anchoring own prior answer versus other", "LLM anchoring anchor distance extreme anchors", "LLM anchoring persists across intervening turns", "serial dependence large language models"; arXiv abstracts and the Kumaran HTML (both halves) opened.
## Papers opened
- Kumaran, Fleming, Markeeva, Heyward, Banino, Mathur, Pascanu, Osindero, de Martino, Velickovic, Patraucean, 2025, "How Overconfidence in Initial Choices and Underconfidence Under Criticism Modulate Change of Mind in LLMs", https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03120 and https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03120 (full text, both halves). Own visible answer: change of mind 13.1% shown against 32.5% hidden; Section 3.5, answer attributed to a different LLM: 33.2% against 31.3%, not significant. Foil separation was set only to tune difficulty, never varied as a factor; no delay or intervening turns. Bears on the own-versus-other part; nothing on distance or lag.
- Owusu and Feldman, 2026, "Anchoring Depends on Confidence and Post-Training in Language Models", https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.16/ (ACL 2026 short). Llama and Qwen base, instruct and distilled; anchoring susceptibility falls with model confidence regardless of accuracy; more susceptible to high than low anchors. No distance or source manipulation in the abstract.
- Huang, Bie, Na, Ruan, Lei, Yue, He, 2025 (v2 2026), "Understanding the Anchoring Effect of LLM with Synthetic Data", https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15392. Anchoring common and tied to shallow layers; abstract reports no distance or source analysis.
- Harshavardhan, 2026, "Self-Anchoring Calibration Drift in LLMs", https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01239. Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.2; building on their own prior outputs over turns shifts expressed confidence and calibration error. Multi-turn self-anchoring on confidence, not a planted number at set distances or lags.
- Bajaj and Tiganj, 2026, "Who Do LLMs Trust? Human Experts Matter More Than Other LLMs", https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13568. Prior answers attributed to friends, experts or other LLMs; experts weigh most. No own-answer condition; no distance.
- Chen, 2026, "Relational Positioning as a Measurable Risk Object", https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11437. A conversation-built relational state persists after its prompt is removed; about stance, not numeric answers. Weak bearing on lag persistence.
- Fischer and Whitney, 2014, "Serial dependence in visual perception", Nature Neuroscience (abstract via Europe PMC); Cicchini, Mikellidou, Burr, 2018, "The functional role of serial dependence", Proc. R. Soc. B (abstract via Europe PMC). Human attraction toward prior stimuli, tuned to proximity and similarity. The human source of the distance-tuning prediction; not on LLMs.
## Not found / caveats
- No LLM paper found that varies the distance of a planted own answer from the model's view and reports uptake falling with distance, nor one that inserts filler turns between a planted own answer and a re-rating.
- Vaccaro 2026 (arXiv 2606.11217), returned by a search on anchor distance, turned out to be a position paper on preregistration with no anchoring results.
- The project's own caveat stands: Kumaran et al. explain own-answer effects by choice-supportive confidence, and the distance curve cannot separate serial-dependence tuning from discarding an implausible far "own" answer.
