## Novelty line
That a single-belief description fits more capable models better is already stated by Smolin and Wilder (2026, arXiv:2609.07943): held-out fit rises along a capability gradient and framing effects are "largely absent in the strongest models"; their nine models include no Claude. Our Claude-family gradient (Haiku +19.4, Sonnet +9.2, Opus +1.9 per claim; Kendall's W 0.54, 0.71, 0.87) points the same way, but the held-out-repeat comparison across Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, Sonnet's mixed reading and Opus's near-agreement on the project's own claims are not in print. Verdict: partly new.
Search: WebSearch for "latent belief" "capability" "framing effects" "LLM" and "Claude Haiku Sonnet Opus elicitation format"; Smolin and Wilder PDF read in full.
## Papers opened
- Smolin, A., Wilder, B., 2026, Beliefs and Behavior in Language Models, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07943 (PDF). "Less capable LLMs score much less highly, and the overall trend in performance aligns with a general gradient of model capabilities"; models ordered by the Artificial Analysis capability index; no Anthropic model tested.
- Temkit, S.-A., 2026, AMEL, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22714. Tests Anthropic Haiku through Opus but on history effects, not format agreement; does not bear on it.
- Lu, Y.-L., Zhang, C., Wang, W., 2025, arXiv:2504.19445, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19445. Binary versus continuous discrepancy across "several models"; models not named on the abstract page; no capability gradient stated.
