# Re-scout around em-dm6f2f2 (does the format gap in an under-70% list survive a wording that fixes the number to the model's own guess?)

Written 2026-11-05 (America/Denver) by a fresh subagent (claude-opus-5-5) that ran none of this bet's pre-registration, calls, analysis or audit. Web reads only (WebSearch, WebFetch; one PDF that WebFetch could not parse was converted with `pdftotext` in the session scratchpad). No model API was called and no local model was loaded. Format: `operators/remap.md` Step 1b.

**The result being re-scouted (em-dm6f2f2, pass unreplicated, Sonnet).** With both templates reworded so the number is about "YOUR guess above" (and, for a "No", the chance that No is right), Sonnet's under-70% lists still differed by format: J_F 0.37 [0.28, 0.46] against J_N 0.64 [0.55, 0.73], D +0.27 [+0.18, +0.37] on 268 questions. The manipulation check did not fully take effect (Yes/No answers with a number below 0.5: probability 0.51 → 0.18, frequency 0.21 → 0.07; the rule asked for ≤ 0.10 in both). The rewording also turned most answers into Yes/No guesses (82–92% per cell). Haiku's gap was +0.27 (reported); Opus ran 20 questions and was untestable.

**Already in the program's reads and not re-read in full:** Tian et al. 2023 (2305.14975), Xiong et al. 2023 (2306.13063), Xia et al. 2026 (2601.08064), Harshavardhan 2026 (2603.01239), Zhang et al. 2026 (2604.05397), Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995 (`novelty-2026-11-05-em-dm6.md`). **Field note.** No paper found asks a model for its confidence as a probability and as an "out of 100 such questions" frequency on the same items, and none tests whether a threshold list survives a change of format against a same-format repeat. The closest kind of result is threshold-set instability under prompt perturbations (Zhao et al. 2025); the closest mechanism is human format dependence (Hansson, Juslin and Winman 2003).

## Read records

### Do We Know What LLMs Don't Know? A Study of Consistency in Knowledge Probing — Zhao, Köksal, Modarressi, Hedderich & Schütze, 2025, arXiv 2505.21701 (v2, 30 May 2025)
- read_depth: partial (arXiv HTML v2, first 100,000 of 118,554 characters; Table 1 and the AskCal prompt read).
- what: six knowledge-probing methods decide which questions a model "knows" (accept) or not (reject); consistency of the accepted and rejected sets under small prompt changes (added space, typo, option shuffling, one-shot) is IoU per set. AskCal asks "Provide the probability that your guess is correct" and thresholds it on a validation set. Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, OLMo-2-7B (Table 1, MMLU), more in other tables; no Claude.
- result: AskCal under option shuffling, IoU of the rejected sets .61 (Mistral-7B), .20 (LLaMA-3.1-8B), .55 (OLMo-2-7B); under an added space .76, .42, .60. Cross-method decision consistency "as low as 7%". Some validation-chosen thresholds were "as high as 0.98".
- remap head: **already shown** (the kind): a verbalized-confidence threshold flags different questions under small prompt changes, measured as set overlap. They change the question's input, not the confidence format, and have no same-prompt repeat baseline; their added-space row is a near-trivial perturbation baseline we could borrow.

### Same Answer, Different Confidence: Protocol Sensitivity in Measuring LLM Confidence — Kim & Kang, 2026, arXiv 2605.27752 (v4)
- read_depth: partial re-read (arXiv HTML, all characters in two passes; Appendix B.1 text read, Table 9's prompts not in the extracted text).
- what: answers, verbalized scores and correctness held fixed; the context in which token probability is read, and the answer supplied for verbalized confidence, are varied. Five open 7–9B models, Qwen2.5 to 72B; no Claude.
- result: the shift "also appears" on a 0–100 scale (App. B.1), but B.1 gives no direct 0–1 against 0–100 comparison. Appendix I: mean confidence for the model's own correct answer .913, own wrong .813, canonical reference .821, plausible wrong .761, off-type .183; canonical minus plausible wrong +.056 [+.050, +.062]; re-supplying the model's own answer moves confidence by at most .022.
- remap head: **rival named**: stated confidence follows the plausibility of the answer string in front of the model more than its correctness. On "Is the answer X?" items that predicts a number tied to how plausible X looks, whichever way the model answers, which is the referent rival in another form; our rewording reduced but did not remove it (0.18, 0.07).

### Naïve Sampling and Format Dependence in Subjective Probability Calibration — Hansson, Juslin & Winman, 2003, Proceedings of the Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society 25
- read_depth: partial (eScholarship PDF via pdftotext; abstract, introduction and Figure 1 with its caption).
- what: people's calibration depends on response format applied to the same beliefs; a naïve sampling model explains the gap between interval production and probability assessment, tested on human data.
- result: Figure 1 (reproduced from Juslin and Persson 2002): with the half-range format (two alternatives, 50–100% scale) "close to zero over/underconfidence", with the full-range format (no choice, 0–100%) "marginal overconfidence", with interval production "extreme overconfidence"; across 100% intervals about 40% of true values fall inside.
- remap head: **design to borrow**: the half-range format (two alternatives, a 50–100% scale) cannot produce a number below 0.5, so it removes the sub-0.5 referent reading by construction. Asking for half-range confidence on the old, open-answer template would separate the referent fix from the switch to Yes/No answers, which this bet could not.

### Beyond 0-100: How Confidence Scale Design Shapes Verbalized Confidence and LLM Metacognition — Dai & Wang, 2026, arXiv 2603.09309 (v4, 24 Oct 2026)
- read_depth: abstract only.
- what: scale granularity, boundary placement and range regularity varied across six LLMs (not named on the abstract page) and three datasets; meta-d'.
- result: "more than 78% of responses concentrating on just three round-number values"; a 0–20 scale improves metacognitive efficiency over 0–100.
- remap head: **rival named**: if the two formats heap on different round values near the cut (0.7 against 70, 75), which answers fall under 70% can change with the scale's heaping rather than with belief. em-dm6f2's secondary thresholds (0.5, 0.6, 0.8 gave similar gaps) argue against it but do not exclude it; a threshold-free rank comparison would.

### On Verbalized Confidence Scores for LLMs — Yang, Tsai & Yamada, 2024 (rev. 2026), arXiv 2412.14737 (v2)
- read_depth: partial (arXiv HTML v2, via a summary of the page; methods list and headline numbers).
- what: 17 prompt methods (0–100, 0–1, letters, labels; "confidence score" against "probability that your answer is correct"; few-shot; combinations) on 11 open and OpenAI models; no Claude.
- result: calibration "heavily depends on how we ask for it"; the "probscore" wording helps tiny models most; large models' ECE "around 0.1". No frequency wording, no thresholds.
- remap head: **changes nothing**: wording moves calibration is already in the program's reads (Tian et al. 2023, Kim and Kang 2026); this adds no format or threshold comparison.

### Are LLM Decisions Faithful to Verbal Confidence? — Wang, Zhou, Devic & Fu, 2026, arXiv 2601.07767 (v1)
- read_depth: partial (arXiv HTML, via a summary of the page).
- what: RiskEval: answer-or-abstain under a penalty λ, optimal threshold λ/(1+λ), with a stated numeric confidence; ten GPT, Gemini, Llama, Gemma, DeepSeek and Qwen models; no Claude.
- result: "models almost never abstain" even when abstaining is optimal; applying the optimal threshold post hoc to their own stated confidence raises GPT-5-nano's normalized utility from −0.688 to −0.004 on HLE (λ ≥ 10).
- remap head: **changes nothing**: our overseer applies the threshold from outside, which is the post-hoc policy this paper finds useful; it varies no format.

### Rethinking Verbalized Confidence for LLM-as-a-Judge — Hsiao, 2026, arXiv 2609.10996 (v1)
- read_depth: partial (arXiv HTML, first 100,000 of 103,064 characters, via a summary).
- what: verbalized 0–100 confidence under a fixed banded rubric, with an overconfidence advisory and self-debate, on Claude Sonnet 4, 4.5, 4.6 and GPT and Gemini models as judges.
- result: Sonnet 4.5 AECE 9.1% → 3.3% on AggreFact with the paper's prompt (Table 2).
- remap head: **changes nothing**: one fixed format; shows Claude judges' stated confidence moves with prompt additions, which our result already assumes.

### Large Language Models Are Overconfident in Their Own Responses — Sanz-Guerrero, Mager & von der Wense, 2026, arXiv 2606.03437 (ACL 2026 Findings)
- read_depth: abstract only.
- result: six open-weight models assign "up to 26% higher confidence to their own responses" than to identical answers from a user; framing the answer as user input improves calibration.
- remap head: **changes nothing**: both our formats rate the model's own guess in the same turn, so ownership is held fixed; it would matter only to a design that moves the guess into a user turn.

- remap head: **changes nothing**: no format comparison on the abstract page.

## What the re-scout changes

Adds a half-range follow-up (Hansson, Juslin and Winman 2003): Sonnet states 50–100% confidence that its own answer is right on the old open-answer template, which rules out sub-0.5 numbers without the Yes/No switch and so separates the referent fix from the answer-form change; archive questions, Sonnet, cheap. Re-prices the threshold-free version upward: round-number heaping (Dai and Wang 2026) is a rival a rank comparison excludes, at $0 on existing data. Adds Zhao et al.'s added-space perturbation as a second noise baseline. Opus replication stays, but needs at least 40 questions to be readable. Odds unmoved: no paper predicts the direction.
