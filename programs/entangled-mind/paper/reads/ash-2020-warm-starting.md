# On warm-starting neural network training (shrink-and-perturb) — Ash & Adams, 2020, NeurIPS
- url: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/288cd2567953f06e460a33951f55daaf-Paper.pdf
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: full (NeurIPS main paper; appendix not included in this PDF)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader C, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: Whether a network trained on half the data and then trained on all of it ("warm start") generalises as well as one trained from scratch; whether L2, confidence penalties, adversarial training, batch size or learning rate fix the gap; and the "shrink and perturb" trick: before each new round, set each weight to lambda x old weight + Gaussian noise (0 < lambda < 1), i.e. shrink TOWARD ZERO, not toward a pretrained model.
- on_whom: ResNet-18, 3-layer tanh MLP, logistic regression; CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN; SGD and Adam; online-learning curves on CIFAR-10; pretraining-then-target experiments with ResNet-18. Std over runs reported in parentheses (n not restated in main text).
- how_scored: Validation accuracy on a held-out third of the data; wall-clock/epochs to train.
- result: Table 1, CIFAR-10 ResNet: random init 56.2 (SGD) / 78.0 (Adam) vs warm start 51.7 / 74.4; CIFAR-100 ResNet 18.2 / 41.4 vs 15.5 / 35.0; logistic regression (convex) not significantly hurt. Regularisation helps but does not close the gap. Shrink-and-perturb closes the gap and trains faster; shrinking need not be aggressive; Figure 9 uses lambda = 0.3, noise scale 0.0001; shrink-perturbed models do at least as well as the better of warm start and random init in the pretraining setting. Shrinking preserves ReLU no-bias networks' hypotheses (only scales the softmax, raising output entropy). Quote: "this warm-starting seems to yield poorer generalization performance".
- limits: About plasticity/generalisation on growing data from the SAME distribution, not about retention of old tasks; no forgetting measurement; vision only; no language models.
- borrow: For T4 this paper is the "shrink toward zero" control arm, not the "shrink toward base" arm: on an LLM, shrink-toward-zero of the full weights would destroy pretraining, but shrink-toward-zero of a LoRA delta IS shrink-toward-base. So T4 should compare (i) LoRA delta scaled by lambda (the WiSE-FT direction), (ii) lambda-scaling plus small noise (the full shrink-and-perturb analogue on the adapter), (iii) no shrink, measuring both retention (old-task/probe) and plasticity on the next task. The paper's claim that shrinking raises output entropy predicts that delta-shrinking will also make the model less confident, a confound to measure (calibration/entropy) when scoring T4.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Understand why warm starts sit in poorly generalising regions; apply shrink-perturb in online and active learning to avoid full retraining.
- already_run_check: T4 nearly (shrink-and-perturb between training rounds, on vision nets, measured for generalisation not forgetting; shrink is toward zero). Others: none.
- transfers: partly — the operation is trivial to apply to a LoRA adapter on our laptop; the evidence is about plasticity, not about keeping old knowledge.
