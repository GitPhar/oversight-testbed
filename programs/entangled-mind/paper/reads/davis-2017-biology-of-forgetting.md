# The biology of forgetting, a perspective — Davis & Zhong, 2017, Neuron 95(3):490-503
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5657245 (NIHMS author manuscript, fetched as full-text XML via NCBI efetch db=pmc id=5657245)
- field: neuroscience
- read_depth: full
- read_by: scout-bmq reader A, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: A perspective arguing that forgetting is an active, dedicated biological process ("intrinsic forgetting"), separate from acquisition. It contrasts two views: "non-forgetting" (the trace is intact and only retrieval fails; reminders bring it back) and active erosion of the trace. Mechanisms reviewed: dopamine "forgetting cells" act through the DAMB receptor and the Scribble/Rac1/PAK/cofilin pathway (fly mushroom body); Cdc42 for anesthesia-resistant memory; hippocampal neurogenesis; AMPA (GluA2) receptor endocytosis. Sleep and rest reduce the dopamine forgetting signal, and activity increases it.
- on_whom: Cited studies in Drosophila (olfactory conditioning), C. elegans, mice, rats and humans. No new data.
- how_scored: Cited studies measure behavioral memory retention over time after genetic, optogenetic or pharmacological manipulation of the forgetting pathways, with acquisition measured separately. Reversal learning serves as an assay of memory updating.
- result: Rac1 activation in flies peaks 1 h after training. Blocking Rac1 extends short-term memory "several-fold", yet the extended memory stays anesthesia-sensitive, so a stronger or longer STM is not automatically consolidated. In mouse hippocampus, constitutively active Rac1 speeds the decay of object-recognition memory "by about 3-fold"; dominant-negative Rac1 prolongs it and completely blocks interference-induced forgetting, with no effect on contextual fear or trace memory. Only 3 of the 12 PPL1 dopamine neurons are required for active forgetting. Retrieval-induced forgetting shows spontaneous recovery with time, which points to retrieval failure of a trace that may be intact. In a human study cited, participants reported no memory of locations while their eyes still dwelt on the correct location. Rats that had forgotten a food location after 3 weeks recovered it after brief re-exposure to the context. Five fly ASD-risk mutants share inflexible reversal learning, attributed to impaired Rac1 forgetting.
- limits: The authors say forgetting is "generally monitored through behavior". Whether traces are actually eroded, rather than merely inaccessible, has "not yet been shown experimentally" for the Rac1/Cdc42 pathways. Vertebrate Rac1 studies are hard to reconcile with one another.
- borrow: (1) The central distinction is retrieval failure (intact trace) vs erosion, and its human example is eye gaze correct while verbal report fails. That is T1's design exactly: a linear probe (the "eye movement") against verbal answers (the "report") after LoRA-induced forgetting. Score: probe AUROC on the old true/false set before and after fine-tuning, against verbal accuracy on the same items. (2) The reminder and context-reinstatement assays (brief re-exposure to the context; a weak training trial) are the T8 recovery tests. After forgetting, give a single weak reminder (one in-context example, or one LoRA step) and measure how much returns, against a never-learned control set. Relearning savings above control means the trace was intact. (3) "Forgetting is the default; consolidation is the judge" maps onto T4/T7: a constant pull toward base weights (decay of the LoRA delta, or L2 toward base), with replay protecting only selected items. (4) "A stronger STM is not consolidated" warns T2 that a strong in-context effect may not survive distillation into weights.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Build unambiguous assays (imaging or otherwise) of engram integrity, run alongside behavior, to separate erosion from retrieval failure. Map the forgetting cells for each memory type. Study active-forgetting deficits in ASD and PTSD.
- already_run_check: none on networks. It frames T1 and T8 conceptually, and its retrieval-failure-vs-erosion test is the question T1 asks of an LLM.
- transfers: partly. The molecular pathways (Rac1, dopamine, actin) do not transfer and are killed. The behavioral logic transfers well to LLMs, because there we CAN read the "engram" directly (activations, probes, weight deltas), which is the very assay the authors say neuroscience lacks.
