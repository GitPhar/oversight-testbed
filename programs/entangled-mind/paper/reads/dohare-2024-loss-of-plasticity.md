- what_tested: Whether standard backprop networks lose the ability to LEARN new tasks (plasticity), as distinct from forgetting old ones, over long task sequences; correlates (dead units, weight growth, effective rank); which fixes help: L2, shrink-and-perturb, dropout, Adam, normalisation, and their "continual backprop" (reinitialise a small fraction of low-utility units).
- on_whom: Continual ImageNet (binary tasks from 32x32 ImageNet pairs, 1,200 train/200 test per task, up to 2,000-5,000 tasks, 30 runs); class-incremental CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 (5 to 100 classes in 20 increments, 30 runs); Online Permuted MNIST (800 tasks, 30 runs); slowly-changing regression (100 runs); PPO ant locomotion. No language-model experiments.
- how_scored: Accuracy on each new task (or vs a network retrained from scratch); fraction of dead/dormant units; mean absolute weight; effective/stable rank.
- result: Early Continual-ImageNet tasks reached up to 88% correct; by task 2,000 backprop had lost substantial plasticity at all step sizes. CIFAR-100: the incrementally trained network ended 5% below a freshly retrained one; continual backprop reached 76.13% on all 100 classes (replacement rate 1e-5, maturity threshold 1,000). Online Permuted MNIST (step size 0.01): up to 25% of units dead after 800 tasks. Weight magnitude grows steadily under backprop but not with L2. L2 reduced plasticity loss; Adam, dropout and normalisation "actually increased" it; shrink-and-perturb reduced but did not eliminate it on CIFAR-100 (noise SD best at 1e-5), continual backprop did. Stationary ant PPO: performance rose for ~3M steps then collapsed (failing every episode by 20M). Quote: "standard deep-learning methods gradually lose plasticity in continual-learning settings".
- limits: Authors: plasticity loss may not occur at all for small numbers of tasks; head reset gives privileged task-change information. LLMs appear only in the introduction (retraining cost) and as a setting where systematic study was infeasible; no LLM or pretrained-model fine-tuning tested.
- borrow: (a) A second outcome axis for any T4/T6 run: after the sleep/shrink step, does the model learn the NEXT task as fast as before (plasticity), not only remember the old one. (b) Cheap diagnostics on a LoRA adapter: dormant-unit fraction is not meaningful for LoRA, but effective rank of the adapter update and weight-norm growth across sequential tasks are, and both can be logged for free. (c) Their claim "may not occur for small numbers of tasks" means a 2-3 task LLM study should NOT expect plasticity loss; that is a null to pre-register.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Compare and refine variants of selective reinitialisation (continual backprop); understand mechanisms of plasticity loss.
- already_run_check: T4 nearly (L2 and shrink-and-perturb as plasticity-preserving operations over task sequences, on vision/RL nets; scored for plasticity, not retention; not LLM). T6: none (no relearning-savings measure). Others: none.
- transfers: partly — the metrics and the plasticity axis transfer; the thousands-of-tasks regime does not fit a laptop LLM study and the authors expect no effect at small task counts.
