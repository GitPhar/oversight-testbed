# Human memory reconsolidation: framework and critical review — Jacob W. B. Elsey, Vanessa A. Van Ast, Merel Kindt, 2018, Psychological Bulletin 144(8)
- url: https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/46729965/Chapter_5.pdf (thesis-chapter version of the published article, from Elsey's 2020 UvA dissertation)
- field: metascience
- read_depth: partial (abstract, framework/criteria section, procedural-memory section and table, declarative-memory sections incl. Hupbach paradigm, replications and alternative explanations; the aversive/appetitive and propranolol sections were only skimmed)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader F, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: A critical review, not a meta-analysis. It sets four criteria a human study must meet to count as evidence for reconsolidation and applies them to procedural, aversive/appetitive and declarative studies.
- on_whom: The published human reconsolidation literature (no pooled N).
- how_scored: The four criteria: (1) a reactivation x manipulation interaction in a 2x2 design; (2) time dependency, with no effect outside the window; (3) memory specificity; (4) an effect at delayed but not immediate test.
- result: There are no pooled numbers. The authors find many observations consistent with reconsolidation but also inconsistent findings, and alternative explanations that block inference to reconsolidation. Many studies lack the no-reactivation/no-manipulation cell, so they cannot show the interaction. Procedural: Walker et al. 2003 was not supported in 4 attempted replications by Hardwicke et al. 2016. Declarative: Klingmüller et al. 2017 could not replicate the Hupbach asymmetric-intrusion effect at first, and found a smaller one only with highly distinct rooms. Sederberg et al. 2011 reproduced the Hupbach results with the temporal context model (context binding), without any reconsolidation process.
- limits: Narrative review, with no effect-size synthesis or bias test. Strong reverse inference from behaviour to a neural mechanism is explicitly disclaimed.
- borrow: (1) The 2x2 design. Any LLM "reconsolidation" claim (e.g., an update applied right after the model retrieves a fact edits it more than the same update without retrieval) must cross reactivation (yes/no) with update (yes/no) and test the interaction, with a Bayes factor for nulls. (2) The context-binding alternative. In a model that conditions on its transcript, apparent "updating after reactivation" can be plain context binding: the new material is bound to the reinstated context. This is the same mechanism as our Program 1 finding that consistency lives in the transcript. A context-only control (reinstate the context, no weight update) is required.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Use full 2x2 designs, Bayesian analysis of nulls, larger samples, and direct tests of boundary conditions (prediction error, memory strength) rather than invoking them post hoc.
- already_run_check: none (human; no network). It bears on T2 (consolidation of context into weights, where context-binding vs. updating must be separated) and T8 (reinstatement and resurfacing).
- transfers: partly — human reconsolidation is "viable but hotly contested". Several flagship effects did not replicate and one has a simpler context-binding explanation, so predictions derived from it are weak and need the 2x2 and context controls.
