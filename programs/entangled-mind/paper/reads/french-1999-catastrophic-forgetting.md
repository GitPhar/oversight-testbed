# Catastrophic forgetting in connectionist networks (review) — Robert M. French, 1999, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 3(4)
- url: https://lead.ube.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/000282-catastrophic-forgetting-in-connectionist-networks.pdf
- field: cognitive psychology
- read_depth: full (all text, boxes 1-4 and outstanding questions; reference list skimmed). Used here as the open SUBSTITUTE for McCloskey & Cohen 1989 (Psych. Learning & Motivation 24) and Ratcliff 1990 (Psych Rev): no open full text of either was found, so their numbers below are as reported by French, not read in the originals.
- read_by: scout-bmq reader B, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: review, no new data. Causes of catastrophic interference in distributed networks with shared weights (representational overlap, "weight cliffs"), and the fixes of 1989-1999: sparse/semi-distributed codes, orthogonalisation, rehearsal, Robins' pseudo-rehearsal, dual-network (hippocampus/neocortex-like) models, fast/slow weights.
- on_whom: backpropagation networks of the period; Box 1 reports rat peak-procedure timing data (sequential vs concurrent learning of 40 s and 8 s durations; n not given).
- how_scored: two measures of forgetting: "exact recognition" (all outputs within 0.5 of the target) and Ebbinghaus-style savings (time to relearn, Hetherington & Seidenberg 1989).
- result: as reported for McCloskey & Cohen: after learning 17 "ones" addition facts, correct answers fell from 100% to 20% within 1-5 learning trials of the "twos" facts, to 1% after five more, and to 0 by 15 trials. Humans in the Barnes-Underwood A-B/A-C paradigm forget gradually. Savings: networks that look completely forgetful on exact recognition often relearn very fast, but "not all catastrophic forgetting is of this 'shallow' sort". Rehearsal of old items and pseudopatterns (random inputs labelled by the old network) both reduce forgetting; pseudopatterns work less well than real items. The rat sequential condition showed no savings; the concurrent condition did.
- limits: narrative review, no meta-analysis; models are tiny (e.g. 2-2-1 XOR); no language models; savings vs exact-recognition dissociation not quantified across studies.
- borrow: (1) score forgetting TWO ways in any LLM fine-tuning test: verbal accuracy AND relearning savings (steps to re-reach criterion). This is the cheapest operational form of T1/T8: "shallow" forgetting = fast savings. (2) "Catastrophic remembering" (Box 3): a network that generalises too well endorses everything as old. This is a failure mode for any probe in T1: check the probe's false-alarm rate on new items, not only its true/false split. (3) Pseudopatterns = the original "dreamed replay" (T3) control: random-prompt self-outputs as replay.
- next_steps_it_suggests: outstanding questions: are neural pseudopatterns produced during REM sleep; can one distributed system get generalisation, discrimination, immunity to interference and episodic memory at once; do humans ever forget catastrophically.
- already_run_check: T3 nearly run in small 1990s networks (Robins 1995 pseudo-rehearsal; French 1997 and Ans & Rousset 1997 dual networks), not in LLMs. T6/T8: savings after apparent forgetting reported for small networks (Hetherington & Seidenberg 1989). Nothing on LLMs.
- transfers: partly — the measures (savings vs recognition) and the pseudopattern control transfer directly to LoRA on Llama-3-8B/Qwen2.5-7B; the architecture claims do not.
