# SuRe: surprise-driven prioritised replay — Hazard, Fountas, Benfeghoul, Oomerjee, Wang & Bou-Ammar, 2025, arXiv preprint
- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.22367
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: partial (abstract, introduction, section 4 with Tables 1, 3, 4, 5, discussion, appendix B.2 Llama-3.1-8B and B.3 CPT; theory section 3 skimmed)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader E, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: which items to replay in continual LLM fine-tuning. SuRe keeps the most surprising sequences (highest NLL under the current model) in the buffer, compared with reservoir, random and gradient-based selection and with label-only surprise. Also tested: whether surprise is recomputed as items are replayed, buffer size (150-1500), replay ratio (1:2 to 1:16), and a fast/slow LoRA dual learner merged by EMA ("integration"). Forgetting is framed as selection error plus integration error.
- on_whom: T5-Large (main) on the Standard CL benchmark (4 text-classification sets, 5,000 train / 500 test each, 3 orders) and the Large Number of Tasks benchmark (15 sets, 1,000 train each, 3 orders). Llama-3.1-8B on orders 1 and 4 (3 runs). Continual pretraining on 5 M2D2 domains.
- how_scored: final average accuracy after the last task; forgetting; perplexity for CPT.
- result: T5, LNT average: reservoir 69.1; surprise 72.1; slow reservoir 72.5; slow surprise 75.1; MTL 76.3; plain sequential fine-tuning 7.4. Standard CL: reservoir 76.9, surprise 77.2, slow surprise 78.1. Label-only surprise fails (64.9 / 61.2). Recomputing surprise during replay ("aging") lowers scores (74.7 / 71.4). The surprise advantage grows as the replay budget shrinks: at 1:16, slow-surprise beats slow-random by +2.48 points. The smallest surprise buffer "does not always beat the random baseline". Llama-3.1-8B: sequential fine-tuning 23.00 / 22.86; replay 65.75 / 50.53; surprise replay 72.25 / 68.53; MTL 76.00 / 69.06. CPT average perplexity: random 15.66 vs surprise 15.72 (no gain); slow surprise 11.63.
- limits: needs task boundaries. Classification tasks scored by accuracy. The Llama results are preliminary (2 orders). Selection uses real stored data, not self-generated data. No calibration, belief or probe measures. Under CPT, surprise selection alone matched random.
- borrow: for T7, surprise (NLL) is the established priority signal and random replay is the bar to beat. Prioritisation pays off mainly when the budget is small, so a test should use a small buffer and a low replay ratio. The fast/slow EMA LoRA is a cheap "consolidation" operator that fits mlx-lm (two adapters plus an EMA merge). An untested twist for us: prioritise by the model's own expressed uncertainty or inconsistency rather than NLL.
- next_steps_it_suggests: task-free (online) buffer balancing, cheaper surprise computation, larger models (Llama, Qwen) and other modalities.
- already_run_check: T7 run (surprise-prioritised vs random replay in LLM continual fine-tuning, including Llama-3.1-8B). Not run: prioritising by uncertainty or inconsistency in the model's own judgments, or by tagged value. T3: no self-generated arm.
- transfers: yes — LoRA-based, already shown on Llama-3.1-8B; the T7 variant using our order-sensitivity signal as the priority is new.
