# Forward-Forward algorithm, "Mortal computation" and "Sleep" sections — Hinton, 2022, arXiv preprint 2212.13345v1
- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13345
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: partial (abstract, MNIST results, section 5 "Sleep", section 9 "Mortal computation", section 10 future work)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader C, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: Forward-Forward (FF) replaces backprop with two forward passes (positive real data, negative data) and a per-layer "goodness" objective. Section 5 asks whether the positive phase (wake) and negative phase (self-generated data, sleep) can be separated in time. Section 9 is an argument, not an experiment: give up copying weights between hardware ("immortal" software) for cheap analogue hardware whose learned weights only work on that physical instance, and move knowledge between instances by distillation.
- on_whom: MLPs on MNIST and non-convolutional nets on CIFAR-10; a next-character predictor (10-character window) in the sleep section. No LLM.
- how_scored: Test error (MNIST), CIFAR-10 test error vs backprop.
- result: FF on permutation-invariant MNIST: 1.36% test error after 60 epochs (backprop ~1.4% in ~20 epochs, per the text); 1.37% with the last three hidden layers' activity; 0.64% with local receptive fields, similar to a backprop CNN. Sleep: an early-draft claim that thousands of positive updates followed by thousands of self-generated negative updates lose little performance could NOT be replicated ("I now suspect it was due to a bug"); separation only works with very low learning rate and very high momentum. Mortal computation: no experiment. Quote: "the computation they perform is mortal: it dies with the hardware."
- limits: Preliminary; FF scaling to large nets unknown; sleep-phase separation unresolved (Hinton calls it the most important outstanding question about FF as a biological model). Mortal computation needs analogue hardware with unknown per-instance variation, which our setting lacks.
- borrow: (a) The negative result is a design warning for T2/T3: long blocks of self-generated ("dream") updates separated from blocks of real updates may simply fail unless the learning rate is tiny; interleave rather than block. (b) Hinton's point that distillation transfers the function (including the probabilities on wrong answers), not the weights, supports scoring T2 consolidation by full-distribution KL, not top-1 agreement.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Find a goodness function that allows separating positive (wake) and negative (sleep) phases; then test whether removing negative-phase updates mimics sleep deprivation.
- already_run_check: T3 nearly-not (wake/sleep separation with self-generated negative data attempted on a character model and failed to replicate; not LLM fine-tuning). Others: none. MORTAL COMPUTATION OFFERS NO TEST on an open-weight software model: it is defined by weights being useless off their physical hardware instance, and our weights are copyable by construction.
- transfers: no — FF is not how our models are trained and mortal computation requires per-instance analogue hardware; only the failed sleep-separation result is a usable caution.
