# EWC (elastic weight consolidation) — Kirkpatrick, Pascanu, Rabinowitz, Veness, Desjardins, Rusu, Milan, Quan, Ramalho, Grabska-Barwinska, Hassabis, Clopath, Kumaran & Hadsell, 2017, PNAS (read as arXiv 1612.00796v2)
- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.00796
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: full (arXiv v2 text incl. appendix; most results are in figures, so few numbers are stated in text)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader C, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: A quadratic penalty that pulls each weight back toward its old-task value, scaled by the diagonal Fisher information (weight "importance"), so sequential training on task B keeps task A. Compared with plain SGD, uniform L2 penalty and SGD+dropout.
- on_whom: Fully connected ReLU MLPs (2-6 hidden layers, 100-2000 units) on permuted-MNIST sequences (up to ~10 permutations); a DQN agent (3 conv + 1024-unit FC, ~4x standard DQN size) on 10 Atari 2600 games played in random order. No language models.
- how_scored: Test accuracy per task over training; Atari total human-normalised score summed over 10 games (max 10). Fisher-overlap between tasks by layer. Weight-perturbation robustness along Fisher directions vs uniform directions.
- result: Text states that with plain SGD the Atari agent "never learns to play more than one game" and its total human-normalised score stays below 1; EWC agents learn several games but do not reach the score of 10 separate DQNs. Giving the true task label instead of inferred labels helped only modestly. Perturbing in the Fisher null space hurt performance as much as perturbing along the inverse Fisher, so the authors judge that the diagonal Fisher under-estimates parameter uncertainty. Permuted-MNIST: EWC keeps old tasks while uniform L2 blocks learning of task B (figure only, no numbers in text). Fisher overlap is high at output-near layers even for very different input permutations. Quote: "selectively slowing down learning on the weights important for those tasks".
- limits: Diagonal Fisher + point estimate (Laplace) is named by the authors as a significant weakness; no replay; task boundaries needed (Fisher computed at each task switch); EWC only switched on after 20M frames of a game. Small MLPs and one DQN; no NLP. The paper does not apply EWC to LLM fine-tuning.
- borrow: (a) The Fisher null-space perturbation control (perturb the weights the method thinks are unimportant and see if behaviour changes) is a cheap check for any "importance" score we compute on a LoRA adapter. (b) The uniform-L2 vs importance-weighted contrast is the right control for T4: shrinking all fine-tuning deltas equally (L2 toward base) is the paper's failing arm; importance-weighted shrinkage is the comparison arm. (c) Fisher-overlap-by-layer could tell us whether order-sensitivity and truth-probe directions share weights.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Replace the point-estimate Fisher with a Bayesian neural-network posterior to capture parameter uncertainty better.
- already_run_check: T4 nearly (uniform L2 pull toward old weights vs Fisher-weighted pull, between tasks, but on MLPs/DQN and as a training penalty, not post-hoc delta shrinkage; not on an LLM). T1-T3, T5-T8: none.
- transfers: partly — EWC on a LoRA adapter is cheap on our M2 (diagonal Fisher from a few hundred examples), but this paper itself gives no language-model evidence.
