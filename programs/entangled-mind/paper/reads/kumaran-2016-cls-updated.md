# Complementary learning systems theory updated — Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland, 2016, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 20(7):512-534
- url: https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcClelland16FinalMS.pdf (author preprint; the authors note that citation numbers differ from the published version)
- field: neuroscience (computational / cognitive)

- read_by: scout-bmq reader A, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: A theory update with no new experiment. CLS: a slow, parametric neocortex learns structure by interleaved learning, and a fast, pattern-separated hippocampus stores episodes and replays them. Three updates. (1) Replay is not a faithful copy: the hippocampus REWEIGHTS experiences (reward, surprise, information value) so the neocortex is "not a slave to the statistics of its environment". (2) Recurrent activation of hippocampal traces (REMERGE) supports some generalisation and inference. (3) Neocortical learning is "prior-knowledge dependent" rather than slow: schema-consistent information can be absorbed fast without catastrophic interference.
- on_whom: Cited rodent replay and schema studies (Tse et al. event arena: 6 flavour-place pairs learned over weeks, then 2 new pairs in one trial) and the authors' simulations (the Rumelhart semantic network: canary/salmon, plus a new item X that is schema-consistent or inconsistent). No new n.
- how_scored: In the cited simulations: speed of learning the new item, interference with existing items, and amplitude of weight changes, for consistent vs inconsistent items.
- result: Replay is time-compressed by a factor of about 20. In the event arena, new pairs survived hippocampal lesion made 2 days after learning, but not lesion made before learning; plasticity genes in neocortex were elevated 80 min after new pairs in the familiar arena compared with a novel one. In simulation, schema-consistent X ("a bird that can fly") was learned rapidly with no disruption; inconsistent X ("a bird that swims, not flies") was learned slowly and needed interleaving to avoid catastrophic interference. At the same learning rate, consistent items produced LARGER weight changes. The original 1995 interference simulations had used only schema-inconsistent new information.
- limits: A theory review. The authors say empirical evidence "does not definitely distinguish" encoding-based from retrieval-based accounts of hippocampal inference. Open questions: the conditions for rapid consolidation, the fate of hippocampal traces, "index maintenance" (do hippocampal codes track a changing cortex), and whether hippocampal traces reconsolidate.
- borrow: (1) A direct test of the schema-consistency effect on LLMs. LoRA a Llama-3-8B/Qwen2.5-7B on N new "facts" that agree with its priors and N that contradict them (equal format and length). Measure steps to learn, interference on a held-out set of old facts, and LoRA-delta norm. Prediction: consistent facts are fast, cheap and non-interfering; inconsistent ones need interleaving with old facts. (2) For our own results: the model's prior ratings play the role of the schema. When transcript content is consolidated into weights (T2), CLS-2016 predicts that own answers consistent with the base model's priors consolidate quickly, while order-induced deviations (inconsistent with the prior) consolidate slowly or are lost. Check whether consolidation pulls order-swung ratings back toward the order-free mean. (3) "Reweighting" replay toward surprising or important items is the T7 lever, with their warning of a PTSD-like failure (runaway reactivation): over-replayed items could come to dominate the weights.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Its own Outstanding Questions: when reweighted replay biases the neocortical model, index maintenance, the fate of traces after consolidation, the conditions for rapid consolidation, reconsolidation of hippocampal traces, and how to design external memory for agents.
- already_run_check: none on LLMs. Its schema-consistency simulation is a small-network analogue that has not been run (by this paper) on a 7-8B LoRA. Related to T7 (reweighted replay) and T2.
- transfers: yes. It is a computational claim about multilayer networks trained by gradient descent (backed by the Saxe et al. analysis), so it applies to LoRA on a 7-8B model directly, and the consistent-vs-inconsistent fact test costs $0 on the laptop.
