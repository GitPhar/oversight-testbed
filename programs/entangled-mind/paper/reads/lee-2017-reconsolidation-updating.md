# Reconsolidation updating (substitute for Nader, Schafe & LeDoux 2000) — Lee, Nader & Schiller, 2017, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 21(7):531-545
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5605913 (NIHMS author manuscript, fetched as full-text XML via NCBI efetch db=pmc id=5605913)
- field: neuroscience
- read_depth: full. The brief allowed a full-text reconsolidation review in place of the paywalled Nader et al. 2000 Nature paper, and this is that review. Nader is a coauthor.
- read_by: scout-bmq reader A, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: A review of "reconsolidation updating". Retrieving a consolidated memory can destabilise it. While it restabilises (a window of hours), the memory can be weakened by amnesic drugs, strengthened by enhancers, or rewritten by new learning (extinction, counterconditioning, interference). It also covers clinical translation and the challenges: state-dependent and "unleashed extinction" accounts, and replication failures.
- on_whom: Cited studies in mice, rats and humans (healthy people; heroin users, smokers, hazardous drinkers, spider-phobic people, PTSD pilots). No new data. Per-study n mostly not given.
- how_scored: Cited studies compare reactivation then treatment within the window against treatment with no reactivation, or against reactivation with the treatment delayed (often 6 h). Outcomes: memory expression later (fear response, craving, intrusions, recall intrusions), plus tests of recovery (spontaneous recovery, reinstatement).
- result: Heroin: only the group reminded then given extinction (no 6 h gap) showed reduced craving lasting at least 6 months. Spider phobia: extinction 10 min after a reminder beat 6 h after, and the effect lasted 6 months. Human fear: after extinction during reconsolidation, there was no recovery of the amygdala threat trace even 18 months later, whereas the standard-extinction group's trace recovered. Retrieval alone is NOT enough to destabilise human fear memory; a prediction error at reactivation appears to be required. A direct replication series failed to reproduce motor-memory reconsolidation (Walker-type finger tapping). Several conceptual replications of retrieval-extinction were "seemingly unsuccessful".
- limits: The authors concede that some amnesia may be state-dependent or extinction-like rather than true reconsolidation. There are no positive markers of destabilisation, so a null result cannot separate "never destabilised" from "updating failed". Clinical studies are small and short (1-month follow-up for smokers), and none shows 100% persistent remission.
- borrow: (1) Treat a re-rating as retrieval plus an update window. Our "own earlier answer planted in transcript" result (own minus other uptake +0.46) behaves like reactivation: showing the model its OWN prior answer re-opens and anchors that judgment. Reconsolidation then predicts that a correction sticks better when it follows retrieval of the own answer AND carries a prediction error. In-context test, $0: (a) correction alone vs (b) own-answer retrieval then correction vs (c) own-answer retrieval then correction after many intervening turns (the "6 h" control). Score the later re-rating with the correction removed from context. (2) For a weights version (LoRA knowledge update), condition the update on a mismatch: train only on items where the model's current answer is wrong, against training on all items. This is a destabilisation gate and links to T7. (3) The review's key methodological demand is a recovery assay after any "forgetting" or update: spontaneous recovery, reinstatement with a weak reminder, relearning savings. This is exactly the control T8 needs: after a LoRA update or unlearning, re-test with a weak reminder prompt and count how much of the old answer returns.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Find markers of destabilisation and restabilisation separately, tailor "behavioural dosing" to each individual, and test recovery against relapse triggers systematically.
- already_run_check: none on networks. It supplies the recovery-assay control for T8 and a retrieval-gated update variant relevant to T2/T5.
