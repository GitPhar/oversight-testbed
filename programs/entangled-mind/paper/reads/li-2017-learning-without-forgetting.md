# Learning without Forgetting (LwF) — Li & Hoiem, 2017, IEEE TPAMI (read as arXiv 1606.09282v3; first version ECCV 2016)
- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.09282
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: full (arXiv v3; per-pair numbers are mostly in Table 1 and figures, read through the text)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader C, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: Add a new task to a pretrained CNN using only new-task data: record the OLD network's outputs on the NEW images, then train new-task loss plus a distillation loss that keeps the old-task outputs on those images (a warm-up of the new head, then joint optimisation). Compared with fine-tuning, fine-tuning FC only, feature extraction, Less-Forgetting Learning (LFL), and joint training with the old data.
- on_whom: AlexNet and VGG pretrained on ImageNet or Places365; new tasks VOC, CUB, MIT Scenes, MNIST; also sequential addition of 3 parts of Scenes/VOC; 3 runs with different new-head initialisations; one tracking experiment (MD-Net) in the appendix. No language models.
- how_scored: Old-task and new-task accuracy (mAP for VOC), reported as differences from LwF.
- result: Places365 to CUB: Places365 accuracy loss 8.4% for fine-tuning, 3.8% for LwF, 1.5% for joint training. LwF beats fine-tuning on the NEW task in most pairs (exceptions ImageNet to MNIST and Places365 to CUB), which the authors read as old-output preservation acting as a regulariser; on the old task LwF beats fine-tuning but often loses to feature extraction. Dissimilar new tasks degrade the old task more. Tracking gain not statistically significant. Quote: "Preserving outputs on the old task is a more direct and interpretable way".
- limits: Image classification only; needs task-specific heads; old-output preservation is computed only on new-task inputs, so it fails when the distributions differ (ImageNet to MNIST). Not applied to LLMs in this paper.
- borrow: LwF is self-distillation on the model's OWN earlier outputs, the nearest classic ancestor of T2's "consolidate own transcripts into weights". The ImageNet to MNIST failure (distillation on off-distribution inputs fails to protect the old task) is a failure mode to plan for in T2/T3: replaying own answers on unrelated prompts may not protect the target behaviour. A cheap LwF arm on a LoRA fine-tune: KL to the frozen base model's logits on the new-task prompts.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Problems outside vision; keep a set of unlabelled representative examples of old tasks; bound old-task performance when outputs are preserved on a different distribution.
- already_run_check: T2 nearly in spirit (distil the model's own prior outputs into the new weights), but on CNN classifiers, with no test of order/context sensitivity or source attribution. T3 partly (replay of old-model responses on current inputs is the "none vs self-generated" contrast without a generator). Others: none.
- transfers: partly — the KL-to-base-model regulariser is standard and cheap with LoRA; the paper's evidence is vision-only.
