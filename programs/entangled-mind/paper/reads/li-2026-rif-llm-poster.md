- what_tested: in-context RIF. Phase 1: study a fictional world with sectors A and B, each with 3-4 named items. Phase 2: elaborate 300-900 words on sector A (the control skips this). Phase 3: free recall of the sector-B items. There are 15 invented worlds with no hits in the pretraining corpus. Also tested: cures given after the elaboration, preventions given before it, the four classic RIF properties, and attention / knockout analyses.
- on_whom: Qwen-2.5-7B and Qwen-2.5-72B instruct; N = 150 at 7B for the main effect; at least 60 per cell.
- how_scored: an LLM judge scores each B item (a Mistral judge was used as a check); "recovery" = the share of the deficit removed.
- result: RIF +11.2 pp at 7B (p < 1e-6), +26 pp at a dose of about 600 words, +24.9 pp at 72B (d = 1.56). Properties at 7B: retrieval specificity (copying -1.1 pp n.s. vs generation -15.4 pp); competition dependence (high overlap +17.4 pp vs low +3.7 pp n.s.); cue independence (+11.5 pp). Dose is a step function near 300 words. Human part-set cuing did NOT transfer (p = 0.83). The cue "list every item by name", given AFTER the elaboration: 100% recovery at 7B, 98% at 72B. The same text given BEFORE: 44% at 7B, -5% at 72B. Preventions fail (warnings 25-30%, n.s.). B-token logits fall by 1.27, and the cure lowers late-layer attention to the original B tokens to 0.42x baseline: "The fix is an addition, not a restoration."
- limits: one model family (Qwen-2.5); fictional-world materials; 200-500-word dose range; cross-architecture generality and per-head circuit patching left for follow-up (poster's scope box). LLM-judge scoring.
- borrow: (1) "bypass, not restore": a recovery of recall can come from a fresh copy in recent context while the original stays suppressed. Any T8 (resurfacing) claim should check whether the original representation came back or a new route was built (probe the original token positions). (2) "Deficit tracks generation, not exposure": our own-vs-other uptake asymmetry may share this cause (self-GENERATED content is privileged), and their copy-vs-generate control is the one to borrow. (3) Their strong-vs-weak item contrast is missing; Anderson et al. 1994 predicts strong items lose more.
- next_steps_it_suggests: cross-architecture tests and per-head circuit patching.
- already_run_check: T5 RUN in context on Qwen2.5-7B (one of our two local models). An in-weights version (LoRA retrieval practice) was not run.
- transfers: yes — the in-context replication on Llama-3-8B (a different family) is $0 and unrun; the in-weights version is the open T5 gap.
