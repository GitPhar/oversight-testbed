# Retrieval-induced forgetting meta-analysis — Kou Murayama, Toshiya Miyatsu, Dorothy Buchli, Benjamin C. Storm, 2014, Psychological Bulletin 140(5)
- url: https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/37052/1/Murayama%20et%20al_2014_PB.pdf (accepted manuscript)
- field: metascience
- read_depth: partial (abstract, introduction on output interference and the RIF properties, methods/coding, overall results, output-interference, materials, retrieval-specificity, delay and individual-difference results; general discussion skimmed)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader F, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: How large retrieval-induced forgetting (RIF) is (practising some items from a category impairs later recall of unpractised items from the same category, relative to baseline items), and which moderators bear on inhibition vs. competition accounts.
- on_whom: 143 papers, k = 512 samples (human; healthy adults, children, older adults, clinical groups).
- how_scored: Hedges' g for Rp- vs. Nrp recall; random-effects models; moderators coded for output-interference control, materials, paradigm, delay, and population; fail-safe N.
- result: Overall g = 0.35 (95% CI 0.32-0.38), I^2 = 63%. Output interference matters most. Studies that did not control output order at test had g = 0.50 (k = 256), while those that did had g = 0.22 (k = 280), roughly halving the effect. The raw RIF is about 11% of items without the control and 4.5-5% with it. Text materials with the control: g = 0.08 (n.s.); highly integrated text g = 0.01. Restudy instead of retrieval: g = 0.07 (n.s.), which supports retrieval specificity. Delay of 24 h or more: g = 0.09 (n.s., k = 38) overall, but 0.40 (k = 21) once text studies are removed. Fail-safe N is 56,111, though fail-safe N is a weak bias test.
- limits: This predates the replication-era bias tests. It uses only fail-safe N (no selection model or PET-PEESE). Much of the headline effect comes from studies that let output interference inflate it. It is not an assessment of mechanism by direct replication.
- borrow: For T5 (RIF in LLMs), the engine must build in the field's own confound, output interference. If the final test lets practised (Rp+) items be produced first, in-context or in a prompt order, any "forgetting" of Rp- items may be output interference or position, not suppression. Pre-register (a) a test that probes Rp- items before Rp+ items, or an independent-cue probe; (b) a restudy arm, since human RIF needs retrieval, not re-exposure, so an LLM "RIF" that appears equally for re-exposure is plain interference, not RIF; and (c) a coherent-text arm, where the human effect is near zero.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Controlled-output designs, independent-probe tests, and more work on delay and integration as boundary conditions.
- already_run_check: none (human; no network). Bears directly on the design of T5.
- transfers: partly — the effect is robust but small once output order is controlled (g about 0.22, about 5% of items) and vanishes for integrated text. An LLM RIF prediction is therefore worth testing only with the output-order and restudy controls; without them a positive result is uninterpretable.
