- what_tested: the DRM paradigm in context: 16 of the original Roediger-McDermott lists studied as one list, then a buffer task (a WHO report excerpt of 1k-32k tokens, standing in for a retention interval), then yes/no recognition of 96 test words, each tested independently.
- on_whom: Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-72B (unquantised Instruct, temperature 1).
- how_scored: discrete yes rate (greedy) and a continuous renormalised p(YES) from the top-20 tokens, for four item types: familiar words, unfamiliar words (bias baseline), familiar lures (false memory), unfamiliar lures (control); Fisher exact and Mann-Whitney tests; logistic slope or Spearman rho on log2 buffer length.
- result: studied words at or near ceiling at every length (Llama 48/48; Qwen at least 46/48). Overt lure false alarms above baseline in Llama-70B (14% vs 2%, OR 7.36) and Qwen-7B (18% vs 7%, OR 2.88, p = .012), not in Llama-8B (5% vs 3%, p = .49) or Qwen-72B. The continuous p(YES) gist signal was present in all four (Llama-8B r = 0.54, lure mean 0.09 vs baseline 0.03; Qwen-7B r = 0.39, lure 0.19). The unfamiliar-lure control was null everywhere. Lure p(YES) rose with context length in Llama-70B (rho = 0.52), Qwen-7B (rho = 0.42) and Llama-8B (rho = 0.37); Qwen-72B showed none. False memory grows while verbatim recognition stays intact, the reverse of the decay story in fuzzy-trace theory.
- limits: (authors') unbalanced item categories from the original lists; trials pooled across lengths and nested in lists, so p-values are optimistic (no mixed model); family and scale confounded; the p(YES) measure needs single-token answers.
- borrow: (1) a ready in-context baseline on BOTH our local model families (Llama 8B and Qwen 7B) with a logit measure that sees sub-threshold gist. (2) The continuous p(YES) with bias baseline and unfamiliar-lure control is the right scoring for any recognition test we run, including a probe-vs-verbal (T1) comparison. (3) Natural next step for T2: consolidate the same lists into LoRA weights (or context-distil) and compare lure vs studied p(YES) to the in-context numbers here (Payne et al. 2009 human prediction: consolidation preserves lures relative to studied words).
