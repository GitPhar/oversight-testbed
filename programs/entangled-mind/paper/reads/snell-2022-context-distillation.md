# Learning by distilling context — Snell, Klein & Zhong, 2022, arXiv preprint
- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.15189
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: partial (abstract, sections 1-3 with Tables 1-3; appendices not read)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader E, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: context distillation. The same model acts as teacher with a rich context (instructions, examples, explanations or a scratchpad) and samples answers on synthetic inputs. It is then fine-tuned as student to give those answers from a minimal prompt. Seven hypotheses tested: internalising instructions; learning from explanations; sequential distillation overwriting earlier distillations (task-id re-assignment); internalising concrete examples (Text-to-SQL) vs plain gradient descent; distilling more examples than fit in context; internalising scratchpad reasoning; transfer of the internalised skill.
- on_whom: TK-Instruct (LM-adapted T5-11B), 10 Natural-Instructions-V2 tasks; 4 classification tasks for task-id overwriting; Incoder on SPIDER (4 and 8 examples); T5-small on 8-digit addition (500 scratchpad examples); TK-Instruct with 10k distillation points mixed with 65,536 NI-V2 examples.
- how_scored: ROUGE-L, exact set match, accuracy. "Correct" vs "wrong" task-id association accuracy (the wrong-id score detects cheating via the input distribution).
- result: internalising instructions: student 9.0 → 34.7 ROUGE-L, teacher 43.4. Explanation in-context margin vs distillation margin r = 0.75. Task-id overwrite: pre 49/48 correct/wrong; naive 68/61; mixed 70/16 (teacher 81). SPIDER, 4 examples: post-distill 22.1 vs direct gradient descent 13.4 (teacher 27.7). Addition: direct answers 0 → 95% (teacher with scratchpad 93; baselines 72 and 61). Mixed into NI-V2 training: direct 8-digit addition 1% → 17% while NI ROUGE-L 57 → 58, i.e. general skill kept when the distillation is mixed with real rehearsal data.
- limits: no order-sensitivity, calibration or consistency measures. Nothing on whether the student knows the content came from context or from itself (no source). Forgetting was checked only by one ROUGE-L number. Preprint; mostly T5 models.
- borrow: this is the T2 operator. Teacher = the model with its earlier transcript in context; student = the same model fine-tuned (LoRA) to give those answers without the transcript. Their "wrong association" control (does the student respond to the cue or to the input distribution?) maps onto our own-vs-other question: after distillation, does an own-labelled planted answer still pull more than an other-labelled one, or is the label ignored? Their mixed-input trick shows that cue-binding needs inputs from several sources in the distillation set.
- next_steps_it_suggests: larger models that learn better from context should make context distillation more useful.
- already_run_check: T2 nearly run on the operator side only. In-context gains were distilled into weights and overwritten sequentially, but no one measured order sensitivity or own-vs-other source after distillation. T1, T3-T8: none.
- transfers: yes — LoRA context distillation of a model's own transcript answers on Llama-3-8B/Qwen2.5-7B in mlx-lm is cheap; the source and order outcome measures would be new.
