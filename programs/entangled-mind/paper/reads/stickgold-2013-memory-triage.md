# Sleep-dependent memory triage — Stickgold & Walker, 2013, Nature Neuroscience 16(2):139-145
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5826623 (NIHMS author manuscript, fetched as full-text XML via NCBI efetch db=pmc id=5826623)
- field: neuroscience
- read_depth: full
- read_by: scout-bmq reader A, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: A perspective, not a new experiment. It argues sleep consolidation is selective ("triage"): salience tags set while awake (emotion, reward, being told of a future test, remember/forget cues) decide what sleep keeps. Sleep then sends kept items down one of three paths: item consolidation, item integration into an existing schema, or multi-item generalization (gist, rules, insight; this includes false memories).
- on_whom: Humans (adults; infants aged 15 months for the artificial-grammar studies) and rodents, through the studies it cites. It reports no new n, and the cited studies' n are not given in the text.
- how_scored: In the cited studies: sleep vs matched wake (or nap vs no nap). Measures: recall/recognition of items, remember-minus-forget recall difference, false recall of DRM lures, transitive-inference accuracy, weather-prediction optimal choices, rate of discovering the hidden shortcut. Physiology (spindles, slow-wave activity) used as correlates.
- result: Transitive inference: 70-75% on inferences after 12 h awake vs 94% on second-order inference (B>E) after 12 h that included sleep; nothing at 20 min. Number-reduction shortcut found by 59.1% after sleep vs 22.7% after wake ("2.6 times more likely"). The 41% of sleepers who missed the shortcut got three times faster at the rote method instead. Weather task: 76 of 100 test trials optimal at immediate test; only the sleep group improved 12 h later. DRM: across sleep, recall of studied words fell but false memory for the gist lure ("SLEEP") did not, whereas across wake both fell. Directed forgetting: the remember-minus-forget recall gap was larger after sleep than after wake, with immediate recall equal in the two groups. Fast spindles (13.5-15 Hz) predicted both more remembered items and fewer forgotten-cue items. Remember/forget tags fade within 24 h if there is no sleep, and fade faster than the item memories themselves.
- limits: A perspective; it lists the inconsistent findings (Diekelmann found a gist benefit only in the poorer half of subjects; Fenn found no benefit with recognition). How tags are made and how gating works is "largely uncharacterized". It does not settle whether tagging is required at all, or which sleep stage does which job. It studies sleep only and sets wake-time offline processing aside.
- borrow: (1) The directed-forgetting design maps directly onto a "consolidation" step for an LLM (context distillation / LoRA on the model's own transcript). Mark half the items in the transcript "will be tested" and half "can forget". Consolidate, then score remember-minus-forget recall. The control is equal immediate in-context recall, as in the cited study. (2) The DRM gist-lure metric. After LoRA on DRM word lists, does the model endorse the never-shown critical lure more than an in-context-only control does? Then check whether studied-item recall falls while lure endorsement holds. That would show semanticisation by consolidation. It is a cheap, falsifiable signature, and it bears on T2 (source and episode are stripped, gist is kept). (3) The tag-decays-faster-than-item finding gives a T2 sub-prediction: after consolidation, metadata such as "this was MY answer" (a tag) fades before the content (the number).
- next_steps_it_suggests: Find how waking salience tags are made and used during sleep, and which stage or oscillation does which kind of integration. Study cycles across the night and across several days, not a single night.
- already_run_check: none on any network. It is the source of the T7 idea (selective, tagged consolidation), but it ran no model.
- transfers: partly. The behavioral designs (tagged remember/forget, DRM lure, transitive inference after "consolidation") can be run as text tasks on Llama-3-8B/Qwen2.5-7B with a LoRA "sleep" step for $0. The spindle and oscillation physiology does not transfer.
