- what_tested: A review of the synaptic homeostasis hypothesis (SHY). Waking learning is net potentiation (costly, lowers signal-to-noise, saturates learning). Sleep is net, activity-dependent DOWN-SELECTION: synapses are scaled down overall, but those strongly and consistently reactivated by spontaneous offline activity are protected, so the "fittest", best-integrated traces win. Sleep should never potentiate, because offline activity is "fantasy" with no reality check. SHY is set against "instructive" active-systems-consolidation (replay potentiates).
- on_whom: Cited rats, mice (YFP-H two-photon spine imaging), Drosophila, cats, humans (TMS-evoked responses), and computer models (Olcese 2010; Hill 2008; Nere 2013; Hashmi 2013). No new data.
- how_scored: Cited markers of synaptic strength across wake and sleep: GluA1-AMPAR levels, slopes of evoked responses, mEPSC frequency and amplitude, spine counts. In models: signal-to-noise, recall, and "matching" (how much brain states driven by the environment differ from those driven by noise).
- result: GluA1-containing AMPARs are 30-40% higher after wake than after sleep (rats). The human TMS-evoked response slope climbs over 18 h awake and returns to baseline after one night's sleep. Fly spines added during 12 h of enriched wake return to baseline only if the fly sleeps. In adolescent mice spine gain dominates wake and loss dominates sleep, with no such effect in adult mice. In models, down-selection raises signal-to-noise and gives consolidation, gist extraction (details in lower areas depressed, invariants in higher areas protected) and integration. Potentiation during sleep ("reactivation then downscaling", or pseudorehearsal) REDUCED signal-to-noise and matching, through strengthening of spurious coincidences. Declarative memories never improve in absolute terms after sleep; sleep only slows forgetting. DRM false memories were present by the end of training and were not created by sleep.
- limits: The markers are indirect (excitability confounds; in vitro vs in vivo). A cat study and the monocular-deprivation data show some potentiation in sleep, which the authors call non-physiological. The role of REM sleep is unresolved. Most wake-sleep changes were measured without specific training.

So run T3 with three arms: dream replay used as SFT data (potentiation), dream replay used only to choose which delta parts to keep (selection), and no sleep. (4) "Sleep slows forgetting but does not raise absolute recall" gives a sharp prediction for any LLM "sleep" step: retention curves should flatten, not rise above the pre-sleep level, for factual items.
