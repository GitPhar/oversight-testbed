# Brain-inspired replay (BI-R) — van de Ven, Siegelmann & Tolias, 2020, Nature Communications 11
- url: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7426273/fullTextXML
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: partial (first ~100k of 235k characters of the PMC full-text XML: introduction, results text, discussion, start of methods; most accuracy values are in figures and were not in the text read)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader C, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: Generative replay made more brain-like: (1) replay-through-feedback (the generator is a VAE folded into the classifier, so one network classifies and generates); (2) conditional replay (Gaussian-mixture prior, one mode per class); (3) gating based on internal context (a different random subset of decoder neurons inhibited per class); (4) internal replay (replay hidden representations, not pixels; conv layers pretrained on CIFAR-10 and frozen); plus distillation (soft targets) for replayed samples. Compared with EWC, online EWC, SI, LwF, standard generative replay, joint training upper bound, and combinations with SI.
- on_whom: Split MNIST (5 tasks, 20 repeats), permuted MNIST (100 tasks, domain-incremental, 5 repeats), split CIFAR-100 (10 tasks of 10 classes, task- and class-incremental, 10 repeats). No language models.
- how_scored: Average test accuracy over all tasks so far, in task-, domain- and class-incremental scenarios.
- result: Lesion numbers in text: permuted MNIST BI-R+SI 0.904 ± 0.005 with replay-through-feedback vs 0.892 ± 0.004 without; CIFAR-100 class-incremental BI-R+SI 0.344 ± 0.002 vs 0.334 ± 0.002. Qualitative (figures): in class-incremental split MNIST only generative replay avoids catastrophic forgetting (EWC and SI fail); standard generative replay beats SI for ~10 permuted-MNIST tasks but degrades after ~15, while BI-R stays above SI at 100 tasks; on CIFAR-100 standard generative replay is worse than the naive baseline in task-incremental, BI-R is best among methods that store no data but far below joint training. Robustness: with only 1 replayed sample per 128 new samples, generative replay stayed competitive (task-IL) and beat non-replay methods (class-IL); a VAE with 10 hidden units per layer (low-quality samples) only moderately hurt; this robustness vanished when the network was re-initialised before each task. Quote: "replaying just a few or low-quality samples can already be enough."
- limits: Authors: internal replay depends on frozen pretrained conv layers; conditional replay and gating need class labels; image-only; only average accuracy measured (no forward/backward transfer); no temporal-sequence structure in replay. No LLM.
- borrow: Two directly usable results for T3: (a) the replay ratio can be tiny (1 replayed per 128 new) — so a T3 LLM run can use a few self-generated samples per batch, keeping laptop cost low; (b) low-quality replay works ONLY when the old knowledge is still in the weights (the re-initialisation control) — replay protects rather than reteaches. That control (re-init/heavily-forgotten adapter, then replay) cleanly separates "protection" from "relearning" and also bears on T8.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Extend to semi/unsupervised settings and other modalities; add sequence structure to replay; test whether generative replay can replace stored episodes in reinforcement learning.
- already_run_check: T3 nearly run (generated replay vs no replay vs joint, on vision; "dream" quality manipulated; not LLM, no real-replay arm matched in size reported in text read). T7: none (replay is class-uniform). Others: none.
- transfers: partly — the replay-ratio and "protection, not relearning" findings carry over as hypotheses; the architecture (VAE folded into classifier) does not.
