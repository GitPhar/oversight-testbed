# Robust fine-tuning of zero-shot models (WiSE-FT) — Wortsman, Ilharco, Kim, Li, Kornblith, Roelofs, Gontijo-Lopes, Hajishirzi, Farhadi, Namkoong & Schmidt, 2022, CVPR (read as arXiv 2109.01903v3)
- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.01903
- field: machine learning
- read_depth: partial (abstract, introduction, method, main results, related work, limitations; long appendix skimmed via search)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader C, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: After fine-tuning a zero-shot model, linearly interpolate in WEIGHT space between the zero-shot weights theta_0 and the fine-tuned weights theta_1: (1 - alpha) theta_0 + alpha theta_1, i.e. shrink the fine-tuning delta toward the base by factor alpha. Compared with standard end-to-end and linear-classifier fine-tuning and with output-space ensembles.
- on_whom: CLIP models (headline ViT-L/14@336px; all CLIP sizes in scatter plots), also ALIGN/BASIC per the abstract; ImageNet plus 5 derived shifts (e.g. ImageNetV2, -R, -Sketch, ObjectNet, -A), 6 further shifts, 7 transfer datasets. No language models.
- how_scored: Top-1 accuracy on the reference (fine-tuning) distribution and average accuracy under distribution shift; alpha swept.
- result: alpha = 0.5 gives +1.6 pp ImageNet and +4 to 6 pp under distribution shift over prior work (figure: +4.5 pp on 5 shifts); 2 to 23 pp robustness gains on six further shifts; +0.8 to 3.3 pp over standard fine-tuning on seven transfer datasets; no extra compute. The interpolation path often has accuracy ABOVE both endpoints (linked to linear mode connectivity). Two-model weight ensembling gave 2-15 pp vs 0.8-1.6 pp reported for 15-model output ensembles elsewhere. Quote: "ensembling the weights of the zero-shot and fine-tuned models (WiSE-FT)".
- limits: Authors: limited to image classification; NLP and detection are future work; optimal alpha per target distribution left open. Measures robustness of the NEW task under shift, and keeps zero-shot behaviour only indirectly; not a continual multi-task forgetting test. Not applied to LLMs in this paper (related work notes Hewitt et al. used output-space ensembles for natural-language generation fine-tuning).
- borrow: This is the T4 operation already validated at scale on vision-language models: scale the fine-tuning delta by alpha. On our setup, scaling a LoRA adapter by alpha is one line in mlx-lm. Copy the alpha sweep (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) and plot the two-axis curve (new-task accuracy vs retained behaviour) instead of a single number; the "above both endpoints" bump is the falsifiable signature to look for.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Extend to NLP and object detection; choose alpha per target distribution.
- already_run_check: T4 nearly run (post-hoc shrinkage of the fine-tuning delta toward the base model, exactly T4's operation, but on CLIP image classifiers, scored by distribution-shift robustness, not on an LLM and not between sequential tasks; no sleep framing). Others: none.
- transfers: yes — the operation needs no training beyond one LoRA run and is directly applicable to Llama-3-8B/Qwen2.5-7B adapters; the outcome on LLM forgetting is what remains to measure.
