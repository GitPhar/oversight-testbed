# Spurious forgetting in continual learning of language models — Junhao Zheng, Xidi Cai, Shengjie Qiu, Qianli Ma, 2025, ICLR 2025

- field: machine learning
- read_depth: partial (abstract, introduction, section 2 motivation experiments, section 3 controlled setup through 3.5 feature perspective, section 5 Freeze summary and the dataset table; theory section and most appendix tables not read)
- read_by: scout-bmq reader F, 2026-11-04
- what_tested: Whether large drops on old tasks during sequential fine-tuning of LMs are lost knowledge or lost "task alignment" (the mapping from knowledge to the task's output format and behaviour), and whether freezing the bottom layers prevents them.
- on_whom: A randomly initialised LM pretrained on a synthetic Biography dataset (200,000 fictitious people, 6 attributes each). LLaMa-2-7B-Chat (safety/AOA alignment, 10 examples). LLaMa-3-8B-Instruct on TRACE continual instruction tuning (8 tasks, 40K) and on ZSRE knowledge editing (10 tasks). Pythia-410M on Concept-1K. 3 seeds for the real-world scenarios.
- how_scored: Old-task accuracy during new-task training; "recovery" accuracy after briefly fine-tuning on half of the old task's data (or on unrelated data) and testing on the other half; loss landscape, weight-update orthogonality, and hidden-state and principal-component shifts per layer.
- result: In the controlled setting, Task 0 accuracy fell from nearly 100% to about 10% within the first 150 steps of Task 1 training, yet it could be recovered, so the knowledge was still there. LLaMa-2-7B-Chat safety fell from 100% to 0% after 10 identity-shift examples, and recovered to about 99% after 10 epochs on 10 self-generated refusal examples. On TRACE with LLaMa-3-8B-Instruct, task accuracy sometimes dropped to zero and then rebounded with later training. Freezing the bottom layers raised sequential fine-tuning accuracy from 11% to 44%, while other CL methods (regularisation, generative replay, merging, gradient-based) peaked at 22%.
- limits: Recovery requires some fine-tuning, so it shows knowledge is recoverable, not that a frozen readout can read it. No linear probe trained before fine-tuning and applied after. The main mechanistic evidence comes from a synthetic setting with a small model trained from scratch.
- borrow: (1) A format-vs-knowledge split is mandatory. Any forgetting outcome in this queue must separate task alignment (format, instruction-following) from knowledge, for example by scoring old-task items both in their original prompt format and in a neutral format, and by a brief "re-alignment" fine-tune on unrelated data to test whether the loss is spurious. (2) Early-step measurement. Most of the drop happens in the first ~150 steps, so checkpoints should be dense early. (3) A layer-freezing baseline is a cheap non-biomimetic competitor for T4 or T7.
- next_steps_it_suggests: Methods that keep task alignment without stored old data; better theory of the orthogonal updates in the bottom layers.
- already_run_check: T1 nearly run. They show knowledge survives while task performance collapses (Llama-3-8B-Instruct and others), but via recovery fine-tuning and feature-shift analysis, not via a fixed linear probe trained before fine-tuning. T8 nearly run: forgotten performance resurfaces after re-alignment on unrelated data. Of T3, generative replay was tried only as a baseline that underperformed.
- transfers: yes — same model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct), same phenomenon. T1 should be framed as the probe-based version of this result. Its novelty is a frozen pre-fine-tuning probe that needs no recovery training, so it must cite this paper as the nearest prior run.
