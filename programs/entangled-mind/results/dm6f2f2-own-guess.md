# dm6f2-f2: does the overseer's format gap survive a template that fixes the number to the model's own guess? (em-dm6f2f2)

Pre-registered in `experiments/dm6f2f2-own-guess.md` (`33d18d7`, D84), seeded from CURBy-RNG pulse 1592560 (`c5f74af`, D85). The design review was fix-first (`71c5089`); its five fixes are D87 (`23a2893`) and the confirmation's one more is D89, confirmed go (`28f05d9`). The data are in `results/dm6f2f2-calls.json` (`7cd951b`). The tables are generated by `experiments/dm6f2f2_own_guess.py --analyse --seed 12892736311446276367` (`results/dm6f2f2-own-guess-tables.md`, `results/dm6f2f2-own-guess.json`). 2,480 fresh single-question calls, **$0.88** of the $1.00 cap at the batch rate, no refusal.

## In plain English

**The question.** An overseer might double-check every answer a model says it is less than 70% sure of. em-dm6f2 found that on Sonnet that list changes with the format the confidence is asked in: a probability from 0 to 1, or "out of 100 such questions". One explanation was the wording. On questions shaped "Is the answer X?", Sonnet sometimes seemed to give the chance that X is right after answering "No", which puts a confident "No" under 70%. This bet re-asked the same 300 questions with both formats reworded the same way: the number is about "YOUR guess above", and for a "No" it is the chance that "No" is right. Each question was asked twice in each format, as before.

**The pre-registered verdict is pass (unreplicated), on Sonnet.** In the pre-registered sentence, with the second sentence replaced as the design review required: "With the number's wording fixed to the model's own guess, an overseer who double-checks every answer for which Sonnet stated a number below 70% would still have checked a substantially different set of questions depending on which of the two formats the number was asked in, by at least the pre-registered margin over two askings in the same format. The rewording did not remove numbers below 0.5 on Yes/No guesses (probability format 0.18, frequency format 0.07), so this result does not separate the old wording's double meaning from other explanations. This design cannot say whether one format flags more questions or other questions." Scored as a hit (outcome 1) at engine odds of 63%.

**What the numbers say.**
- **Sonnet (the rule).** On the 268 questions with a number in all four askings, the two formats' lists overlapped 0.37 [0.28, 0.46] (Jaccard; 1 = the same list). Two askings in the same format overlapped 0.64 [0.55, 0.73]. The gap is +0.27 [+0.18, +0.37], above the 0.10 margin and larger than em-dm6f2's +0.22 with the old wording. It holds with the questions bootstrapped by category (+0.23 to +0.34), and no single question's removal changes it. The probability format flagged about 24% of answers and the frequency format about 15%.
- **The rewording only partly took effect.** The check, fixed before the data, was the share of Yes/No answers given a number below 0.5. With the old wording it was 0.51 (probability) and 0.21 (frequency). With the new wording it is 0.18 and 0.07. So the reading the rewording targeted dropped by about two thirds in the probability format, but not to the 0.10 the rule asked for. That is why the second sentence above says the result does not separate the double meaning from other explanations.
- **The new wording also changed the answers' form.** It turned most answers into Yes/No guesses (Sonnet: 82–92% per asking, against 0–70% by category with the old wording). That change is part of what this bet compared, and the design cannot separate it from the referent fix (§6 of the pre-registration, D87).
- **Haiku (reported, not the rule).** Haiku's lists also differed by format beyond its same-format noise: 0.54 across formats against 0.81 within, a gap of +0.27 [+0.21, +0.34]. With the old wording Haiku's gap was +0.08, under the margin. Its share of "No" science answers below 0.5 stayed high in both formats (0.79 and 0.71).
- **Opus (reported).** Only 20 questions ran (its last two chunks were held back by the worst-case cap check), and Opus flagged almost nothing (6% in each asking). Its lists were identical across formats and askings, so the design could not read it (untestable by em-dm6f2's 40-question floor).

**What it means.** For Sonnet on these 300 questions, an under-70% check picks a substantially different set of answers depending on the confidence format, even when the wording says outright that the number is about the model's own guess. The gap did not shrink when the most suspicious reading was mostly removed; it grew. So the double meaning, as far as this rewording removed it, does not explain the gap. But the rewording also changed the answers to Yes/No, and it did not fully remove the old reading, so the design does not say what does explain it.

**Caveats.**
- **Unreplicated.** One model by the rule, one question pool, two formats, one rewording. Haiku's pass is reported, not tested.
- **Not the same comparison as em-dm6f2.** The answers changed form, and this run is fresh calls on a later day; a larger gap than em-dm6f2's cannot be pinned on any one difference.
- **Cut-off answers.** 76 of Sonnet's 1,200 calls stopped at the 300-token cap (em-dm6's setting), mostly worked calculations; they have no number and leave the four-cell set (268 of 300 remain).

**Novelty:** partly new. That a verbalized-confidence threshold flags different questions when only the prompt changes is in print: Zhao et al. (2025, arXiv:2505.21701), with a "probability that your guess is correct" prompt on open 7–8B models, find that shuffling the answer options leaves the rejected sets overlapping only .61 (Mistral-7B) and .20 (Llama-3.1-8B) on MMLU, and Kim and Kang (2026, arXiv:2605.27752; open models, no Claude) caution that a threshold set under one prompt may not hold under another. In people, calibration depends on the response format (half-range against full-range; Hansson, Juslin and Winman 2003, CogSci Proceedings). No paper found compares a probability format with an "out of 100 such questions" frequency format for model confidence, measures the flagged-set change against two askings in the same format, or fixes the number's referent to the model's own guess; ours does all three on Sonnet 5.5 (gap +0.27, unreplicated) and agrees in direction with the prompt-instability work, but cannot separate the referent fix from the switch to Yes/No answers. Search: WebSearch plus arXiv and eScholarship opens for "verbalized confidence" with "frequency format", "out of 100 similar questions", "confidence-frequency effect", "half-range full-range format dependence", "selective prediction threshold prompt instability", "answer polarity yes/no confidence", "probability that your answer is correct" referent; 2026-11-05. claude-opus-5-5.
