## In plain English

**The question.** An overseer might double-check every answer a model says it is less than 70% sure of. On Sonnet that list changes with the format the confidence is asked in (a probability, or "out of 100 such questions"), even when both formats say the number is about the model's own guess (em-dm6f2f2, pass, unreplicated). This bet asked Opus the same reworded questions. To give Opus a list to compare, 30 of the 40 questions came from those on which Sonnet and Haiku had each given a number below 70% at least once (mostly low numbers on "No" guesses, and where Sonnet's own gap sat); 10 were claim questions. Each was asked twice in each format.

**The pre-registered verdict is untestable.** "Too few questions carried a confidence number in all four cells, or too few were flagged under 70% in any cell, to read the rule on Opus." 34 questions had a number in all four askings, above the floor of 25, but only **5** of them were flagged below 70% in any asking, under the floor of 6 fixed before data (D98: with fewer flags the overlaps are set by one or two questions). Untestable is not scored, so the engine's 0.28 and Sean's 0.65 on this bet are not scored either.

**What the numbers say** (reported, not a verdict).
- **Opus was confident even on these questions.** It put 9–15% of its answers under 70% per asking (P 0.09 and 0.15, F 0.12 and 0.12). The five flagged questions (h058, h065, h150, h237, h253) all sat between 0.50 and 0.75 in every asking, close to the line in both formats.
- **The "No"-guess reading that drove the smaller models' numbers did not appear.** Of Opus's answers guessing "No", none carried a number below 0.5 (0 of 39 in the probability format, 0 of 38 in the frequency format), against 66% of Sonnet's and 95% of Haiku's qualifying numbers on these questions in em-dm6f2f2. Nearly all its guesses were Yes or No (0.99 and 1.00).
- **On five questions, the overlaps say nothing.** Across formats the lists overlapped 0.78 [0.50, 1.00], and between two askings in the same format 0.60 [0.25, 1.00]; the gap D = −0.18 [−0.67, 0.00] points the other way, but with five flagged questions every interval spans most of the scale.

**What it means.** On these 38 questions Opus's under-70% list was too short to compare across formats; the bet cannot say whether its list depends on the format. What it does show is narrower: on questions where Sonnet's and Haiku's lists were driven by low numbers on "No" guesses, Opus gave no such numbers, so the reading that inflated their lists was absent on Opus here. That fits em-dm6f2f2's 20 Opus questions, where Opus also flagged almost nothing.

**Caveats.**
- **Not a fail.** Untestable means the design could not read the rule, not that the gap is absent on Opus; at the dry run's Opus-like setting it came out untestable 24% of the time and fail 37%.
- **Two of the 40 questions did not run** (the worst-case cap check held them back), and four of the 38 lost an answer to the 300-token limit (9 calls at max_tokens). Complete-case analysis leans toward questions without long calculations.
- **Selected questions, one model, one pool, two formats.** The question set was chosen by the other two models' answers (D98), so even a readable result would have spoken to those questions, not to a fresh sample.
