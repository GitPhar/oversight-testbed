# gm2-f1: question order with nothing in the transcript but questions (em-gm2q)

Pre-registered in `experiments/gm2q-questions-only.md` (`2f30696`, D64), seeded from CURBy-RNG pulse 1592278 (`1ba7f8c`). The design review was fix-first (`927cdb4`), with fixes D69 and D70 written before any call and confirmed go (`6a17968`). The data are in `results/gm2q-conversations.json` (`bbf5b71`). The tables below the plain-English section are generated by `experiments/gm2q_questions_only.py --analyse --seed 17882636728661590601` (`results/gm2q-questions-only-tables.md`, `results/gm2q-questions-only.json`). gm2's 10 items, four orders, 10 repeats per cell, on `claude-haiku-5-5` and `claude-sonnet-5-5`. 2,000 conversations, **$2.80** of the $3.50 cap, no refusal, no missing answer.

## In plain English

**The question.** gm2 found that when the model's earlier answers were hidden, the order of the earlier questions still changed its final answer. But "hidden" meant a stand-in line, "[rating recorded]", sat where each answer had been, and that line might itself have caused the effect. This test removes the stand-in and every answer. The four earlier questions go into one message, in each of gm2's four orders, followed by the main question, and the model gives only its final rating. Alongside it, gm2's ordinary turn-by-turn version (the model answers each question and sees its answers) was re-run the same day as the yardstick.

**The pre-registered verdict is mixed: Sonnet passes, Haiku fails.** In the pre-registered sentence: "With nothing in the transcript but the questions, their order still moved the final rating on Sonnet but not on Haiku by the pre-registered margin: whether the gm2 order effect needs the placeholder depends on the model or on the items." Scored as a miss (outcome 0) at engine odds of 10%.

**What the numbers say, model by model.**
- **With only questions in the conversation, the raw order gate is significant on both models** (permutation p = 0.0002 on both, the smallest p the test can give). On Sonnet it stays significant with the flagged answers dropped. On Haiku that cleaned gate cannot be computed, which is the case D70 reads as not settling whether order moves the judgment or the model is rating a listed line. One item carries most of each effect: h261 holds 88% of Haiku's questions-only between-order variance and h191 holds 82% of Sonnet's. Without that item the questions-only effect is 0.60 (Haiku) and 0.80 (Sonnet) times the turn-by-turn one. It is 15.5 points on Haiku against 9.0 turn by turn, and 10.4 points on Sonnet against 5.5.
- **Sonnet passes every part of the rule.** The ratio of the questions-only to the turn-by-turn effect is 1.88 [0.75, 8.38]. Its lower end is above the 0.5 bar, and the order effect stays significant with the suspect answers removed. No single item decides it: leaving any one item out keeps the pass. Without h191, the item with the largest questions-only effect, the ratio is 0.80 [0.73, 1.36] (exploratory).
- **Haiku fails on the rule added by the design review to catch "wrong-target" answers.** Those are answers where the model may have rated the last-listed statement instead of the main question. Its ratio is 1.73 [0.50, 4.99]; the lower end, 0.505, clears the 0.5 bar. Its order effect is significant. But in three cells the check leaves fewer than 2 answers: h261 O2 (0 of 10 kept), h261 O1 (1 kept) and h112 O1 (1 kept). The rule, fixed before any data, counts any one of them as a fail.

**Read this fail carefully.** The flag was added so that a model rating the wrong line could not pass for an order effect. In h261 order O2 it may well be right: the 4th-listed statement is one the model rates 99, and all 10 answers sit at 90–97. But in the same item's order O1, Haiku also answered about 90, and the flag caught 9 of those 10 as well. There the 4th statement is rated 12 and Haiku's usual answer is 9. Those answers are nowhere near the 4th statement; the rule flags them only because they are even further from the usual answer. On h261, Haiku answers high when the statement it rates 99 comes first or last, and low when it sits in the middle. That pattern does not show the model rating the 4th line, but it fits a model rating whichever line comes first or last just as well. In O1 the first statement is the one rated 99, and Haiku's ~90 is also 100 minus the 12 it gives the last statement ('The answer is not November 8, 2022'). The design cannot tell these from an order effect on the judgment. In item h112, the flag also removed answers of 35–45 against a usual 68, inside the range Haiku gives turn by turn (its O2 mean is 34). So Haiku's fail comes from a check that was meant to guard against an artefact, and that here also caught answers that are plausibly real. It stands, because the rule was frozen before the data, but it is not evidence that Haiku's order effect is absent. With the flagged answers dropped and the two items that then lose a cell set aside (exploratory, not the rule), Haiku's ratio is 0.80 [0.66, 0.95].

**What it means.** On Sonnet, by the full pre-registered rule, an order effect at least half the size of the turn-by-turn one remains with nothing in the conversation but questions. It is not the same effect: it sits on different items and orders (pattern correlation 0.16), and 82% of it is one item, h191, where turn by turn there is none. So this shows that removing the placeholder does not remove order effects. It does not show that the turn-by-turn effect itself survives. The design review's statement that a pass needs the effect on the same items (D69) did not hold for this pass. On Haiku the rule does not establish that. Its questions-only gate and the exploratory checks point the same way, but they are not the rule. The bet did not die, so the fail sentence the pre-registration held for that case ("the gm2 pass was the placeholder, and the main paper must say so") does not apply. The main paper can say: question order moved the answer with no answers or stand-in in the transcript on Sonnet (pass, unreplicated, 10 items); on Haiku the order effect was significant but the pre-registered rule failed on its wrong-target check.

**Caveats.**
- **Different orders, different items.** The orders that move the questions-only answer are mostly not the ones that move the turn-by-turn answer. The correlation of order patterns between the two is 0.04 on Haiku and 0.16 on Sonnet. The largest questions-only effects sit on items with little turn-by-turn effect (Haiku h261: 46 points questions-only, 4 turn by turn; Sonnet h191: 30 against 0); turn by turn, the largest were Haiku h112 and Sonnet h058. Hiding the answers leaves an order effect; it does not leave the same one. gm2b found the same with the stand-in.
- **A few big jumps.** Much of the questions-only effect comes from a few cells where the answer jumps from near 5 to near 90: Haiku h261 O1–O2, Sonnet h191 O3. In Sonnet's h191 O3, 7 of 10 answers sit close to the 4th statement's rating (84), so a wrong-target reading cannot be ruled out there. But Sonnet passes without h191.
- **What was removed.** "Nothing but questions" removes a bundle, not one thing. In the questions-only version the model does not see or give sub-answers; it reads one numbered list instead of five turns; and it is told to rate only the last line. The design cannot say which of these matters.
- **Scope.** Two models, 10 items from one pool, one wording. Opus was not run (the job named Haiku and Sonnet; em-dm4's Opus label was one item deep).
- **The redacted chain today.** Re-run on Haiku, it gave an order effect of 11.6 points (gm2 on 4 November: 16.7). Its ratio to turn by turn is 1.29 [0.66, 3.29], so gm2's redacted pass repeats on Haiku today. The questions-only to redacted ratio is 1.33 [0.47, 1.64] (secondary).

Audited: `ledger/audits/em-gm2q.md` (stands, mixed; 5 cuts, four applied here; the fifth was plant pl-030, caught, and is not applied to the real record). The audit also found that the leave-one-item-out cleaned gate reuses the 10-item cell check; with these data it changes no label (audit, row 19).

## Exploratory (written after the data; not pre-registered; never promoted)

From `experiments/gm2q_explore.py`:
- With D69's first, narrower flag (within 5 points of the 4th statement), 3.0% of Haiku's and 0.3% of Sonnet's questions-only answers are flagged, and both cleaned gates give p = 0.0002.
- With D70's flag, dropping the flagged answers on the items where every cell keeps at least 2: Haiku keeps 8 items (h112 and h261 out), R = 0.80 [0.66, 0.95]; Sonnet keeps all 10, R = 0.80 [0.73, 1.37]. Both gates p = 0.0002.
- Without the item with the largest questions-only effect: Haiku without h261, R = 0.60 [0.48, 0.91]; Sonnet without h191, R = 0.80 [0.73, 1.36].
- The flagged cells, answer by answer, are printed by the script (h112 O1 and O4 and h261 O1 and O2 on Haiku; h191 O3 on Sonnet).
