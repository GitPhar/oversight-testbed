# gm2q-f3: question order with only questions in the message, on Opus and on Sonnet's 30 new items (em-gm2q2)

Pre-registered in `experiments/gm2q2-opus-sonnet30.md` (`1e0c9c1`, D80), seeded from CURBy-RNG pulse 1592505 (`74b63b1`, D81). The design review was fix-first (`65c4365`); its four fixes are D82 (`089eebd`), confirmed go (`fe408fc`). The data are in `results/gm2q2-conversations.json` (`0d60004`). The tables are generated by `experiments/gm2q2_opus_sonnet30.py --analyse --seed 8982550231960562870` (`results/gm2q2-opus-sonnet30-tables.md`, `results/gm2q2-opus-sonnet30.json`). 1,520 conversations: Opus on gm2's 10 items and Sonnet on gm2b's 30 further items, four orders, questions-only and turn by turn. **$3.83** of the $4.25 cap at the batch rate, no refusal, no missing answer.

## In plain English

**The question.** em-gm2q put four statements and a question into one message, with no answers in the conversation, and found that the order of the statements still changed the final rating on Haiku and Sonnet. But each model's effect sat mostly on one topic, and Opus was never tested. This bet asked Opus the same question on the same 10 topics, and asked Sonnet on 30 topics it had never seen. Each time, the ordinary turn-by-turn version ran beside it as the yardstick. A pass needed five things on both models: a real turn-by-turn effect, a real questions-only effect, that effect surviving the removal of answers that sit on the first- or last-listed statement, a size at least half the turn-by-turn one, and every one of these still holding with any single topic left out.

**The pre-registered verdict is fail.** In the pre-registered sentence: "With nothing in the message but the questions, neither Opus nor Sonnet met the pre-registered rule (first failing parts: Opus leave-one-item-out, Sonnet wrong-target check)." On Opus, the effect depended on single items (h058, h279); it was the turn-by-turn yardstick that broke without them, not the questions-only effect. On Sonnet, the order effect could not be told apart from answers sitting on the first- or last-listed statement. Scored as a miss (outcome 0) at engine odds of 7%.

**Model by model.**
- **Opus (gm2's 10 items, 4 conversations per cell).** On all 10 items Opus met every part of the rule. Both order effects were significant: turn by turn p = 0.016, questions only p = 0.0006, unchanged with the wrong-target check (it flagged no answer). The questions-only effect was 4.3 points against 3.4 turn by turn, a ratio of 1.26 [0.68, 3.02]. Here, unlike em-gm2q, the two effects sat on the same items and orders (pattern correlation 0.63). But Opus barely moves with order at all: on 5 of the 10 items its mean answer moves by less than 3 points across the four orders, in both versions. Nearly all of the turn-by-turn effect is two items, h058 and h279, and the yardstick is no longer significant with either one left out. By the rule frozen before the data, that is a fail. The questions-only effect itself is also concentrated: h058 holds 78% of it.
- **Sonnet (gm2b's 30 further items, 5 per cell).** Both raw order gates were significant (p = 0.0002 each), and the questions-only effect was about the size of the turn-by-turn one (6.0 against 6.4 points, ratio 0.94 [0.63, 1.34]). It was not concentrated on one item (the largest share was h214, 37%). But it fell on different items and orders from the turn-by-turn effect (pattern correlation 0.04). The wrong-target check flags an answer that is far from what Sonnet gave turn by turn in the same order, and nearer the rating of the first- or last-listed statement. It flagged 9.8% of questions-only answers, against 2.3% of turn-by-turn ones, and left fewer than 2 answers in 10 cells across 5 items (h135, h115, h214, h019, h294; h115 and h214 in three orders each). By the rule, any such cell is a fail. This is the case the pre-registration said the rule would now treat as not separated from rating a listed line (§6).

**What it means.** With only questions in the message, the order of the statements did move both models' final ratings, by the raw gates. But neither model showed it in the form this bet required. On Opus there is almost nothing to move: turn by turn its order effect rests on two topics. On Sonnet's 30 new topics, the questions-only effect is a different effect from the turn-by-turn one, and much of it lands on answers that could be ratings of the first or last statement. So this bet does not extend em-gm2q's Sonnet pass to new topics or to Opus. The project's belief claim ("the number is built by the asking") still has no clean questions-only result beyond em-gm2q's one-model, 10-item pass.

**Caveats.**
- **What failed is the rule, not the gates.** Both models' raw questions-only gates were at or near the floor of the test. A reader who dropped the wrong-target check and the leave-one-out requirement would see a questions-only order effect on both. Both were frozen before data to stop exactly that reading (the em-gm2q audit).
- **Opus's fail is about the yardstick.** The leave-one-out sets that broke failed on the turn-by-turn gate, not on the questions-only parts. A bigger Opus item set (most of gm2b's 30 items sit near 0 or 100 for Opus, if em-dm2's formats are a guide) would be needed to tell more.
- **Sonnet, levels as well as order (secondary, not tested).** On several mid-scale items Sonnet's questions-only answers sit well below its turn-by-turn answers in every order (h115: 60, 86, 68, 34 turn by turn against 10, 40, 30, 15; h294: 69, 72, 53, 55 against 36, 48, 21, 40). The design compares order effects, not levels, and cannot say why.
- **Differences from em-gm2q** (§6): models, items, repeats, the Batches API, the two-ended same-order flag, leave-one-out in the rule. A result different from em-gm2q's on Sonnet cannot be pinned on any one of these.
- **Timing.** Questions-only answers came from round 1's batch and turn-by-turn finals from round 5's, about 15 minutes later the same day.
- Scope: two models, one item pool, one wording. "Every", "all" and "models" are not claimed.
