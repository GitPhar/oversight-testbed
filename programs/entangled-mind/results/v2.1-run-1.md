## Reading

- **Primary: not met. The honest-hedge quantum encoding ties its classical twin.** On the locked test set, A2.1 scores 0.84 [0.75, 0.90] and F2.1 scores 0.82 [0.76, 0.88]. The paired difference is +0.017 [−0.064, +0.095].
- **The honest hedge did not help.**
  - Both fits drove the hedge-uncertainty weight λ_h to **0**, so neither uses the separate uncertainty term. Only the neutral π/2 orientation of no-lean claims remains.
  - A2.1 is indistinguishable from v2's A2 (paired +0.002 [−0.042, +0.047]).
  - F2.1 is slightly *worse* than F2 (−0.021 [−0.044, −0.004]).
- **No effect of noise was detected here (Addendum C).** A2.1 scores the same on the noisy `ibm_fez` simulator and noiseless (paired +0.000 [−0.020, +0.023]). A2.1 chose no evolution steps (N = 0) and small couplings (k_sup = −0.15, k_con = 0.39), so its circuit is shallow and close to a product state.
- **Run-2 held-out set (secondary): A2.1 0.84 vs F2.1 0.72,** paired difference +0.116 [+0.013, +0.225]. This repeats v2's secondary pattern (A2 0.82 vs F2 0.71). Almost all of it comes from SimpleQA items (0.92 vs 0.67).
  - With N = 0 and weak couplings, the quantum model's distinctive ingredient is its tilted readout basis (α = 0.40 rad), which mixes each claim's amplitude and phase in the measurement. That is a property of single-claim geometry, not of entanglement.
  - This is a secondary set, and it is the pool that motivated v2.1 (D14), so it is not an independent test.
