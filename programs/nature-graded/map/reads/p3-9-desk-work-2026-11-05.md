## In plain English

The test P3-9 runs was written for a language model in program 1 and found nothing there. Before pointing it at the chip, where physics says the effect must be there, two things had to be true: the test had to be the exact one its authors define, and it had to say "contextual" on a published experiment that everyone agrees showed the effect. Both now hold. The code is the authors' own linear program, it agrees with the older exact criterion on 450 random systems where both apply, it says "noncontextual" on 60 made-up classical systems even when they were given deliberate context-dependent errors, and on the published ion-trap numbers of Kirchmair et al. 2009 it says "contextual" however the unpublished parts of that data are filled in.

## What was read in full

- **Dzhafarov & Kujala 2016, "Contextuality-by-Default 2.0: Systems with Binary Random Variables"** (arXiv:1604.04799v4), all 17 pages, PDF from arXiv. What the code takes from it:
  - Definition 1: a multimaximal coupling is maximal on every subset of a connection.
  - Theorem 1 and Corollary 1: for binary variables, the multimaximal coupling of a connection exists, is unique, and puts mass only on the k + 1 "threshold" strings (sorted by Pr[R = 1]), with masses p1, p2 − p1, …, 1 − pk.
  - Definitions 2–3: a system is noncontextual iff it has a coupling whose connection subcouplings are multimaximal.
  - Theorem 2: for binary variables, multimaximality can be checked on adjacent sorted pairs.
  - Theorem 3: every subsystem of a noncontextual system is noncontextual. So if one order's six-context system is contextual, the system with order folded into context is too.
  - Section 4: for connections of two variables (the six-context PM system with order fixed), CbD 2.0 and CbD 1.0 coincide.
- **Kujala & Dzhafarov 2019, "Measures of contextuality and noncontextuality"** (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 377:20190149, arXiv:1903.07170), sections 1–6 in full, 7–8 read. What the code takes from it: the reduced vector p = (p_l, p_b, p_c) (§3.1–3.9; §3.8: connection k-marginals with k > 2 are redundant); the matrix M (§4.3); noncontextual iff M x = p* for some x ≥ 0 (eq. 29); **CNT2 = min ‖p*_b − M_b x‖₁ over x ≥ 0 with M_l x = p*_l and M_c x = p*_c** (eqs. 38–40), the L1 distance of the observed bunches from the noncontextuality polytope.

## The code: `experiments/cbd_lp.py`

The LP's columns are restricted to products of the connections' threshold strings (3⁹ = 19,683 for a six-context PM system, against 2¹⁸ = 262,144). By Theorem 1 this is exact, not an approximation, because every x meeting M_l x = p*_l and M_c x = p*_c has zero mass elsewhere. `full_lp=True` builds the unrestricted LP for checking. Encoding: 1 means the eigenvalue −1 was recorded. Whether the LP is feasible does not depend on the encoding, but CNT2's size does.

Checks run on 2026-11-05, scratch scripts (the committed `--check` of P3-9's runner repeats them):

## The Kirchmair check (the map's debt)

Kirchmair et al. 2009 (Nature 460:494, arXiv:0904.1655) published, for the singlet, every observable's mean in each of its two contexts and the six line products (transcribed by Massé 2021, arXiv:2105.13821, §XI.D, eqs. 75–92 and 104–109, stored in `cbd_lp.KIRCHMAIR_MEANS` and `KIRCHMAIR_PRODUCTS`). The pair correlations inside each context were **not** published, so a bunch distribution is not fixed by them. The check therefore asks the strictest question the published numbers allow: is the system contextual under **every** completion? The smallest CNT2 over all completions is itself one linear program, because CNT2 is an LP in the bunch vector and the bunch vector is linear in the free cells (`cbd_lp.completion_bounds`).

| quantity | this code | published |
|---|---|---|
| χ (s_odd of the line products) | 5.463 | 5.463 (Massé eq. 112), 5.46(4) (Kirchmair) |
| Δ0 | 0.166 | 0.166 (Massé eq. 74) |
| Massé's CNTX, recomputed (his formula, not exact CbD) | 0.565 | 0.566 (eq. 115; rounding of his inputs) |
| **exact CbD 2.0 LP, smallest CNT2 over every completion** | **0.141 > 0: contextual under every completion** | none (no exact LP on these data was found) |
| CNT2 of the maximum-entropy completion | 0.141 | none |

So the exact LP agrees with Massé's adapted formula on the sign: Kirchmair's ion data stay contextual after the CbD correction. This is the "sees it where it is" check on a published experiment, done before any chip data. It is also, as far as the search below found, the first exact CbD 2.0 LP run on published PM hardware data. That claim is about a desk calculation, not a finding of this program.

## Prior work line for P3-9 (rule 13, brought up to date)

> **Prior work:** On every platform tried, Peres–Mermin exceeds its noncontextual bound of 4 in χ:
> - two trapped ions, each observable read out sequentially: 5.46(4) (Kirchmair et al. 2009, arXiv:0904.1655);
> - Google's Willow superconducting chip with mid-circuit measurement and reset: 5.618 ± 0.005 (Kumar et al. 2025, arXiv:2512.02284; no CbD or marginal analysis);
> - IBM's Heron r2 ibm_kingston, product-only readout: 5.31, no error bars (Kelleher & Holweck 2025, arXiv:2505.21243);
> - 2019-era 5-qubit IBM devices: about 4.99 (Laghaout et al. 2022, arXiv:2009.10751).
>
> The only CbD treatment of PM hardware data is Massé 2021 (arXiv:2105.13821, unrefereed). His adapted, not exact, formula leaves Kirchmair's data contextual with a margin of 0.566. The exact CbD 2.0 LP gives CNT2 ≥ 0.141 on the same published numbers (this note). **Prior work predicts a pass for both input states.** No exact CbD LP has been applied to any PM data taken on superconducting hardware, and no CbD analysis of sequential superconducting PM data was found. What this bet adds: the exact CbD 2.0 LP with a CNT2 bootstrap on sequential mid-circuit-measurement data from an IBM Heron r2 (ibm_fez), in both measurement orders, for two input states. It is an instrument check of program 1's method where physics fixes the answer, not a new physics claim; a fail would be about the device's measurement disturbance, never about quantum theory.
