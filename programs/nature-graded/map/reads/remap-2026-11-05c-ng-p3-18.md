## 2. Papers read

### Randomized compiling on a noisy superconducting processor. Hashim, Naik, Morvan et al., 2021, Phys. Rev. X 11, 041039, https://arxiv.org/html/2010.00215v2
- read_depth: full (HTML v2, both parts; `paper/novelty.md` finding 11 had cited it from the abstract)
- what_tested: whether randomized compiling (RC) turns coherent error into stochastic Pauli noise, and whether that makes circuit outcomes predictable from benchmarked error rates.
- on_whom: four transmons (Q4 to Q7) of an eight-qubit AQT@LBNL processor (Trailblazer8-v5.c2) with cross-resonance CNOTs. It is not IBM hardware. The circuits were:
  - single-qubit tomography after 50 random gates (12 randomizations);
  - a four-qubit QFT with 13 CNOTs (50 randomizations);
  - random Clifford+T circuits (100 per depth; 20 randomizations × 200 shots).
- how_scored: unitarity RB for the coherent share of error, cycle benchmarking and cycle error reconstruction (CER), and total variation distance (TVD) against the ideal output.
- result:
  - **Coherent share of CNOT error:** 2.0 to 2.9 × 10⁻² of a total RB infidelity of 3.3 to 4.4 × 10⁻².
  - **Single-qubit tomography:** TVD fell from 0.073 to 0.008 in the Z basis and from 0.170 to 0.029 in the X basis. Fidelity went from 0.862 to 0.879, while purity fell from 0.938 to 0.881: RC trades coherent error for mixedness.
  - **QFT:** more than 81% of inputs improved, with a mean TVD ratio of about 1.9. Random circuits improved by about 1.7×.
  - **Prediction:** a Pauli model from CER predicted RC circuits to about 10%. A complete model (with coherent errors) predicted bare circuits unreliably.
  - **Drift:** tune-up parameters drifted, so CER maps differed between experiments.
  - **ZZ:** residual ZZ on spectators; one large ZZ (Q5 during the (7, 6) CNOT) stayed unexplained.
- limits: one lab's four-qubit device. The simulations assume no correlated gate errors, and the authors report that CER showed none significant. RC's benefit falls as the coherent share falls and at long depth. Fidelity shows no general benefit.
- borrow: the twirled-versus-bare design, and RC's sign in tomography (purity down, fidelity about level), as the predicted signature in P3-20 below.
- next_steps_it_suggests: RC for variational algorithms and in the fault-tolerant era (both untested); targeted tune-up gives diminishing returns.
- predicts_for_us:
  - P3-20 (twirled arm): the rotation-type term is converted to blur, so it is no longer needed. **Pass**, for the part of the coherent error that sits in the twirled two-qubit layers.
  - P3-19: no bearing.
- Quote: "converting coherent errors into stochastic noise"
- **Already shown.** Coherent and stochastic error coexist on a superconducting processor, and the coherent part drifts between experiments. Finding 11's verdict stands. Its description needs two corrections: the device is AQT@LBNL, not a vendor's chip, and the comparison is with the authors' own benchmarked Pauli model, not a vendor noise model.

### Estimating coherent contributions with cycle error reconstruction. Carignan-Dugas, Ranu, Dreher, 2024, Quantum 8, 1367, https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09945v3
- read_depth: full (the main text, Appendices A to C and the references). The ng-p3-5c novelty file had the abstract only.
- what_tested: the method folds a hard cycle x times. Coherent error grows as x² and decoherent error as x, so fitting both separates the coherent part of a CNOT-plus-idle-spectator cycle.
- on_whom: ibmq_guadalupe, ibmq_manila and ibmq_montreal (fixed-frequency, cross-resonance; not Heron), plus a simulator. 30 random compilations per length, lengths 4 to 32, folds of 1 to 9 CNOTs, 10,000 to 20,000 shots.
- how_scored: a 12-parameter global least-squares fit, R² 0.894 to 0.990.
- result: the ancilla's coherent error is Z-only. X and Y are consistent with zero on every device. Coherent Z error is 0.00081(9), 0.0035(6) and 0.0008(1) against other error of 0.0029(5), 0.004(1) and 0.0055(8) on montreal, guadalupe and manila. The authors attribute it to static ZZ, which causes "both coherent and incoherent errors".
- limits: one CNOT type and one idle ancilla; no repeats on different days; no correction experiment. The decoherent and constant terms are strongly anti-correlated. Competing interest: Keysight / True-Q.
- borrow: folding as a way to separate coherent from decoherent error without tomography (not needed here). "Coherent is mostly ZZ" is the reason two-qubit twirling should reach most of the rotation term.
- next_steps_it_suggests: knowledge-based DD and compiled compensation.
- predicts_for_us:
  - P3-20: if the rotation term is mostly ZZ, then **pass**.
  - P3-19: no bearing.
- Quote: "the nature of the coherent error is predominantly Pauli ZZ error."
- **Already shown** (on IBM chips): a coherent ZZ-type part sits beside a larger decoherent part.

### Suppressing correlated noise via context-aware compiling. Seif, Liao, Tripathi et al. (IBM), 2024, ISCA 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06852v2
- read_depth: full (100,000 of 102,469 characters; the unread tail is the end of the references)
- what_tested: correlated coherent errors on IBM ECR devices, and compilers that suppress them with context-aware DD or compensation.
- on_whom: ibm_nazca, ibm_brisbane, ibm_sherbrooke and ibm_penguino1. The paper does not name the generation. They are fixed-coupler ECR devices, not Heron's CZ.
- how_scored: Ramsey-style fidelity against depth, and layer fidelity.
- result:
  - **Error sources:** always-on ZZ, spectator ZZ during ECR, Stark-shift ZZ (about 20 kHz on ibm_nazca), charge-parity ZZ of random sign, and next-nearest-neighbour ZZ (about 0.1 kHz, rising to about 10 kHz near collisions).
  - **Layer fidelity** of a 10-qubit layer: bare 0.648 ± 0.058, DD 0.743, context-aware DD 0.822, context-aware compensation 0.881 ± 0.002.
  - **Twirling** converts these errors to stochastic noise. A known coherent error can then no longer be undone by its inverse.
- limits: no Heron and no tunable couplers; no time stability; one tested layer.
- borrow: P3-5c's rotation term may be spectator or context ZZ from the other qubits in the 14-qubit circuits, not error on the pair itself. P3-5c's spectator check found that 0.1 rad of spectator ZZ is detected as rotation on h268 (20 of 20 draws).
- next_steps_it_suggests: suppress known coherent error before twirling; avoid bad contexts at scheduling.
- predicts_for_us:
  - P3-20: two-qubit twirling with the "active-accum" strategy tailors idle spectator ZZ too: **pass**. Charge-parity ZZ is already stochastic, so twirling leaves it as blur.
- Quote: "A prevalent two-qubit crosstalk error in superconducting architectures is the always-on ZZ interaction."
- **Rival named:** spectator or context ZZ as the source of the rotation term. Neither P3-19 nor P3-20 separates it from on-pair error. P3-20 removes both, which is why its event is about removal, not location.

### Recovering coherent errors from randomized-compiling labels. Vyas, 2026, arXiv 2607.26756v2 (fetched directly; v1 29 Jul, v2 19 Aug 2026)
- read_depth: full
- what_tested: whether keeping each shot's twirl label lets coherent error be estimated from RC data.
- on_whom: IBM hardware.
  - Single-qubit test on ibm_marrakesh, 2026-07-21, 1,620 circuits at 3,000 shots.
  - Two-qubit pair (qubits 53, 54) and a star (qubits 4, 3, 5), 2026-08-16.
  - The paper calls ibm_marrakesh "127-qubit Eagle r3". Qedma's 2508.10997 (below) calls ibm_marrakesh "an IBM Heron device", so the two papers disagree.
- how_scored: θ̂ = arcsin(E[s·b]/v) against an injected phase.
- result:
  - **Injected phase:** 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 rad were recovered as 0.0493, 0.0996, 0.1985 and 0.2978; the null gave 0.0002, and the largest deviation was 0.0063 rad. With the labels discarded, the same shots gave "no usable signal".
  - **Static ZZ, κ:** not resolved. The estimates were +0.004 [−0.028, +0.035], +0.002 and +0.010 rad, against a sensitivity of about ±0.02 to 0.03 rad.
  - **8 µs idle:** visibility loss grew with the number of pulsed neighbours, read as drive-induced leakage. It was not quantified.
- limits: two-qubit coupling and leakage are validated only at the recovery-circuit level; no Cramér–Rao converse; cruder than GST.
- borrow (P3-20): twirl with explicit Pauli frames and keep the labels. Then the pooled counts test "removed", and the labelled counts can say whether the coherent error was hidden or truly gone (a secondary).
- next_steps_it_suggests: more shots; choosing the twirl group as experiment design; pseudo-twirling for calibration.
- predicts_for_us:
  - P3-20: pooled twirled counts show no coherent signal: **pass**.
- Quote: "What remains is more shots, not more theory."
- **Design to borrow.**

### Tunable coupling architecture for fixed-frequency transmons. Stehlik, Zajac, Underwood et al. (IBM), 2021, Phys. Rev. Lett. 127, 080505, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07746 (text extracted locally)
- read_depth: full
- what_tested: a bus-below-qubits tunable coupler, with an adiabatic CZ made by flux-pulsing the coupler.
- on_whom: 11 isolated two-qubit devices, not a processor.
- how_scored: interleaved RB, and stability over 19 to 20 hours without recalibration.
- result:
  - **ZZ:** below 10 kHz with the coupler off.
  - **Best gate:** a 46 ns CZ with error per gate 0.0015 ± 0.0001.
  - **Calibration:** the CZ is calibrated by choosing the pulse amplitude that gives a π phase difference, then applying software Z corrections. A miscalibrated amplitude is therefore a coherent ZZ or Z error.
  - **Stability:** excursions at worst 60% worse; "good stability for at least 24 hours".
- limits: isolated pairs. The paper does not say this is ibm_fez's coupler, and has no spectators.
- borrow: nothing for these follow-ups.
- predicts_for_us:
  - P3-20: the CZ's calibrated phase is the kind of coherent error that twirling tailors: weakly **pass**.
  - P3-19: no bearing.
- Quote: "the calibration is stable over one day."
- **Changes nothing.** These are isolated test devices. One day's stability says nothing about ibm_fez over two days, where the rotation LRs changed size (250.8 against 183.6, and 271.5 against 237.6).

### Readout-error-mitigated state tomography on superconducting qubits. Aasen, Di Giovanni, Rotzinger, Ustinov, Gärttner, 2024, Commun. Phys. (DOI 10.1038/s42005-024-01790-8), https://arxiv.org/html/2312.04211v2
- read_depth: full (100,000 of 104,800 characters; the unread tail is references)
- what_tested: tomography of the state together with tomography of the detector (Pauli-6 POVM), against plain tomography, under induced readout noise: amplifier, photon number, effective T1/T2, detuning and temperature.
- on_whom: one KIT transmon (T1 30 µs, T2 28 µs) and 25 Haar-random states, at 80k shots per basis.
- how_scored: reconstruction infidelity.
- result:
  - **Mitigation:** cut infidelity by 5 to 30 times depending on the noise; about 3 to 4 times for the T1/T2 manipulation, about 4 times across detunings, and about 30 times for combined noise.
  - **Detector calibration shots:** 1k shots already halve infidelity, and the curve flattens near 100k.
  - **The POVMs:** carry coherent, off-diagonal errors, mainly in the x and y bases, under induced noise. The z basis shows none beyond statistical noise. Temperature acts as state-independent depolarizing error.
- limits: one qubit. It assumes perfect preparation, and drift was small in this experiment. Correlated multi-qubit readout is not addressed.
- borrow:
  - P3-19 needs a stated caveat: P3-17's per-qubit classical knobs cannot represent coherent readout error in the x and y measurement bases. Such an error could pose as part of the rotation term, which on h088 rests mostly on settings YZ and ZX.
  - The 5 November job's calibration (1,000 shots per prepared state) is at the low end of this curve. That supports the audit's third item, resampling A, as a secondary.
- next_steps_it_suggests: multi-qubit tests, overlapping tomography, and recalibration on drift.
- predicts_for_us:
  - P3-19: classical readout knobs absorb little of a coherent or state-dependent error: **pass**, meaning both terms survive.
- Quote: "observed decreases in readout infidelity by a factor of up to 30"
- **Rival named:** coherent readout error in the measurement bases. Neither follow-up excludes it. P3-20 does not touch measurement because measurement twirling stays off, so a rotation term that survives twirling there would point at it.

### Detecting and tracking drift in quantum information processors. Proctor, Revelle, Nielsen et al., 2020, Nat. Commun. 11, 5396, https://arxiv.org/html/1907.13608v3
- read_depth: partial (the first 100,000 of 173,190 characters: the main text and methods)
- what_tested: spectral analysis of per-shot time series ("clickstreams"), with a Bonferroni-controlled test, and time-resolved GST.
- on_whom: one trapped-ion ¹⁷¹Yb⁺ qubit and simulations. No superconducting data.
- result:
  - **Ramsey:** detuning drifted by about ±0.5 Hz over 8 hours, tracking lab temperature (Spearman 0.92).
  - **GST:** the idle gate's z-axis coherent error dominated and drifted, about 25% in diamond distance. After drift control it fell about 10 times.
- limits: one platform. Confidence regions hold only within the model.
- borrow (P3-20): save per-shot memory in time order. `data/p3-18-raw.json` holds counts only, so within-job drift cannot be tested on the archive.
- predicts_for_us: coherent errors are the kind that drift, which is consistent with the job-to-job change in rotation size. No bearing on either pass rule.
- Quote: "Only long circuits containing repeated applications of" [the idle gate drifted]
- **Design to borrow.**
