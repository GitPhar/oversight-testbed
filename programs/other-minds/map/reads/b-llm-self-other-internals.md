# Cluster B: self versus other inside and across language models

Reading pass, 2026-11-04. Web reading only: no model was run, no API was called. **How to read the depth labels.** Most "full" reads went through WebFetch, which passes the whole arXiv HTML text, or the first 100,000 characters plus a second pass at an offset, to a summariser that pulled out the record fields. Two PDFs were extracted with `pdftotext` and read line by line (Jamali et al. 2023; Aoki & Kawahara 2025). Every number below comes from those passes. When a number appears only in a figure, the record says so and gives no value. 22 papers: 15 full, 5 partial, 2 abstract only.

## Cluster summary

**What this literature has settled.** Open-weight models from about 7B parameters up hold linearly decodable information about whether a story character's belief is true or false. They also hold information about the true world state, which these papers call the "oracle" or "self" belief. The two sit in different places and fail in different ways. World-state information appears early, is decodable even in Pythia-70m (95%), and holds up when the prompt changes. Character-belief information appears in middle layers, depends on model size and fine-tuning, and breaks under prompt variations (Zhu et al. 2024; Bortoletto et al. 2025). Steering along these directions changes false-belief answers (Zhu: Mistral-7B Forward-Belief "Both" 0.31 → 0.58; Bortoletto: Llama-2-13B-chat Backward-Belief 31 → 87 with CAA). The circuit-level account is pointer-based "lookbacks" over the transcript, built from ordering IDs, binding, and visibility. A character's belief is retrieved by pointing back into the text, not stored as a separate summary (Prakash et al. 2025). On the introspection side, the self-versus-other-model control is now standard and the results are mixed. Fine-tuned self-prediction beats a cross-trained other model by about 13–17 points on simple properties (Binder et al. 2025). Without fine-tuning and with model similarity controlled, there is no self-advantage on grammaticality, word prediction, or temperature (Song, Hu & Mahowald 2025; Song et al. 2025). Probes on a model's own hidden states beat probes on a peer model's only on items where the two models disagree, only for factual recall, and only slightly (+0.015 to +0.034 AUC), with none for math (Ashuach et al. 2026). Under concept injection, Claude Opus 4/4.1 report an injected concept about 20% of the time with no false positives (Lindsey 2026). Self-recognition of one's own text exists. In Llama3-8b-Instruct it runs through a causal residual-stream direction whose Tuned-Lens tokens are "my/I" versus "their/other" (Ackerman & Panickssery 2025), and it goes with self-preference (Panickssery et al. 2024). Kumaran et al. (2025) find an own-versus-other asymmetry on Gemma 3 12B that matches program 1's planted-answer result: the choice-supportive effect of a visible earlier answer disappears when that answer is credited to another LLM.

**What it has not looked at, and what our instruments can reach cheaply.** No paper tests whether the model's representation of its *own* belief and its representation of *another agent's* belief share a direction or a circuit. The "self" in Zhu and Bortoletto is the omniscient narrator, which is the same as the world truth. No item has the model's own belief differ from the truth, so "self" and "fact" are confounded, and nobody has computed a cosine or a cross-perspective probe-transfer score. The one direct test of a shared mechanism is Aoki & Kawahara (2025). A linear map from a third-person false-belief representation to a first-person representation of a redacted story has a causal but small effect: about 0.2 net flip rate at best, on roughly 40 test items. Their "perspective-taking" condition is literally the model reading its own transcript with the unseen events deleted, which is our redaction instrument in another form. Nobody has run program 1's manipulations (question order, hidden versus shown earlier answer, six elicitation formats, judge × author interaction) on third-person belief attribution, or checked whether a probe's belief readout moves with order and format the way the verbal answer does. Four gaps are cheap to reach. (i) Matched first-person and third-person item pairs through the existing API instruments, testing whether the order effect, the format dependence, and the reliance on the memory record show up in attributions to a story character as they do in self-reports. (ii) On a laptop model (Qwen2.5-7B / Llama-3.1-8B / Gemma), cross-perspective probe transfer and cosine between "I believe" and "Noor believes" directions, plus the Aoki redaction-projection intervention at 7–8B scale. (iii) Ackerman's self-authorship direction as a candidate mediator of the own-versus-other uptake gap: project it out and see whether own − other shrinks. (iv) Ashuach's disagreement-subset trick added to our 2 × 2 judge × author design, so shared correctness cannot mask a privileged-access premium, applied both to self-report and to belief attribution.

## B1. Zhu, Zhang & Wang 2024, "Language Models Represent Beliefs of Self and Others"

- **Citation:** Wentao Zhu, Zhining Zhang, Yizhou Wang. ICML 2024 (PMLR 235). arXiv:2402.18496 (v3 read).
- **Read depth:** Full. Main text, then a second pass over the remaining 63k characters, which turned out to be mostly prompt templates and per-condition answer-change counts.
- **What was tested:** Logistic-regression probes on each attention head's output at the last token of (story + belief statement). They decode (a) the protagonist's belief status, (b) the "oracle" belief status, where the oracle is an omniscient observer, so in practice the truth of the statement given the full story, and (c) a 4-way joint label. Then inference-time intervention (ITI) on the top-K = 16 heads along +protagonist, −oracle, or the joint +T_pF_o direction (protagonist True, oracle False).
- **On whom:** Mistral-7B-Instruct, plus DeepSeek-LLM-7B-Chat. BigToM (Forward Belief, Forward Action, Backward Belief; true- and false-belief items); ToMi for transfer.
- **Result:** Oracle belief is decodable from many middle-layer heads; the top head (14, 31) reaches 97.8% (95% CI 97.5–98.1). Protagonist belief is near chance in most heads; the best head (10, 16) reaches 78.3% in the appendix table (the main text says "over 80%"). The joint 4-way probe peaks at 79.0% (head 12, 27), against 25% chance. Steering Mistral-7B on Forward Belief (TB / FB / Both): baseline 0.95 / 0.33 / 0.31; +protagonist 0.96 / 0.30 / 0.29, which does nothing; −oracle 0.84 / 0.49 / 0.41; +T_pF_o 0.85 / 0.66 / 0.58. Backward Belief FB 0.22 → 0.44, TB 0.91 → 0.61. Probe accuracies on BigToM and ToMi correlate at 0.86. MMLU 57.5 → 57.7.
- **Limits (authors):** Two 7B models, one benchmark family. The authors warn against reading the results as evidence of self-awareness. **Ours:** "Self" here is the oracle, which equals the world state; the paper never has the model's own belief differ from the truth. No cosine or overlap analysis between the oracle and protagonist directions is reported. Steering helps when it moves the *oracle* direction or the joint direction, not when it moves the protagonist direction. That suggests false-belief answers depend on suppressing the model's own world-state reading, a relational computation, rather than on a free-standing "other's belief" variable.
- **Borrow:** The per-head probe sweep. The joint 4-way label (agree / disagree between the two perspectives) as a target. The ITI recipe (top-K heads, α × std). The finding that −oracle steering moves false-belief answers.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* how belief representations develop during training; MoE and larger models; better ToM methods. *Ours:* (1) Break the self = truth confound. Build items where the model has been given a wrong prior, or a planted "own earlier answer" (program 1's instrument), so that "my belief", "the protagonist's belief", and "the truth" can all come apart. Probe all three. (2) Compute cosine and cross-probe transfer between the oracle and protagonist directions at matched heads. (3) Run program 1's question-order manipulation and test whether the protagonist-head readout shifts with order the way the verbal rating does. Feasible on a laptop with a 7B model.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Partly.** The probe and steering machinery transfer directly to a laptop model. The "self" label does not mean what our seed question needs.

## B2. Bortoletto, Ruhdorfer, Shi & Bulling 2024/2025, "Benchmarking Mental State Representations in Language Models" / "Brittle Minds, Fixable Activations"

- **Citation:** arXiv:2406.17513 (v3, May 2025, retitled "Brittle Minds, Fixable Activations: Understanding Belief Representations in Language Models"). A search snippet places it at EMNLP 2025 Findings; we did not check that against the proceedings.
- **Read depth:** Full (first 100k of 107k characters; the rest is references and appendix).
- **What was tested:** Residual-stream linear probes for oracle and protagonist beliefs across sizes and fine-tuning stages. A low-dimensional check (probes on the top-k PCs; permuted-label controls). Robustness to prompt variations: adding the Initial Belief, misleading statements, random tokens, time specification. Steering by contrastive activation addition (CAA) compared with ITI.
- **On whom:** 12 models: Pythia 70M–12B plus Pythia-6.9B-chat; Llama-2 7B/13B/70B plus their chat versions. BigToM Forward Belief for probing; Forward Action and Backward Belief for steering transfer.
- **Result:** Oracle belief forms early and approaches 100% (Pythia-70m 95%). Protagonist belief emerges in intermediate layers and scales with size (Llama2-13B about 80%, Pythia-12B about 60%). Fine-tuning helps small models most (+29 Llama2-7B-chat, +26 Pythia-6.9B-chat) and Llama2-70B only marginally. Permuted-label probes stay at chance; top-k PCs recover most of the accuracy (low-dimensional). **Robustness asymmetry:** oracle probes stay at about 95% under misleading prompts, while protagonist probes are brittle: adding the Initial Belief raises accuracy, and misleading statements or random tokens lower it. CAA beats ITI: Llama-2-13B-chat Backward-Belief (Both) 31 → 87; Llama-2-70B about 90% or higher on several tasks.
- **Limits (authors):** One dataset, two families, beliefs only. Early-layer probes overfit, with some below-chance accuracies left uncorrected. **Ours:** The paper does not test whether the oracle and protagonist directions are shared. As in B1, "self" equals the world state.
- **Borrow:** The prompt-variation battery (Initial Belief, misleading, random tokens, time spec) as a format-dependence manipulation at the representation level. CAA steering, which needs no probe training. The permuted-label control.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* effects of different fine-tuning types; robustness benchmarks; desires and intentions; perspective-taking built into the architecture. *Ours:* The robustness asymmetry is a testable prediction for the seed question. If third-person attribution is "reading the transcript" while the self/world readout is closer to a stored state, attributions to others should be *more* order- and format-dependent than self-reports. Program 1 found self-reports already format-dependent on Haiku. Test with matched items through the six-format instrument (API), and at the probe level on a laptop model with the Bortoletto variations.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Partly.** The robustness asymmetry and the CAA recipe transfer. The self/other framing is again self = truth.

## B3. Prakash et al. 2025, "Language Models Use Lookbacks to Track Beliefs"

- **Citation:** Nikhil Prakash, Natalie Shapira, Arnab Sen Sharma, Christoph Riedl, Yonatan Belinkov, Tamar Rott Shaham, David Bau, Atticus Geiger. arXiv:2505.14685 (v3).
- **Read depth:** Partial. First 100k of 168k characters: the main text and the mechanism sections, but not the full appendices with the 405B and Qwen results.
- **What was tested:** Causal mediation, interchange interventions, and patching on CausalToM. These are template stories in which two characters each change the state of an object, with no-visibility and explicit-visibility conditions.
- **On whom:** Llama-3-70B-Instruct (main); Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (appendix). 80 correctly answered examples per analysis.
- **Result:** The "lookback" mechanism: an address is copied alongside a payload, a pointer to it is copied to a later token, and attention dereferences the pointer. Ordering IDs (OIs) tag characters, objects, and states. The *binding lookback* retrieves the state OI for a (character, object) query, and the *answer lookback* retrieves the state token. When one character can see the other, a *visibility lookback* links them through a visibility ID. Layers in Llama-3-70B: source OIs at 20–34; binding at the state token at 33–38; answer pointer at 34–52; answer payload after 56; visibility ID at 10–23; visibility address/pointer alignment at 24–31.
- **Limits (authors):** Simple templates, two characters, and the visibility payload's content is uncertain. **Ours:** The paper does not test whether the model's own knowledge of the world state shares machinery with a character's belief. But the mechanism is *pointer-based retrieval from positions in the transcript*. A character's belief is the state written at the position that character could "see", which is structurally the same as program 1's "repeat consistency is memory" result.
- **Borrow:** CausalToM's counterfactual pair design. The idea of testing self-report as a lookback: when the model reports its "own earlier answer", does attention dereference that transcript position the way the answer lookback does?
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* use the same method on other ToM and reasoning algorithms. *Ours:* (1) Build a first-person CausalToM variant ("You put the milk in the cup; Bob, unseen, swaps it… What do you believe?") alongside a third-person variant. Test on a small model (Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3.1-8B; the effect would first need confirming at that size) whether the same OI/binding/answer-lookback heads carry both. (2) In program 1's planted-answer setting, patch the planted-answer position and check whether uptake depends on a lookback that keys on the *owner tag* (assistant turn vs "another person said").
- **Transfer verdict:** **Partly.** The mechanism is the most concrete account of how other-belief is computed, and it suggests a shared transcript-pointer operation. It has not been demonstrated for self-belief and needs 70B in the paper.

## B4. Wu et al. 2025, "Sensitivity Meets Sparsity: The Impact of Extremely Sparse Parameter Patterns on Theory-of-Mind of LLMs" (journal title: "How large language models encode theory-of-mind: a study on sparse parameter patterns")

- **Citation:** Yuheng Wu, Wentao Guo, Zirui Liu, Heng Ji, Zhaozhuo Xu, Denghui Zhang. arXiv:2504.04238 (v1). Journal version reported by secondary sources as npj Artificial Intelligence 2025; we did not verify that against the journal.
- **Read depth:** Full (100k of 102k characters).
- **What was tested:** A diagonal empirical Fisher score per parameter on ToM data, masked to exclude parameters important for general (C4) language modelling. The top κ ≈ 10⁻⁵ of parameters are perturbed by setting them to the matrix mean.
- **On whom:** Llama3-8B/3.1-8B/3.2-1B/3.2-3B (+Instruct), Qwen2/2.5-7B (+Instruct), two DeepSeek-R1 distills, and Jamba-1.5-Mini (Mamba-based, non-RoPE). Kosinski false-belief tasks (Unexpected Contents, Unexpected Transfer).
- **Result:** ToM accuracy drops: Llama3-8B 61.69 → 49.00, Qwen2.5-7B 66.88 → 43.44, with modest perplexity rises (Llama3-8B 6.14 → 7.46). Jamba did not drop (61.81 → 63.44). Random masks of the same size have "virtually no effect". The sensitive parameters cluster in W_Q/W_K at RoPE dominant-frequency indices. Perturbation shifts attention sinks (q–k_BOS angle 66.46° → 69.22° in Llama3-8B). MMLU subsets also drop, most of all business ethics, logical fallacies, and moral scenarios.
- **Limits:** One small ToM benchmark; one non-RoPE model; inconsistent descriptions of the mask construction. No separation of belief questions from reality questions. **Ours:** The perturbed parameters also hurt a "contextual localization" (repeat-the-text) task. That fits ToM failure as position-tracking failure in the transcript (compare B3), not damage to a dedicated mental-state module.
- **Borrow:** Fisher-masked ablation as a cheap laptop tool. Compute the ToM-sensitive mask from third-person false-belief items, then test whether the same mask damages first-person belief report and reports of the model's own earlier answer.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* no explicit future-work section. *Ours:* A shared-mechanism test on a laptop model: compute Fisher masks separately for (a) third-person false-belief, (b) first-person belief-after-redaction, and (c) recall of own earlier answer. Measure mask overlap (Jaccard) and cross-damage. High overlap with (c) would support "attribution = reading the record".
- **Transfer verdict:** **Partly.** The method transfers cheaply to 1–8B models. It does not address self versus other.

## B5. Jamali, Williams & Cai 2023, "Unveiling Theory of Mind in Large Language Models: A Parallel to Single Neurons in the Human Brain"

- **Citation:** Mohsen Jamali, Ziv M. Williams, Jing Cai. arXiv:2309.01660 (v1, Sept 2023).
- **Read depth:** Full (PDF extracted with pdftotext and read line by line).
- **What was tested:** 76 trials (paired true/false-belief stories adapted from the authors' human dmPFC single-neuron study), each with a "belief" and a "fact" cloze question. Per-dimension Mann-Whitney U tests on hidden embeddings averaged over question tokens (TB vs FB). L2 logistic decoding of trial type from whole layers. Controls: question-only input, and word-shuffled statements.
- **On whom:** Falcon 1b/7b/40b, LLaMA-family 3b–33b fine-tunes, Pythia/Dolly 3b/7b/12b, GPT-2 medium/large/xl.
- **Result:** TB belief accuracy averages 68% (range 56–77), fact 70%, FB 52% (range 26–69; LLaMA-33b best at 69%). Large models (≥12b) beat smaller ones on FB (t = 2.88, p = 0.01). Question-only control: 47%. Shuffled control: 55%. Best layer's share of "responsive" dimensions: Falcon-40b 6.3% (layer 25), LLaMA-30b 5.6%. Large models average 3.9%, small 0.6%; human dmPFC was 23% (49/212). Decoding: large models 75% (Falcon-40b 81%), small 67%, shuffled 55%.
- **Limits (authors):** Open models only; dimensions selective to both trial types were excluded. **Ours:** The per-dimension test uses p < 0.05 with no correction across thousands of dimensions, so about 5% of dimensions should pass by chance. Large-model rates of 3.9–6.3% are not clearly above that floor; the shuffled-control comparison carries the evidential weight. There is no self condition at all. One item ("Ned and you take a photo…") puts "you" in the story, but it is never analysed separately.
- **Borrow:** The question-only and word-shuffle controls are cheap and should be standard in our own probe work. The belief question and fact question asked on the same story give a within-item self/fact versus other contrast.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* test larger, closed models. *Ours:* Rerun with correction for multiple comparisons, and add a first-person version of each story ("you leave the room…"). Then test whether the same dimensions respond to TB/FB for "you" and for "Ned": the single-unit analogue of a shared-mechanism test. Runs on a laptop at 7B.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Partly.** The controls transfer; the main statistic is weak.

## B6. Andreas 2022, "Language Models as Agent Models"

- **Citation:** Jacob Andreas. Findings of EMNLP 2022. arXiv:2212.01681.
- **Read depth:** Full.
- **What was tested:** A position paper with one toy experiment. Claims: (C1) LMs infer and represent the beliefs, desires, and intentions of the agent producing a context, and of agents mentioned in it; (C2) those representations causally shape generation. Toy: an LSTM trained on an "encyclopedia" written by authors with inconsistent belief sets.
- **On whom:** The toy LSTM; otherwise a review of prior evidence (sentiment neuron, Li et al. 2021 entity states, TruthfulQA persona prompting).
- **Result:** Toy: a probe recovers author identity at 98%, and conditioning on an author type yields that author's propositions 89% of the time. Cited: entity-state probes up to 97%; a "truthful author" prompt raises TruthfulQA from about 38% to 58%. Key conceptual point: the LM as a whole holds no consistent belief set. Agent-like states are built incrementally from context and exist only for the agent being simulated.
- **Limits:** Narrow slices of BDI; English only; syntactic explanations not ruled out.
- **Borrow:** The framing. On Andreas's account, the model's "own" belief is just the belief of whichever author or agent the context makes it simulate. Self-attribution and other-attribution are then the same operation with different context, which is exactly the seed hypothesis.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Author:* whether complex beliefs can emerge from text alone; separating belief from syntax; scaling from narrow probes to general agent states. *Ours:* Andreas predicts that an "author" or "assistant" representation and a "character" representation are the same kind of object. A cheap test: train a probe for "the assistant believes p" on assistant-turn contexts and test it zero-shot on "Noor believes p" in narrative contexts, and vice versa.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Transfers** as theory and as a prediction to test. It contains no self-versus-other data.

## B7. Binder et al. 2024/2025, "Looking Inward: Language Models Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection"

- **Citation:** Felix J. Binder, James Chua, Tomek Korbak, Henry Sleight, John Hughes, Robert Long, Ethan Perez, Miles Turpin, Owain Evans. arXiv:2410.13787 (v1 read); ICLR 2025.
- **Read depth:** Full (main text and a second pass over the appendices).
- **What was tested:** M1 is fine-tuned (about 30k samples) to answer hypothetical questions about its own temperature-0 behaviour, such as the second character of its answer or whether it would pick an option in a set. The cross-prediction control is M2 fine-tuned on M1's actual behaviour with the same data. Pairs run in both directions; 6 training and 6 held-out tasks; no CoT. A behaviour-change test fine-tunes M1 on about 1k Claude-3.5-Sonnet outputs and asks whether self-prediction tracks the new behaviour.
- **On whom:** GPT-4o, GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Llama-3.1-70B.
- **Result:** GPT-4o self-prediction rises from 32.6% to 49.4% on held-out tasks. Self versus cross: Llama 70B on itself 48.5% vs GPT-4o predicting Llama 31.8%; GPT-4o on itself 49.4% vs Llama predicting GPT-4o 36.6%. GPT-3.5's advantage is only +0.8%. Cross-prediction plateaus (35.2% after about 10k samples; 36.2% for GPT-4 after about 20k vs 49.6% self). Calibration (MAD): Llama 23.0 → 8.7 after self-training vs 21.3 after cross-training. After behaviour change, the model predicts its new behaviour at 35.4% vs its old at 21.7%. Failures: long-output properties (sentiment of its own story, length) do not beat the most-common baseline. **No self-advantage on sycophancy-type properties:** Llama self 74.5% vs GPT-4o predicting Llama 76.5%. Self-prediction training does not raise overall SAD situational-awareness scores.
- **Limits (authors):** Simple tasks only; mechanism (self-simulation) untested; little out-of-distribution generalisation. **Ours:** The advantage is produced by fine-tuning; Song et al. (B8) argue that model dissimilarity is not fully controlled. The null result on "Are you sure?" sensitivity matters for us: the property closest to program 1's own/other planted-answer effect shows no privileged access.
- **Borrow:** The M1 vs M2-trained-on-M1 cross-prediction design is the template for any "privileged access" claim. The behaviour-change test (does the self-report follow a changed disposition?).
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* test self-simulation in open-weight Llama 70B; introspective CoT; harder properties. *Ours:* (1) A third-person arm: train M1 to predict *a named character's* behaviour in M1-generated stories, against M2. Is the self-advantage specific to the self, or does it hold for any agent M1 simulates? (2) Without fine-tuning, on the API: Haiku predicts its own vs Sonnet's hypothetical answers, against Sonnet predicting both, using program 1's judge × author interaction. That is already built.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Transfers** as a control design. The positive result is contested and does not reach the sycophancy property.

## B8. Song, Hu & Mahowald 2025, "Language Models Fail to Introspect About Their Knowledge of Language"

- **Citation:** Siyuan Song, Jennifer Hu, Kyle Mahowald. arXiv:2503.07513 (v3, COLM 2025 camera-ready).
- **Read depth:** Full.
- **What was tested:** "Direct" preference (log-prob difference between strings) against "Meta" preference (answer-token probability under a metalinguistic prompt). Introspection is defined as a model's Meta responses predicting its *own* Direct probabilities better than another model's, after controlling for similarity: a feature-based category (self / seed variant / base-instruct / same family / other) and an empirical Direct–Direct correlation.
- **On whom:** 21 open models (Llama-3, Qwen-2.5, OLMo-2, Mistral; 1.5B–405B; >70B 4-bit). Exp. 1: grammaticality on 670 BLiMP + 378 LI pairs, with 294 high-disagreement pairs in the main analysis. Exp. 2: word prediction on 4 × 1,000 items (wikipedia, news, nonsense, random sequences).
- **Result:** Direct–Meta consistency is low (Cohen's κ ≈ 0.25; within-model Meta–Direct r never above .25; overall r = .32). The Direct–Direct similarity effect is β̂ = .10, p < .0001. The self category is *not* above the others: "other" and "same family" each β̂ = .05, p < .01 relative to self. OLMo seed variants, which share training data, show no self effect (all p > .25). Same result in >70B models.
- **Limits (authors):** Open models only; larger or closed models might differ. **Ours:** This is the cleanest no-fine-tuning self-versus-other design, and it matches program 1's "no privileged self-knowledge" result. Prompted self-report draws on different knowledge from the one generating the probabilities: "prompted responses should not be conflated with models' linguistic generalizations."
- **Borrow:** The similarity-controlled regression (Meta_i ~ Direct_j + similarity(i, j) + self(i = j)). The seed-variant control (same data, different seed) as the strongest "other" baseline. Restricting to high-disagreement items.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* more controlled studies before attributing metacognition. *Ours:* Apply the same regression to belief attribution. Does model i's verbal account of what a story character believes track model i's *own* internal (probe or logit) reading of that character's belief better than model j's, controlling for similarity? Doable on a laptop with 3–5 open models of different families. If there is no self effect for self-report and none for other-attribution either, the two are processed alike.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Transfers.** Laptop-feasible, open-weight, and its design matches the seed question.

## B9. Song, Lederman, Hu & Mahowald 2025, "Privileged Self-Access Matters for Introspection in AI"

- **Citation:** arXiv:2508.14802 (v1, Aug 2025).
- **Read depth:** Full.
- **What was tested:** A definitional argument: introspection is a process yielding information about internal states more reliably than any third-party process of equal or lower cost. Experiments: temperature self-report (after Comşa & Shanahan) with framing (factual / neutral / crazy) × subject (elephants / unicorns / murlocs); then self-reflection vs within-model prediction (same model, fresh context) vs across-model prediction.
- **On whom:** GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.0-flash, Gemini-2.5-flash; temperatures 0–2.
- **Result:** Reported "HIGH/LOW" follows the framing (crazy → HIGH, factual → LOW), not the actual temperature. In Study 2 (sentences generated at ≤0.5 or ≥1.5 only), all conditions are at the random baseline. "No advantage for self-reflection," and self-reflection is no better than across-model prediction. Exact accuracies are in Fig. 2(b) only.
- **Limits:** Small models and one internal state. The authors do not claim larger or fine-tuned models lack introspection. **Ours:** The three-arm design (self in the same context / self in a fresh context reading the transcript / other model reading the transcript) is the cleanest way to separate "privileged access" from "reading the transcript".
- **Borrow:** The **within-model, fresh-context arm**. This is exactly the comparison program 1's transcript editing can produce: the same model reading its own earlier answer as if written by someone else.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* test larger and fine-tuned models. *Ours:* A three-arm version for belief: (a) the model reports its own belief in context; (b) the same model, fresh context, attributes a belief to "the assistant in this transcript"; (c) it attributes one to "a person who wrote this transcript". If (a) ≈ (b) ≈ (c) in order and format effects, first- and third-person attribution run on the transcript the same way. Cheap on the API.
- **Transfer verdict:** **Transfers.** The three-arm control lifts directly.

## B10. Ashuach, Ein-Dor, Gretz, Katz & Belinkov 2026, "Masked by Consensus: Disentangling Privileged Knowledge in LLM Correctness"

- **Citation:** arXiv:2604.12373 (v2, Apr 2026); ACL 2026 (aclanthology 2026.acl-long.483, per search).
- **Read depth:** Full.
- **What was tested:** Correctness probes on a model's *own* hidden states (self-probe) against probes on a *peer* LLM's or an embedding model's hidden states reading the same question (external probes). Math shows none or a negative premium (GSM1K −0.024 to −0.173). The factual premium emerges from about layers 10–15 (normalised depth 0.25–0.40). Lexical-only inputs recover 54–76% of probe performance.

- **Borrow:** The **disagreement-subset evaluation** and the premium-gap statistic. Both slot into our 2 × 2 judge × author design: restrict scoring to items where judge and author disagree.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Authors:* steer along the correctness direction to test causality; coding and commonsense; hallucination monitoring. *Ours:* (1) Rerun program 1's judge × author interaction on the disagreement subset. - **Borrow:** Eliciting the model's verbal user model and checking it against a probe: the B19 approach applied to an epistemic attribute.
- **Next steps it suggests:** *Ours:* Does the "user seeks validation" representation share a direction with the model's representation of its *own* commitment to an earlier answer (B18)? Both pull toward agreement.

## B22. Baez, Karny & Pataranutaporn 2026, "Dissociating the Internal Representations of Sycophancy in LLMs" (added)

- **Citation:** arXiv:2607.07003 (v3, Aug 2026); ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop.

- **What was tested:** Linear probes and steering vectors for factual-sycophancy and opinion-sycophancy, with cross-subtype transfer and LDA.
