## Verdict

## Novelty line
Binder et al. 2024 (arXiv 2410.13787) report that a finetuned model predicts its own behaviour better than a second model trained on the same facts, while Song, Hu and Mahowald 2025 (arXiv 2503.07513) and Blandfort and Pawar 2026 (NeurIPS 2026 AI4GOOD workshop) find no self-specific advantage by prompting; our prompt-only "you" versus "Sam" contrast on anchoring effects is undetermined, leaning self. That models misreport what moved their answers is in print (Turpin et al. 2023, arXiv 2305.04388; Chen et al. 2025, arXiv 2505.05410), but no paper states that reported anchor influence runs opposite to measured uptake, the same under self and other labels. Verdict: partly new. Search: WebSearch for "LLM self-prediction vs other-model prediction introspection", "self-report anchoring influence LLM", "Nisbett Wilson 1977"; arXiv abstract pages for each lead.
## Papers opened
- Binder, Chua, Korbak, Sleight, Hughes, Long, Perez, Turpin, Evans, 2024, "Looking Inward: Language Models Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection", https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787. States "M1 outperforms M2 in predicting itself" after finetuning on self-prediction, GPT-4, GPT-4o, Llama-3, on simple tasks only. Same kind of comparison as ours but with finetuning and a different model as the "other"; ours labels the same model as "Sam" and is undetermined.
- Song, Hu, Mahowald, 2025, "Language Models Fail to Introspect About Their Knowledge of Language", https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07513. 21 open models; "we do not find evidence that LLMs have privileged self-access"; another model with near-identical knowledge predicts as well. Opposite direction to Binder; ours sits between (point estimate favours self, interval includes 0).
- Blandfort, Pawar, 2026, "Strangers to Themselves: What Language Models Say About Themselves Is Generic", https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165307 (workshop listing, abstract only). Prompted self-prediction r = +0.04, +0.24 with the items shown, a generic "capable AI agents" framing +0.28; "other models' answers about themselves predict the target model at least as well as its own". Bears on the self-versus-other arm; no causal self-report test.
- Lindsey, 2026, "Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models", https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01828. Concept injection; introspection "highly unreliable and context-dependent". Does not bear on self-versus-other prediction or on anchoring reports.
- Turpin, Michael, Perez, Bowman, 2023, "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think", https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388. Models "systematically fail to mention in their explanations" the biasing features that moved their answers. Same kind as our causal-report result; different task (CoT explanations, not a quantitative influence report).
- Chen et al., 2025, "Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think", https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410. CoTs reveal hint use "often below 20%" of the time. Same kind; not an anchoring uptake comparison.
- Abdi, 2026, "The Self-Report Inversion" (self-hosted empirical page, no arXiv id, not peer reviewed), https://abdul-abdi.github.io/self-report-inversion/. Claude Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 4.6, planted numeric anchors; with "could bias" wording the flag "is detached from the influence"; with "actually changed" wording 29.2% versus 5.8% flagging on anchor-effective versus other pairs, 8 of 60 pairs effective. Closest to our causal-report result but does not state an anticorrelation with measured uptake and is not a publication.
- Nisbett, Wilson, 1977, "Telling more than we can know: Verbal reports on mental processes", Psychological Review 84(3), 231-259, https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/92167. Existence and citation verified from the repository listing; text not opened (see caveats).
## Not found / caveats
- Nisbett and Wilson 1977: both PDF copies found (Notre Dame, Princeton) are scanned images that could not be read, and the Michigan record returned 403; the citation is verified, the content is not quoted here.
- No 2025 or 2026 paper was found that compares a model predicting itself under a "you" label against the same model under a named-other-AI label without finetuning, nor one that compares a model's quantitative report of anchor influence with the measured anchoring effect across distances and sources.
- The "Masked by Consensus" (arXiv 2604.12373) and "Know Thyself?" (arXiv 2510.03399) papers surfaced in search were not opened; the search summaries describe them as finding no self-advantage on correctness probes and near-random self-recognition.
- Blandfort and Pawar is a workshop poster listing read from the abstract only.
