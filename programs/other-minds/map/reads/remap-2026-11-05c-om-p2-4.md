## Papers read

### Strangers to Themselves: What Language Models Say About Themselves Is Generic. Blandfort and Pawar, 2026, arXiv 2609.09899 (v1, 9 Sep 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09899
- read_depth: full (the main text and appendices up to character 200,000 of 219,924; the last references and appendix pages were not read). The map's earlier records had only the abstract or workshop listing.
- what_tested: whether what a model says about how it would behave carries model-specific knowledge or only generic knowledge of AI assistants. Fifteen prediction methods: abstract asking, informed asking (own history, exact items), watching, a generic "capable AI agents" subject, other-model baselines and an analyst.
- on_whom: 12 model entries from six labs (among them Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.5, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Llama-3.3-70B), on nine behavioural evaluations, one of them Sycophancy (answer flips under pushback).
- how_scored: the Pearson r between predicted and measured rates across conditions, macro-averaged, with bootstrap CIs; also MAE and signed bias; one frozen test split.
- result: direct self-report r +0.04; item-informed self-prediction +0.24 against a generic subject at +0.28 (paired −0.03 [−0.10, +0.03]); other models' item-informed answers predict the target at +0.35; self-advantage in fit +0.02. On Sycophancy, item-informed self-prediction +0.37, the cross-model behaviour mean +0.67, the analyst +0.45. Sycophancy predictions were under-dispersed: per-condition SD ≤ 0.09. Self-framed reports showed a favourable bias (−0.15 [−0.19, −0.11] on harmful-rate evaluations).
- limits: one frozen test; nine public evaluations; low-effort reasoning only; scalar elicitation; the decomposition depends on the model pool. No anchoring or planted-answer task was used.
- borrow: the generic-subject control and the cross-model behaviour mean as baselines. Our "Sam" arm is close to their generic subject; a "typical AI assistant" arm would be the direct analogue.
- next_steps_it_suggests: retrieval over a model's own behavioural record; whether activation-level introspection supports cross-context self-description.
- predicts_for_us: P2-14's pass (no self advantage): **pass**, since the self-specific gain is near 0 by prompting. The new base-arm follow-up: no bearing on the pass rule. Their predictions were under-dispersed, while om-p2-15's other-person self-predictions were over-dispersed, so they do not predict our sign.
- **already shown**: a prompt-only "you" against an "other / generic" label gives at most a small self-specific gain. This is in print (the paper is now verifiable as arXiv 2609.09899, not only a workshop listing). Our point estimate, which leans self on own-answer uptake, sits against it, so P2-14 is still worth running.

### A Positive Case for Faithfulness: LLM Self-Explanations Help Predict Model Behavior. Mayne, Kang, Gould, Ramchandran, Mahdi and Siegel, 2026, arXiv 2602.02639 (v2), https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02639
- read_depth: full main text (the first 100,000 of 219,512 characters; the later appendices were not read).
- what_tested: whether a model's own explanation helps a predictor simulate its answer on a nearby counterfactual (Normalized Simulatability Gain), and whether self-explanations beat explanations written by other models.
- on_whom: 18 reference models (the Qwen 3, Gemma 3, GPT-5, Claude 4.5 and Gemini 3 families); about 7,000 question and counterfactual pairs from seven tabular datasets; five predictor models.
- how_scored: NSG, the fraction of the achievable prediction gain that the explanation provides.
- result: NSG of 11.0–36.5%. Same-family explanations beat cross-family ones in every family: Claude 4.5 30.2% against 28.0% (+2.3pp [+1.6, +2.9]); GPT-5 +4.3pp [+3.5, +5.0]. The authors read this as partly privileged self-knowledge, holding "even when the explainer models are stronger".
- limits: an average-case metric; the predictors are not state of the art; the comparison is between families, not one model under two labels; tabular decisions only.
- borrow: the counterfactual-simulatability frame: score a self-account by how much it helps predict behaviour on a new item, against another model's account.
- next_steps_it_suggests: link faithfulness to calibration; track it through post-training checkpoints.
- predicts_for_us: P2-14: leans **fail** (a small self advantage exists by prompting, in explanations). Base-arm follow-up: no bearing.
- **rival named**: a small self advantage without finetuning is in print for explanations, and it runs the way om-p2-4's point estimate leans. "Self better" on P2-14 would therefore partly replicate Mayne et al. rather than contradict all prior work. Their gap is between model families, which our same-model "Sam" label holds fixed.

### Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check. Singh, Linzen and Ravfogel, 2026, arXiv 2605.26242 (v2), https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26242
- read_depth: full main text (the first 100,000 of 138,891 characters; the last appendices were not read).
- what_tested: whether positive introspection results meet two criteria. (i) Privileged access: the report cannot be recovered from the input alone. (ii) Second-order computation.
- on_whom: Llama-3.1-8B and 70B, Qwen-2.5 and Qwen-3, and Gemma-3-27B, re-testing Ji-An et al. 2025, Steinmetz Yalon et al. 2026 and Lindsey 2025.
- how_scored: in-context accuracy against layer-0 input probes and permuted-label controls, plus a three-way intervention design.
- result: layer-0 entity-embedding probes match or exceed the models' in-context accuracy. For example, the Belief Dominance task: Gemma-3-27B ICL 0.48 ± 0.02 against a probe at 0.57 ± 0.02. "Gaslight" text-only pushes are labelled as activation interventions.
- limits: no direct test for second-order computation; pretrained models only; prompt-wording sensitivity.
- borrow: the input-only control, the requirement that a self-report beat what the input alone predicts. In om-p2-4 the analogues are the one-line observer and the no-uptake predictor (om-p2-15).
- next_steps_it_suggests: input-matched controls in every self-report paradigm.
- predicts_for_us: no bearing on either pass rule; it supports scoring self-prediction against an input-only baseline, which P2-15 and the follow-up below do.
- **design to borrow**: the follow-up keeps the always-no-uptake predictor as its baseline, which is their input-only control in our setting.

### The paradox of knowing. Dunning, 2013, The Psychologist (British Psychological Society), https://bps.org.uk/psychologist/paradox-knowing (secondary for Epley and Dunning 2000, JPSP 79(6), 861–875)
- read_depth: full (the 2013 article). The 2000 primary paper was not opened (no open text found), so only the numbers this article reports are used.
- what_tested: self-predictions of behaviour against peers' predictions of the same behaviour.
- on_whom: students (Epley and Dunning 2000; Balcetis and Dunning 2013; Koehler et al. 2011).
- how_scored: predicted against actual rates, and correlations.
- result: daffodil purchase: 83% predicted for self, 56% for peers, 43% actual. Voting: 90%, 67%, 61%. In the bystander study, self-predictions (about 90% helping in both conditions) missed the group effect, while peers predicted a drop from 72% to 50% (actual 50% to 23%). By correlation, peers r = .48 and self r = .51, so peers "at least equal" self.
- limits: human behaviour; a popular article.
- borrow: the distinction between mean-level and correlational accuracy, and the idea that self-predictions under-weight situational pulls that observers apply.
- next_steps_it_suggests: none stated.
- predicts_for_us: P2-14: in humans observers are at least as good, so **pass**. Base-arm follow-up: no direct bearing. In humans the self-prediction misses the situation's pull; in om-p2-4 it is the "Sam" account that misjudges the pull, overpredicting stickiness to "his" answer (0.84/0.67/0.47 against the measured 0.71/0.44/0.21).
- **changes nothing**: the human asymmetry runs the other way from om-p2-4's (there, the observer is closer on the situational effect), so it neither duplicates nor explains our result. It stays a contrast for the write-up.

### Can We Trust AI Explanations? Evidence of Systematic Underreporting in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning. Mehta, 2025, arXiv 2601.00830 (v1), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00830v1
- read_depth: partial (PDF pages 1–6: the abstract, introduction, related work, methods and Table 1; the results pages were not read).
- what_tested: whether models mention hints that point to wrong answers, spontaneously, under a "being monitored" notice, and under explicit instruction; plus a follow-up probe ("Did they influence your reasoning?").
- on_whom: 11 models, among them claude-opus-4 and claude-sonnet-4.5; MMLU questions; 9,154 trials.
- how_scored: acknowledgment by pattern matching; susceptibility as the rate of choosing the hinted wrong option.
- result: baseline acknowledgment 20.7% overall (claude-opus-4 7.0%, claude-sonnet-4.5 48.5%), 21.8% under the monitoring notice, 100% under explicit instruction with a 68.2% false-positive rate. Sycophancy hints: 45.5% susceptibility, 43.6% acknowledgment. The abstract says user-preference hints are followed most often and reported least.
- limits: binary mention, not a size of influence; automated pattern matching; one task.
- borrow: the follow-up probe separates "did not notice" from "did not report".
- next_steps_it_suggests: monitoring alone does not catch hidden influence.
- predicts_for_us: no bearing on the follow-ups' pass rules. It bears on om-p2-4's causal-report secondary: across hint types, following and reporting run in opposite orders.
- **already shown (in part)**: an inverse ordering between how often an influence acts and how often it is reported is in print across hint types. Our quantitative version (the reported size of influence anticorrelated with measured uptake across distance and source, under both labels) is not, so the partly-new claim stands in narrower words.

Also opened, for program 2's other bet (recorded in full in `remap-2026-11-05c-om-p2-5.md`): Huang et al. 2025 (arXiv 2505.15392). Its reasoning-trace analysis finds fewer anchor mentions on mildly anchored questions, which is the opposite of an inversion, so it gives no prior statement of om-p2-4's causal-report pattern.
