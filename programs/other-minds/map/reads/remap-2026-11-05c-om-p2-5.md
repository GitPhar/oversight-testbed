## Papers read

### Understanding the Anchoring Effect of LLM with Synthetic Data: Existence, Mechanism, and Potential Mitigations. Huang, Bie, Na, Ruan, Lei, Yue and He, 2025, arXiv 2505.15392 (v2, March 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15392
- read_depth: full (100,000 of 103,349 characters; only the last references were not read).
- what_tested: whether LLMs anchor on a stated comparison value (semantic priming) or an irrelevant number (numerical priming); a mechanism by causal tracing; mitigations.
- on_whom: 11 models from Qwen2.5-0.5B to GPT-4o, Qwen3-235B (thinking) and DeepSeek-R1; SynAnchors, 60 semantic and 40 numerical questions.
- how_scored: an Anchor Index above 0.4 or a relative error above 0.2, with t-tests over 100 samples per anchor.
- result: 22% to 61% of questions anchored. GPT-4o 27.3%, Qwen3-235B (thinking) 22.0%, DeepSeek-R1 25.0%, against 34.7%–60.5% for the small open models. Larger and reasoning models were less affected. No mitigation removed the effect. High and low anchors were not analysed separately (absolute indices).
- limits: 100 questions; mechanism on one 8B model; no Claude model; no own-answer or attributed-rating condition.
- borrow: nothing for the design. Its scale trend is a rival for om-p2-5r.
- next_steps_it_suggests: a larger dataset; deeper mechanisms.
- predicts_for_us: the anchors follow-up below: leans **fail**, since stronger models take up any planted number less, so on Sonnet the uptakes, and the gaps between them, may stay too small to clear 0. P2-17: no bearing.
- **rival named**: om-p2-5r's null may be a property of stronger models (smaller uptake of any number) rather than of foreign anchors. On Opus the own re-rating's upward uptake was not detectably above 0 (+0.045). The anchors follow-up keeps uptake reported per model, so this rival can be read off its result.

### Anchoring Bias in Large Language Models: An Experimental Study. Lou and Sun, 2024, arXiv 2412.06593 (v2), https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06593
- read_depth: full.
- what_tested: whether low and high anchor hints (fact or "expert" anchors) shift LLM forecasts, and whether CoT, principles, "ignore the hint", reflection or both-anchors mitigation help.
- on_whom: GPT-4o, GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 Turbo; 62 questions from the "Play the Future" human study; 30 runs per condition at temperature 0.8.
- how_scored: whether the shift between the high- and low-anchor answers had the sign of the anchor difference (t-tests); an anchoring index.
- result: 11 of 162 differences had the wrong sign, 9 of them from GPT-3.5, so the stronger models were more consistently anchored. Expert anchors had no sign exceptions on 12 questions. GPT-4's anchoring index was about 0.45, against 0.61 in the human study. Only showing both anchors brought answers near the no-anchor benchmark, and only when the two anchors bracketed it.
- limits: OpenAI models only; 62 questions; no upward-against-downward split.
- borrow: the both-anchors finding. In om-p2-5's OWN cells the transcript holds two numbers, the planted own answer V and Alex's m. The self re-rating ("give your rating again", asked right after Alex's m) may be pulled back toward m by bracketing, while Sam's rating is not asked as a re-rating.
- next_steps_it_suggests: more anchor types; mitigation by multiple perspectives.
- predicts_for_us: the anchors follow-up: no clear bearing on its sign. It runs against Huang et al. on scale (stronger models anchored more consistently in direction). P2-17 (wording): bracketing gives a mechanism for "own resists more than Sam", so it is consistent with P2-17 passing.
- **rival named**: bracketing by Alex's number, acting on the re-rating more than on the third-person question. P2-17's recall and reworded self question test this already; nothing new to add.

### Noise Increases Anchoring Effects. Lee and Morewedge, 2021, Psychological Science (DOI 10.1177/09567976211024254), https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2021-lee.pdf
- read_depth: partial (PDF pages 1–6 and 11–14 of 16: the framework, pretests, Experiments 1a–1b, 5, 6a–6b, the general discussion opening; Experiments 2–4 not read).
- what_tested: whether anchoring effects grow with judgmental noise (the width of the range of plausible values) while anchoring bias stays the same.
- on_whom: 11 preregistered experiments, N = 3,552 MTurk adults; hotel prices, dog weights, calories, dot counts.
- how_scored: anchoring effect as the difference in point estimates across anchor conditions; noise as range width; bias as a skew index.
- result: anchoring effects were larger for larger-magnitude, noisier stimuli. In Experiment 1a, high anchor against no anchor on the four-star hotel: mean difference CI [93.19, 172.45], d = 0.93; low anchor against no anchor: [25.04, 76.30], d = 0.55. In Experiments 6a and 6b the skew index (bias) did not differ by magnitude (Fs ≤ 1.11). Noise predicted SD linearly (β1 = 0.44, R² = .87). An irrelevant anchor did not grow with magnitude (Experiment 5).
- limits: human point estimates of quantities; no bounded probability scale; no judgment about another person.
- borrow: elicit the plausible range (minimum and maximum) as a noise measure. A self range and a Sam range on the REF cells would test whether Sam's rating is the noisier judgment, as this account needs for Sam to take up more.
- next_steps_it_suggests: use psychophysical noise to separate bias from noise in judgment.
- predicts_for_us: a vaguer judgment (what a stranger would say) takes up a relevant anchor more. That predicts Sam ≥ self. It also predicts that Sam takes up Alex's relevant number. Haiku did not (0.02), but Sonnet and Opus did (+0.21 and +0.16, about as much as their own numbers). For the anchors follow-up: **mixed** (it predicts a Sam-over-self gap, but also predicts Alex's number pulls Sam about as much, which would fail the dU conjunct).
- **rival named**: a noise account of "Sam moves more" that needs no egocentric leakage. It fits Sonnet and Opus (Sam followed Alex as much as its own number) better than Haiku. A range-width arm is a cheap design to borrow (about $0.02 on Haiku: 32 statements, 2 questions, 5 repeats), as a secondary of the follow-up or of P2-17.

### Same Answer, Different Confidence: Protocol Sensitivity in Measuring LLM Confidence. Kim and Kang, 2026, arXiv 2605.27752 (v4, 23 Oct 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27752
- read_depth: full main text (the first 100,000 of 150,432 characters, which hold the main text and early appendices; the later appendices were not read). The map's P2-17 record had the abstract only.
- what_tested: how measurement choices change the comparison of verbalized confidence with token confidence. It is **not** a test of how the wording of the elicitation question shifts stated confidence: its prompt ablation varies the prompt that reads token scores.
- on_whom: Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3.5-9B and Ministral-3-8B, with larger checks; four QA datasets; no Claude model.
- how_scored: ECE, AUROC and AURC, with paired bootstrap intervals.
- result: a supplied canonical reference gets +.114 more verbalized confidence than an alias [.105, .123]. On questions the model got wrong, its own wrong answer gets +.044 more confidence than another model's wrong answer [.037, .050]. Re-supplying the model's own answer changes its confidence by at most .022.
- limits: open 7–9B models; short-answer QA; greedy decoding; one verbalized prompt plus a 0–100 variant; no multi-turn dialogue.
- borrow: nothing for the design.
- next_steps_it_suggests: report protocol choices with any confidence comparison.
- predicts_for_us: P2-17: **no bearing**. The map's P2-17 Prior work line says this paper shows "instruction wording drives stated-confidence shifts, which favours the wording rival", and its text does not support that. The anchors follow-up: no bearing.
- **changes nothing** for the follow-ups, but it **corrects P2-17's Prior work line**, which should drop the Kim and Kang clause or restate it as protocol sensitivity of verbalized against token confidence. The small own-answer preference (+.044) is a weak analogue of program 1's own-answer pull, not of om-p2-5's Sam-over-self gap.

## What the re-scout changes

Adds one candidate: the om-p2-5r audit's anchors test, om-p2-5r's design on Sonnet with planted values built from Sonnet's own base ratings (om-p2-6r's archive, $0), plus a reduced Opus arm, about $1.80. Names two rivals the map lacked: stronger models take up any number less (Huang et al. 2025), and a vaguer judgment of a stranger takes up any relevant number more (Lee and Morewedge 2021). A range-width secondary (about $0.02) tests the second. Kim and Kang 2026 do not support P2-17's Prior work line; that clause should go. P2-17 otherwise unchanged.

**Novelty:** partly new. The manipulation is in print: Choi, Hong and Kim (2024, arXiv 2407.12007; NAACL Findings 2025) gave GPT-4, Claude 3 Opus, Llama 2 70B and Mixtral one option as the model's own choice, and its estimate of how many peers would choose it shifted (a false-consensus effect). They did not compare that pull with the pull on the model's own re-rating, and showed no third party's number. On Haiku, ours found the rating attributed to another mind moved more than the model's own re-rating (0.50 against 0.36 of the way; a third party's number 0.02), from upward plants only. This did not replicate on Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 with Haiku's anchors, where "Sam" followed the third party's number about as much as the model's own (om-p2-5r). So the partly-new part is a one-model result, and a noise account of anchoring (Lee and Morewedge 2021, Psychological Science) is a rival in print.
