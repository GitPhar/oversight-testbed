# Re-scout around sv-b2 (declining under a stated penalty), for the candidate follow-up SV-2w

Written 2026-11-05 (America/Denver) by a fresh subagent (claude-opus-5-5) that ran none of program 7's bets, pre-registrations or audits. Web reads only (WebSearch, WebFetch; PDFs that WebFetch could not parse were saved and converted with `pdftotext` in the session scratchpad). No model API was called and no local model was loaded. Format: `operators/remap.md` Step 1b and `operators/scout.md` Step 2.

**The result being re-scouted (sv-b2, fail after audit).** In a fresh conversation the model answered yes, no or pass under "+1 right, -3 wrong, 0 pass"; in another fresh conversation it gave a 0-100 rating. The rating tracked declining (Sonnet AUROC 0.82, Haiku 0.69) but did not beat the one-bit "is it SimpleQA trivia?" guess (0.78, 0.77) by 0.10. The audit found the two are largely separate signals: within SimpleQA alone the rating separated declines from answers at 0.69 (Haiku) and 0.83 (Sonnet).

**The candidate follow-up (SV-2w).** On fresh SimpleQA-style items only (one question type, so the trivia bit is constant), does the separately stated number predict declining under the same stated penalty better than the remaining simple guesses (constant, rating side, question length) by 0.10 AUROC, on Haiku and Sonnet (about $0.55)?

**Already in the program's reads and not re-read except as noted:** Koriat & Goldsmith 1996 (re-read now, in full, for the within-item-set control analysis; it was abstract-only before), Kumaran et al. 2026 arXiv 2603.22161, RiskEval, I-CALM, AbstentionBench, Hampton 2001, Kiani & Shadlen 2009, Feng et al. 2024, Wen et al. 2024 survey.

## Read records

### Reported confidence tracks commitment more than correctness — Dharshan Kumaran, 2026, arXiv preprint, arXiv 2606.29490 (verified: v1 28 Jun 2026, single author, only version)
- read_depth: full (arXiv HTML v1, all 226,630 characters in three passes: main text, supplementary Tables 3-17)
- what_tested: whether verbal confidence tracks the model's later commit-or-abstain decision more than it tracks whether the answer is right. Phase 1 answer (no mention of abstaining); Phase 1b a separate call that sees the question and its own answer and gives verbal confidence (10-class labels, or an integer 0-100 for non-reasoning models); Phase 2 a separate call that sees the question and its answer and chooses commit or abstain. No stated payoff; the standard prompt is cautionary ("better to be safe than sorry"), with a neutral variant.
- on_whom: non-reasoning Gemma 3 27B, Gemma 4 31B, GPT-4o, Qwen3 235B (no-think); reasoning Gemini Flash 3 preview, Kimi K2 Think, GPT-oss-120B, Qwen3 235B (think). SimpleQA as 4-option multiple choice (N = 2,000 non-reasoning, 1,000 reasoning), MMLU-Pro STEM (500), SuperGPQA-hard (500), HLE (500). No Claude.
- how_scored: abstention AUROC and correctness AUROC of each confidence signal within each model-dataset cell; their difference (the decision-truth gap); residualisation on calibrated log-probabilities (Cal-LP) and on an item-difficulty score (share correct over 20 GPT-4o runs at temperature 0.8).
- result: within SimpleQA alone, separately elicited confidence predicted abstaining at AUROC 0.79 / 0.78 (Gemma 3, class / numeric), 0.80 / 0.60 (Gemma 4), 0.71 / 0.84 (GPT-4o), 0.65 / 0.88 (Qwen3), against correctness AUROCs of 0.51-0.62. SimpleQA abstention rates 0.681, 0.153, 0.722 and 0.090. Pooled, the class-confidence gap averaged +0.16 over 46 cells (t = 11.6). After residualising on Cal-LP, adding item difficulty changed the preserved share of abstention AUROC by under 2 points in every SimpleQA cell but one (Gemma 4 numeric, 12.1% to 6.8%). No question-length, topic or domain predictor was reported. Quote: "verbal confidence is better understood as a behaviour-facing readout of an internal commit-readiness state".
- limits: multiple choice; no stated penalty; the abstain prompt frames safety; no Claude; no simple-guess baselines beyond the difficulty score; mechanistic work on Gemma only; some cells underpowered (4-14 trials in key quadrants).
- borrow: (1) the within-benchmark, separate-call design is SV-2w's design, so its AUROC range is the closest published reference for SV-2w's "said" side. (2) An item-difficulty covariate (share correct across repeated runs) as an extra rival: residualise the stated number on it before scoring, in SV-2w's secondary analyses. (3) Report the gap between abstention AUROC and correctness AUROC as a secondary line: it separates "the number drives the action" from "both follow correctness".
- next_steps_it_suggests: author: find the cues behind post-answer confidence; anchor the commit-readiness signal more tightly to correctness. For us: the same within-SimpleQA test on Claude, with an announced penalty and the program's simple guesses.
- predicts_for_us: SV-2w: pass in direction (within SimpleQA the separate number predicted abstaining at 0.60-0.88 on four non-Claude models, and an item-difficulty control removed almost nothing); whether that clears a 0.10 margin over question length is not tested.
- remap head: **already shown** (on four non-Claude models, without a penalty): within one question type, separately elicited verbal confidence predicts abstaining. SV-2w must add Claude, the stated penalty and the simple-guess margin, or it is a replication.

### Two axes of LLM abstention — Benedikt J. Wagner, 2026, arXiv preprint, arXiv 2607.08456 (verified: v1 9 Jul 2026, only version)
- read_depth: full (arXiv HTML v1)
- what_tested: whether one answer-confidence score can cover both reasons to withhold: an answer that would be wrong (correctness) and a question that should not be answered (unanswerable or false premise).
- on_whom: Gemma 2 2B-it, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B; SelfAware (150 answerable + 150 unanswerable per model; 120 each for Qwen 3B), CREPE (500 normal + 500 false-premise). Analyses within each dataset.
- how_scored: AUROC of output readouts (mean token log-probability, first-token entropy), P(IK), P(True), a premise check, hidden-state probes; a bag-of-words surface baseline; certified two-threshold answering policies.
- result: on SelfAware, output readouts separated correct from wrong at 0.64-0.84 but answerable from unanswerable at only 0.54-0.67, while hidden probes reached 0.97-0.99 for answerability. **A bag-of-words baseline on the question text reached 0.87 for answerability.** On CREPE, raw answer-confidence gave 0.49-0.58 and the bag-of-words baseline 0.59. A factorised policy reached 0.75 coverage at 8B against 0.31 for confidence alone. Quote: "the answerability axis barely separates C from W".
- limits: small open models; SelfAware answerability is largely recoverable from surface text (the author says so); no verbal 0-100 rating; no penalty; no Claude.
- borrow: a **surface-text baseline** (bag of words on the question) as a stronger simple guess than question length. Where it goes: a secondary rival in SV-2w, fitted on held-out items, so that "the number beats question length" cannot be won only because length is a weak summary of the question.
- next_steps_it_suggests: author: causal tests on the answerability direction; larger models. For us: whether part of what a Claude model withholds on is a property of the question's wording, which a cheap text model can read without the rating.
- predicts_for_us: SV-2w: no direct bearing on the pass rule (no verbal rating), but it warns that a text-only guess can be far stronger than length, so a pass against length alone would leave this rival open.
- remap head: **rival named**: declining follows question-surface features, which question length captures only crudely. The cheapest test that excludes it is a bag-of-words guess fitted on other items, scored as a secondary rival in SV-2w at no extra API cost.

### SimpleQA (measuring short-form factuality) — Wei et al., 2024, arXiv preprint, arXiv 2411.04368
- read_depth: full (arXiv HTML)
- what_tested: a benchmark of short, single-answer, adversarially collected fact questions; whether models answer, decline ("not attempted") or err; calibration of stated confidence and of answer frequency.
- on_whom: Claude-3-haiku, Claude-3-sonnet, Claude-3-opus, Claude-3.5-sonnet, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, o1-mini, o1-preview; 4,326 questions.
- how_scored: correct, not attempted, incorrect (graded by a prompted model); stated 0-100% confidence binned into 15 intervals against accuracy; frequency over 100 samples.
- result: "not attempted" was 75.3% (Claude-3-haiku), 75.0% (Claude-3-sonnet), 39.6% (Claude-3-opus) and 35.0% (Claude-3.5-sonnet), against 0.9-1.0% for GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini. Stated confidence correlates positively with accuracy but sits below the diagonal: "models consistently overstate their confidence". Topics (classified post hoc): science and technology 19.8%, politics 16.4%, art 12.7%, other 11.0%, geography 9.8%, sports 8.5%, music 7.9%, TV shows 6.8%, history 4.0%, video games 3.1%; answer types: dates 32.8%, person 24.1%, number 15.3%, place 9.9%. No per-topic results; no analysis of whether confidence predicts declining.
- limits: older Claude models; declining is unprompted (no penalty); confidence asked in the same response as the answer.
- borrow: (1) the topic and answer-type labels as **within-SimpleQA item-property guesses**: a one-bit or few-category guess built from them is the within-type analogue of sv-b2's trivia bit. (2) Fresh SimpleQA items for SV-2w should be drawn across topics in these shares, so a topic effect cannot hide in the sample.
- next_steps_it_suggests: authors: whether short-form factuality relates to long-form factuality. For us: whether, inside SimpleQA, the topic or answer type predicts a Claude model's declining as well as its number does.
- predicts_for_us: SV-2w: no prediction for the margin, but Claude models decline often on SimpleQA, so enough declines to test are likely (a design fact, not an outcome).
- remap head: **design to borrow** (topic and answer-type labels as within-type rivals; Claude declines heavily on this item type).

### Metacognition in animals: how do we know that they know? — Jozefowiez, Staddon & Cerutti, 2009, Comparative Cognition & Behavior Reviews 4:29-39, doi:10.3819/ccbr.2009.40003
- read_depth: full (journal PDF, main text; references skimmed)
- what_tested: whether the two usual criteria for animal metacognition (the uncertain response rises with difficulty; free-choice trials are more accurate than forced ones) can be met by a model with no metacognition: a behavioural economic model (BEM) that picks the response with the higher expected payoff given a noisy percept, with risk aversion.
- on_whom: simulations fitted to rat data (Foote & Crystal 2007) and compared with rhesus monkey (Hampton 2001) and pigeon (Sutton & Shettleworth 2008; Inman & Shettleworth 1999) delayed-matching data.
- how_scored: least-squares fits of decline rates and of forced versus free-choice accuracy (fitted noise 0.38 and risk exponent 0.46 for Foote & Crystal).
- result: BEM meets both criteria in categorisation tasks. With the percept alone it fails delayed matching (its decline curve is non-monotonic in delay), and it succeeds once the decision can also use the retention interval itself. Pigeons decline more at long delays without any accuracy gain on free-choice trials, which the authors explain by the retention interval, an observable property of the trial that predicts low payoff, controlling the choice. One of Hampton's monkeys showed the same pattern. The criteria are met only when behaviour is controlled by a dimension correlated with the subject's own chance of success; if a more salient dimension overshadows it, they fail. Quote: "a necessarily ill-defined concept".
- limits: a modelling paper with no new data; delays treated as noiseless in the simplified version; fits are partial (BEM underestimates declines at the range ends).
- borrow: the **accuracy-gain check**: declining driven by an item property gives no accuracy gain beyond that property, while declining driven by the item-level confidence does. For SV-2w: accuracy on answered items, compared with what the item property alone predicts, as a secondary statistic.
- next_steps_it_suggests: authors: develop and test explicit models of the behaviour rather than refine criteria. For us: write the rival as a model (decline = f(item property)) and ask whether adding the stated number improves it, which is SV-2w's comparison stated as nested models.
- predicts_for_us: SV-2w: no outcome prediction; it predicts that sv-b2's result (an item property predicting declines as well as the number) is the expected outcome whenever such a property exists, so holding the property constant is the right next test.
- remap head: **rival named**: the "uncertain" choice follows an observable trial property correlated with payoff (pigeons: delay; sv-b2: question type), not the subject's confidence. SV-2w's within-type design is the cheapest test that excludes the one property sv-b2 found; topic or surface features remain (see Wagner; Wei).

### Monitoring and control in the strategic regulation of memory accuracy — Koriat & Goldsmith, 1996, Psychological Review 103:490-517, doi:10.1037/0033-295X.103.3.490 (already in the program's reads at abstract depth; re-read now for the control analysis)
- read_depth: partial (authors' PDF from newiipdm.haifa.ac.il: Experiment 1 method and control analyses, Experiment 2 method and results; Experiment 3 and the general discussion skimmed)
- what_tested: whether people's decision to volunteer or withhold an answer follows their own confidence, across items within one item set, under a stated penalty, and whether it still does when confidence is a poor guide (deceptive items).
- on_whom: Experiment 1: 71 University of Haifa undergraduates, a 60-item general-knowledge test (recall or recognition); Phase 1 forced report with a probability rating per answer, Phase 2 free report with payment +NIS 1 right and -1 (moderate) or -10 (high) wrong. Experiment 2: a 90-item test with standard and deceptive subsets, 30 recall participants.
- how_scored: Goodman-Kruskal gamma and ANDI between Phase-1 confidence and Phase-2 volunteering, per participant; estimated report criterion.
- result: volunteered items had mean assessed probability .89 against .29 for withheld items. Confidence-volunteering gamma averaged .97 for recall (range .89-1.0) and .93 for recognition (.68-1.0), higher than confidence-correctness gamma (.87 and .68). The high penalty raised the criterion (mean confidence of volunteered items .93 against .84; 26.9 against 30.9 answers volunteered). On deceptive items, where confidence barely tracked correctness (gamma .26), volunteering still followed confidence (gamma .88; .95 on standard items). Quote: "put more faith in their subjective confidence than was actually warranted".
- limits: confidence was rated in Phase 1 on the same items the same session, so the volunteering decision can draw on memory of the rating; human memory tests, not language models; no item-property baseline (the deceptive split is an item property, but withholding was not predicted from it).
- borrow: (1) the deceptive-item split: a within-type subset where the number is a poor guide to correctness but may still drive withholding, which separates "number drives declining" from "both follow correctness". (2) Gamma per participant is the human analogue of SV-2w's per-model AUROC.
- next_steps_it_suggests: authors: study other means of regulating reports (grain size). For us: SV-2w on Claude is the item-level confidence-to-withholding test on one item set, with the rating in a separate conversation (stricter than Phase 1 to Phase 2 in one session).
- predicts_for_us: SV-2w: pass in direction (in people, within one item set, confidence predicts withholding under a stated penalty almost perfectly, more tightly than it predicts being right).
- remap head: **already shown** (in humans): within one item set, stated confidence drives withholding under a penalty. It sets the human reference; it does not answer whether a Claude model's separately stated number does.

### An IRT-based model for omitted and not-reached items — Jinxin Guo, 2019, arXiv preprint, arXiv 1904.03767 (verified: v1 7 Apr 2019, only version)
- read_depth: partial (PDF via pdftotext: §2 model, §5 PISA analysis, §6 discussion; simulation sections skimmed)
- what_tested: a joint model of answers and omissions in which the probability of omitting item j by person i depends on a person missingness trait τ_i, an item missingness parameter ζ_j, earlier omissions and the response, with τ correlated with ability θ.
- on_whom: simulations; 2015 PISA computer-based science subtest, Dominican Republic, 493 students, 17 items.
- how_scored: DIC and LPML for the nonignorable versus ignorable model; posterior item parameters.
- result: 22.9% of responses missing (9.1% omitted, 13.8% not reached). The nonignorable model fitted better (DIC 5504 against 5857; LPML -2895 against -3016). Two items (DS465Q01C, DS438Q03C) had much higher item missingness ζ than the others: some items are skipped more for reasons of the item itself.
- limits: no confidence measure; one small national sample; illustrates the method rather than testing a hypothesis about why people omit.
- borrow: the **decomposition of omission into an item term and a person term**. For us, the "person" is fixed (one model), so the analogue is: an item's decline propensity estimated from other models or other runs, used as a rival guess. In SV-2w a cross-model item propensity (does Haiku decline this item?) is a cheap secondary rival for Sonnet, and vice versa, at no extra API cost.
- next_steps_it_suggests: author: polytomous models; response times to tell omitted from not reached. For us: the item term is a property any overseer can estimate from other models' behaviour, without asking the model for a number.
- predicts_for_us: SV-2w: no bearing on the pass rule (no confidence); it supplies a rival the rule does not include.
- remap head: **design to borrow** (an item-level decline propensity as a rival guess).

## What the re-scout changes

Kumaran 2026 (2606.29490) already shows SV-2w's direction on four non-Claude models: within SimpleQA, a separately elicited number predicts abstaining (AUROC 0.60-0.88), and an item-difficulty control barely dents it. So SV-2w is partly a replication. What it adds is Claude, an announced penalty and the simple-guess margin. The re-scout adds three cheap secondary rivals: a bag-of-words guess (Wagner 2026), SimpleQA topic or answer type (Wei 2024), and cross-model item decline propensity (Guo 2019). It drops nothing and leaves the price unchanged. The reads point toward a hit in direction; how big the margin is was not found in print.
