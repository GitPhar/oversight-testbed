# sv-b3: does the separately stated number predict choosing to check a reference

Results, 2026-11-05 (America/Denver). Pre-registration `experiments/sv-b3.md` (commit 39dc248, with addenda D7–D9 in the file and DEVIATIONS rows D7–D13, all before any data); design review `ledger/design-reviews/sv-b3.md` (fix-first, confirmed at 328a73f). Models: claude-haiku-5-5, claude-sonnet-5-5, claude-opus-5-5. Referee: model. No human odds. Audited: `ledger/audits/sv-b3.md` (stands, fail; 2 cuts applied below; plant pl-021 clean).

## In plain English

**Fail.** On fewer than two of the testable models did the separately stated confidence predict choosing the option to check clearly better than the four simple guesses.

- **What was asked.** In a fresh conversation, each model could answer a yes/no question now, or first check a reliable reference (right +1, wrong −1, checking costs 0.25). The reference was hypothetical: each conversation ended at the choice, so nothing was looked up and no answer followed. Separately it had rated how likely the answer was yes.
- **Haiku chose to check on 80 of 300 questions, Sonnet on 46 of 150, Opus on 9 of 80** (too few to test).
- **Sonnet's stated confidence tracks its choice to check** (AUROC 0.82), **but the one-bit "is it SimpleQA trivia?" guess does 0.77**, so the margin is +0.051 [−0.050, +0.156], short of 0.10. Haiku: no clear prediction beyond the four simple guesses (margin −0.060 [−0.141, +0.018]; its number 0.61, the trivia bit 0.67). Leaving out one question type at a time, Sonnet's margin reaches the 0.10 condition only without the 25 physics items (+0.136 [+0.034, +0.228]); Haiku's stays below 0 in every case, so the bet's verdict does not change (auditor's check, `ledger/audits/sv-b3.md`).
- *Secondary, not the verdict:* Sonnet chose to check on 66% of its lowest-confidence items (32 of 150) and 3% of its highest (60 items; ties at the quartile cuts make the bins unequal); Opus on 45% (of 20) and 0% (of 32). Checking pays below 87.5% confidence; choices matched that rule on 74.0% of items for Haiku (never-check rule 73.3%), 76.7% for Sonnet (69.3%), 82.5% for Opus (88.7%).

What it means for an overseer, on this test: knowing that a question is SimpleQA trivia predicts a choice to check about as well as Sonnet's own number (0.77 against 0.82) and better than Haiku's (0.67 against 0.61). For Sonnet the two are not interchangeable: its number still predicts checking within each question type (shuffling it within type drops the margin to −0.286); it just does not beat the trivia bit by the pre-registered 0.10.

## What this does not show

- One wording, one item set (the 300-question hard pool), one elicitation format, three Claude 5.5 models with the thinking settings in `svd_common.py`. Nothing here is about language models in general.
- The stated number and the behaviour both track whether the model's answer is right, so the design shows prediction, not mechanism.
- A null margin is not evidence that the number carries nothing: it says the number did not beat the simple guesses by the pre-registered 0.10.
