## Papers read

### Adding Error Bars to Evals — Evan Miller (Anthropic), 2024, arXiv 2411.00640 v1 [stat.AP], https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640v1
- read_depth: full (PDF text; §2–4 read line by line)
- what_tested: a statistical framework for evaluation scores. It treats questions as draws from a super-population, gives clustered standard errors, splits variance into a between-question part and a within-question part, explains resampling answers, paired differences between models, and power.
- on_whom: worked examples, plus a re-analysis of real Anthropic model scores on DROP, RACE-H and MGSM (Table 4).
- how_scored: standard errors of the mean, with and without clustering; the variance of the mean under K resamples per question.
- result: Var(µ̂) = (Var(x) + E[σᵢ²])/n. "The simplest way to reduce the variance of µ̂ is to increase n, the number of sampled questions." Resampling K times per question reduces only E[σᵢ²]/K. In the binary-score example, Var(µ̂ | K) = Var(µ̂ | K = 1) × (1 + 2/K)/3: a reduction of 1/3 at K = 2, 1/2 at K = 4 and 5/9 at K = 6, with an upper limit of 2/3. Clustered SE on DROP is 1.34 against a naive 0.44 (3.05 times); RACE-H 1.10 times; MGSM 1.88 times. Pooling all K·N answers as independent is "inconsistent".
- limits: questions assumed independent draws from an infinite super-population; worked examples assume uniform difficulty; the power parameters are "fictional but reasonable"; it does not treat a whole run shifting at once.
- borrow: the variance split goes into the pre-registration's power section for follow-up 1. With sg-b6's figures, compare the study spread of T (Var(x)) with the call noise (E[σᵢ²]) to show whether more repeats or more studies narrows T's interval.
- next_steps_it_suggests: report clustered, paired standard errors and power before running.
- predicts_for_us: follow-up 1 (120 studies): no bearing on which side of 6.3 T lands. It predicts the interval narrows mainly through the added studies. The audit's third same-60 run: little narrowing, because once E[σᵢ²]/K ≪ Var(x), "increasing K further will have little effect on the standard error". Follow-up 2 (Opus repeat): no bearing.
- **design to borrow.** On the reader's own rough arithmetic from the audit's figures (per-grade repeat SD 4.54, almost all on the flawed cells; per-study T SD 10.9), call noise is at most about a fifth of the variance of per-study T. So a same-60 third run alone can barely move sg-b6's interval. That re-prices the audit's cheapest test: it is folded into follow-up 1 rather than drafted alone.

### Hidden Measurement Error in LLM Pipelines — Solomon Messing, 2026, arXiv 2604.11581 v6 (13 May 2026), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11581
- read_depth: partial (abstract, §1, §3 framework and D-study, §4 AILuminate and MMLU demonstrations, §6 limitations; the SI appendix was not read)
- what_tested: splits the uncertainty in an LLM evaluation pipeline into parts with a linear mixed model: items, prompt variants, temperature, judge model, their interactions, and noise across "repeated API calls with identical inputs". A decision study (D-study) then projects the standard error for other designs.
- on_whom: (i) the AILuminate safety benchmark: 36 prompts × responses from Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.1, DeepSeek Chat v3.1 and Grok 4.1 Fast (141 items), scored under 5 prompt variants × 3 temperatures × 3 judges × 8 replications = 50,760 calls; (ii) a 200-item MMLU pool; (iii) Chatbot Arena data.
- how_scored: variance components by REML, D-study standard errors, 95% CI coverage against an answer key.
- result: naive standard errors are 40–60% smaller than the corrected ones. On AILuminate, the judge panel carries 43.8% of the variance of the estimate, item×judge 16.7%, item spread within categories 20.8%, and prompt×judge 12.4%. Replicate noise and cell-level error "each contribute less than 0.5%" to the variance of the mean. 141 items, 3 prompts, 3 judges and no replication cut the SE by 49% at 9 times the cost; the full factorial with 8 replications adds only 5% more at 120 times the cost. On MMLU the D-study sends budget to more items first (43.4% of variance). "Naive wastes two-thirds of calls on replications that provide negligible returns."
- limits: variance from reasoning traces is not separated; judge×system interactions fall outside the decomposition; English only, frontier models; it cannot say whether the items span the construct; no item set like ours, where the judge and the author are the same model.
- borrow: a D-study from sg-b6's archive (studies, domains, repeats) before follow-up 1 is pre-registered, to project the interval at 120 studies and 40 domains against more repeats. The judge facet does not apply here (the graded model is the judge, by design).
- next_steps_it_suggests: run a small pilot, estimate the components, and spend on the facet the D-study ranks first.
- predicts_for_us: follow-up 1: no bearing on the outcome; it predicts more studies narrow T's interval more than repeats do. Follow-up 2: no bearing.
- **design to borrow.**

### Non-Determinism of "Deterministic" LLM Settings — Atil, Aykent, Chittams, Fu, Passonneau, Radcliffe, Rajagopal, Sloan, Tudrej, Ture, Wu, Xu & Baldwin, 2024, arXiv 2408.04667 v5 (2 Apr 2025), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04667
- read_depth: full (PDF text: abstract, §3–8)
- what_tested: whether models set to be deterministic (temperature 0, top-p 1, fixed seed) give the same outputs and accuracies across repeated runs.
- on_whom: GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4o, Llama-3-70B-Instruct, Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, on eight tasks from BBH and MMLU (100 to 282 items each), zero-shot and few-shot, 10 runs each (20 runs of GPT-4o and Llama-3-70B on college math).
- how_scored: accuracy per run; TARr@N and TARa@N, the share of items with identical raw or parsed outputs across N runs.
- result: accuracy varies by up to 15% across naturally occurring runs, and the gap between best and worst possible performance reaches 70%. Mixtral-8x7B on college math ranged 72 points (75% to 3%). The 20-run accuracy distributions are not normal (Kolmogorov-Smirnov p < 10⁻⁹). Llama-3-8B run locally without serving optimizations was deterministic. The authors suspect continuous batching, chunked prefill or prefix caching on hosted APIs. "None of the LLMs consistently delivers repeatable accuracy across all tasks".
- limits: no Claude model and no graded-score judging task; runs are not timed or dated, so a whole run shifting at once is not separated from per-item noise added up over a run; the answer extraction is hand-coded.
- borrow: nothing new for the design; it supports reading the old 60 studies inside follow-up 1's run against sg-b6's run (the audit's third-run check), as a test of a whole-run shift.
- next_steps_it_suggests: report the spread across several runs (maximum minus minimum) instead of a single number.
- predicts_for_us: follow-up 1: no bearing on the outcome; it names the rival that a single run's T can sit off its long-run value. Follow-up 2: same rival for Opus's one-repeat +5.03, with no direction.
- **rival named.** A whole-run shift is possible on hosted APIs. Nothing here tells it apart from per-call noise, and sg-b6's two Haiku repeats were taken in the same run, so sg-b6 does not either. The old-60 half of follow-up 1, graded in a new run, is the cheapest test that does, and follow-up 2 does it for Opus.

### Same Input, Different Scores — Fiona Lau, 2026, arXiv 2603.04417 v1 (4 Feb 2026), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04417
- read_depth: full (PDF text; most numbers are only in figures, which pdftotext does not render)
- what_tested: how stable an LLM judge's numerical scores are across ten repeated runs, how models differ on identical inputs, and the effect of temperature.
- on_whom: GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Claude-Haiku-4.5 and Claude-Sonnet-4.5, at temperature 0 and 1, on question–answer pairs from an enterprise RAG chatbot (the count is not stated in the text read; not public).
- how_scored: 0–1 scores for relevance, accuracy and completeness; the SD across ten runs per question.
- result: at temperature 0 the models were not deterministic, with visible spikes for the Claude models. Completeness varied most. Lowering the temperature reduced the SD for the GPT models and most for Gemini (near zero in several cases), but "the reduction in standard deviation is minimal" for the Anthropic family. For one Claude setting, completeness SD at temperature 0 was higher than at 1. No numbers are stated in the text.
- limits: one prompt template; proprietary data; no run-level or day-level structure; no self-evaluation.
- borrow: nothing; sg-b6 already measures per-call repeat spread on Haiku (55% identical, SD of change 4.54).
- next_steps_it_suggests: test prompt designs that reduce inconsistency; add open-source models; compare with human raters.
- predicts_for_us: follow-ups 1 and 2: no bearing.
- **changes nothing.** It measures per-input spread across repeated runs of a Claude Haiku judge. That is the noise sg-b6's two repeats already measured. It does not separate a whole-run shift.

### Replicating studies in which samples of participants respond to samples of stimuli — Westfall, Judd & Kenny, 2015, Perspectives on Psychological Science 10(3):390–399, DOI 10.1177/1745691614564879 (with the companion: Westfall, Kenny & Judd, 2014, J Exp Psychol Gen 143(5):2020–2045, DOI 10.1037/xge0000014)
- read_depth: abstract (PubMed efetch, both papers; the full texts at jakewestfall.org returned HTTP 410 and at SSRN HTTP 403)
- what_tested: (2015) how to replicate studies whose result rests on a sample of stimuli; (2014) power and optimal design when participants and stimuli are both random factors.
- on_whom: methodological; the 2015 abstract's example is the risky-shift literature, "almost entirely based on a single stimulus sample that was later discovered to be highly unrepresentative".
- how_scored: power analysis for crossed random factors (2014).
- result: power "typically does not approach unity as the number of participants goes to infinity" but approaches a maximum set by the stimulus sample (2014). A replication should use "a new but comparable sample of stimuli", and the stimulus sample must often be enlarged. The abstract recommends "expanded stimulus sets", meaning the original stimuli plus new ones (2015). No numbers in the abstracts.
- limits: abstract only; about human participants, where our repeated calls play the role of participants and our studies the role of stimuli (the reader's mapping, not the authors').
- borrow: follow-up 1's design, sg-b6's 60 studies plus 60 new studies in 20 new domains, graded together. It tests whether +5.26 belongs to this study sample, which more Haiku repeats on the same 60 cannot do.
- next_steps_it_suggests: sample stimuli as carefully as participants; enlarge the stimulus set for power.
- predicts_for_us: follow-up 1: no bearing on the outcome; it predicts that added studies, not added repeats, decide it. Follow-up 2: no bearing.
- **design to borrow.**

## What the re-scout changes

Miller 2024 and Messing 2026 show that repeats on the same items cannot shrink the part of an interval that comes from item spread, and the audit says spread sets sg-b6's width. So the audit's cheapest test, a third run on the same 60 studies, is dropped as a standalone follow-up. It is folded into an expanded-set run (Westfall, Judd and Kenny 2015): the old 60 plus 60 new studies, graded together. Atil et al. 2024 name a whole-run shift as an unseparated rival; the old-60 half and the Opus repeat test it. No odds are stated.

**Novelty:** partly new. Novelty line stands:

> **Novelty:** partly new. Papers in print already show, in our direction, that a model goes easier on the same text in its own assistant turn than in a user turn (Khullar, Hopkins, Wang and Roger 2026, arXiv 2603.04582 v1, strongest on-policy; Tsui 2025, arXiv 2507.02778 v3), and they show the rival this test removes: LLM judges disagree with themselves across identical runs (Haldar and Hockenmaier 2025, Rating Roulette, arXiv 2510.27106: Krippendorff's alpha 0.265 to 0.563 over three runs on MT-Bench, three open-weight judges), and human rating separates drift within a session from "scale instability" across sessions (Quellmalz 1980, CSE Report 136, abstract only). The nearest case of a self-favouring effect shrinking under a stricter comparison is Roytburg et al. 2026 (arXiv 2601.22548 v2: "only 51% of initial findings retain statistical significance" once a judge's votes for its own wrong answers are set against its votes for outcome-matched other models' wrong answers), which changes the baseline, not the run, and reports no repeated runs. A direct search found nothing in print that re-measures a quoted-copy leniency gap with every cell graded in one run, set against the same gap read across runs. Here Haiku 5.5's gap stays above zero but is 46% of its same-run blunting, under the 6.3 bar with an interval that cannot rule the bar out (its fall from sg-b4 is split between the two cells, neither shift distinguishable from zero, so run-to-run noise is neither shown nor ruled out as the cause), while Opus 5.5's holds at about its full size (+5.0, against sg-b4's +4.0).
