### The Self-Correction Illusion: Role Relabeling Gates Explicit Error Flagging — Chen, Su, Lin, Li & Chiang, 2026, arXiv 2606.05976 v2 (31 Jul 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05976v2
- read_depth: **re-read**, only for the parts this result touches. Read: abstract, §3.1–3.3, §4.1–4.5, §5.1–5.3 (limitations), Tables 1–4 and 7, Appendix C.1 (Table 13). This paper is under check by a separate reader as possibly misread in the program's earlier lines, so every number below is from this session's text.
- what_tested: whether a model's failure to correct a wrong claim in its own `<thought>` block comes from the claim's role label. The claim is kept byte-identical and moved into a user message, a tool response or a system `<memory>` block, with one appended audit instruction held fixed.
- on_whom: nine models across 12 model-domain settings (Qwen2.5-72B, Llama-3.3-70B, gpt-oss-20B, Gemma-3-12B, Phi-4-14B, Qwen3-30B, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite), on math, generated logic and BBH logical deduction. n = 30 paired tasks per setting, drawn from a failure pool (tasks where the model had not corrected the claim on its own).
- how_scored: the explicit-correction rate. A locked Qwen-72B judge at T = 0 says YES or NO to "names and rejects the wrong claim". Paired bootstrap.
- result:
  - Relabelling raised correction by 23 to 93 points, significant in 10 of 12 settings.
  - Wrapper ladder on Qwen-72B math (Table 3): 16.7% with no wrapper; 33.3–40.0% with label-free boundaries (brackets, a NOTE: prefix, an XML tag); 70.0% with a system `<memory>` tag.
  - Audit swaps with the claim left in the model's own `<thought>` (Table 4): 16.7% with "Audit your reasoning", against 3.3%, 0.0% and 23.3% under three self-distrust audits.
  - Audit paraphrases (Table 7): with the claim in place, correction ran 10.0% to 33.3% across five paraphrases. **With the claim already outside the model's turn (`<memory>`), the paraphrases alone moved correction from 40.0% to 70.0%.**
  - Tag wording at a fixed system role (Table 13): a nonsense tag `<xqzy>` reached 30.0%, against 50.0–70.0% for meaningful tags, so the label's words, not only a boundary, carry the effect.
- limits: verifiable tasks only; n = 30 per setting; a failure pool rather than ordinary use; correction is a binary named-and-rejected, not a graded score; the claim is a single wrong intermediate value or ordering, not prose; no Claude 5.5 model. The authors list free-form reasoning as future work.
- borrow: the nonsense-tag control (a label with no role meaning) and the wrapper ladder, which change one surface element at a time.
- next_steps_it_suggests: test whether addressability governs self-correction beyond verifiable tasks; extend to free-form reasoning.
- predicts_for_us:
  - **F1 (closing-line re-run):** no clean prediction. With the claim outside the model's turn, which is our quoted copy's position, rewording the closing audit moved Qwen-72B by up to 30 points, larger than with the claim inside. Either sign is possible.
  - **F2 (header or labels):** a label's words can carry an effect beyond a bare boundary, which leans toward a role-word label mattering. But Chen's labels are chat-template roles, not words inside one user message.
- **Head: design to borrow** (the nonsense or neutral label control, for F2). It also **corrects how the earlier lines read it**. The Novelty line's "less than moving the text out of the model's own turn" holds only with the claim inside the model's turn. Table 7's out-of-turn row, the closer analogue to a quoted copy, shows the closing instruction moving correction by as much as 30 points.

### JudgeSense: A Benchmark for Prompt Sensitivity in LLM-as-a-Judge Systems — Bellibatlu, Raff & Zhang, 2026, arXiv 2604.23478 v3 (18 Sep 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23478v3
- read_depth: **re-read**, only for the parts this result touches. Read: abstract, §1 (the JSS definition, Eq. 1), Table 1's Claude rows, §5.1 findings 1–6, §5.4 (scale width), §5.9 (limits), Appendix D (setup, checkpoint table). This paper is also under check as possibly misread, so every number below is from this session's text.
- what_tested: whether a judge returns the *same label* when its instruction is reworded without changing what it asks. Each item is judged under template A, A again, reworded B, and B again.
- on_whom: 25 judges from six providers, among them claude-haiku-4-5-20251001, claude-sonnet-4-5 and claude-opus-4-7. 880 items on four tasks: TruthfulQA factuality, SummEval coherence (1–5 scale), TREC-COVID relevance, MT-Bench preference.
- how_scored: JSS is the share of item pairs given an *identical* label (Eq. 1); ΔJSS = JSS_paraphrase − JSS_repeat; item-clustered bootstrap.
- result:
  - claude-haiku on coherence: JSS 0.792 against repeat agreement 0.998, ΔJSS −0.206 [−0.258, −0.154].
  - claude-opus-4-7 on coherence: 0.736 against 0.916, ΔJSS −0.180 [−0.236, −0.128]. Opus 4.7 ran at the provider's default temperature, every other judge at 0.
  - Pooled coherence ΔJSS: −0.181.
  - Collapsed to a binary scale (ratings 4–5 against 1–3), pooled coherence falls to −0.074. "Nearly three-fifths of coherence's excess is the width of its decision space."
- limits: one fixed set of templates per task; English only; no OpenAI model; Sonnet and Opus preference cells cut short by budget; intervals cluster by item, not by source record.
- borrow: nothing new for a mean-shift design. JSS is a match/no-match agreement rate and has **no direction**: it cannot say whether a reworded instruction is stricter or kinder. On a 0–100 scale almost any rewording changes some grades, so a JudgeSense-style instability predicts noise in S, not a sign.
- next_steps_it_suggests: measure judge stability against a repeat baseline taken in the same transport the judge will run in.
- predicts_for_us:
  - **F1:** no bearing on the sign or size of S.
  - **F2:** no bearing.
  - Correction to the earlier lines: on identical prompts at T = 0 claude-haiku agrees with itself 0.998 on coherence. So "judges disagree with themselves on identical prompts" is not what its coherence row shows: the −0.206 is the cost of *rewording*, net of repeats.
- **Head: changes nothing** for either follow-up. It does **correct the earlier lines' use of it**: an agreement drop, about three-fifths of it scale width on this task, was read as rewording "moving graded verdicts", which implies a shift.

### DialDefer: detecting and mitigating LLM dialogic deference — Rabbani, Sahoo, Mathew, Mondal, Ketharaman, Bozdag & Hakkani-Tür, 2026, arXiv 2601.10896 v2 (4 Jun 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10896
- read_depth: full (main text §1–6 and limitations; appendices not read).
- what_tested: whether the same claim gets a different verdict when it is posed as a statement to verify ("Is this statement correct?") or attributed to a speaker in a dialogue ("Is Speaker 2 correct?"). §5.2 runs speaker-label ablations with the content held fixed.
- on_whom: GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5-mini, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Gemma-3-12B; nine Q/A datasets (2,964 test instances) plus 280 Reddit r/AIO conflicts; 3,244 evaluation items in all. The label ablations are on GPT-4o-mini, TruthfulQA.
- how_scored: Dialogic Deference Score, DDS = (accuracy change on correct speakers) − (accuracy change on incorrect speakers), in points. Positive means deference, negative means skepticism. McNemar tests.
- result:
  - Mean |DDS| was 15.9 points while accuracy moved under 2 points.
  - Four of five models deferred significantly (Qwen +33.8, Gemma +29.5, GPT-4o-mini +9.0, GPT-5-mini +8.3); GPT-4o was near neutral (−1.1).
  - The sign depends on the domain: GPT-4o ran −53.0 on GPQA and +57.9 on r/AIO.
  - Label ablations: abstract labels (A/B against Q/A) and names shifted DDS only −5.2 to +2.4. Labelling the answering speaker "LLM" instead of "User" dropped DDS by 16.2 points, a 17.7-point swing: content labelled as an AI's is judged *more skeptically*.
- limits: the binary correct/incorrect verdict is not a graded penalty; the label ablation covers one model and one dataset; no Claude model; English, Western platforms.
- borrow: the neutral-label control (A/B), which separates "any label change" from "a role word", for F2; and a directional score next to agreement, as our S already is.
- next_steps_it_suggests: calibrate deference without over-correcting into skepticism; study human-versus-AI attribution further.
- predicts_for_us:
  - **F2:** a role label naming the text's author as an AI ("Assistant:") should make the judge *stricter*. Removing it would then make the grade kinder, and the labels' share B would be ≤ 0, a **fail** for a labels-carry-it rule.
  - **F1:** no bearing.
- **Head: rival named** (for F2). A label effect may run *against* sg-b4's leniency: the AI-assistant label adds skepticism rather than self-identification. A labels-only cell with a neutral-label control separates the two.

### Sycophancy negatively affects LLM-as-a-judge in conflict evaluation — Farzi, Dietz & Carton, 2026, GEM workshop (ACL Anthology 2026.gem-main.45), PDF https://www.cs.unh.edu/~dietz/papers/farzi2026sycophancy.pdf
- read_depth: full (main text §1–6, limitations; the appendix prompt examples not read).
- what_tested: whether replacing one speaker's username with "Me" in a transcript's speaker labels (no other change) biases blame, attacker identification and attack detection.
- on_whom: Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B (10 samples each at τ = 0.7) and gpt-4.1-mini (1 sample); Conversations Gone Awry (Wikipedia talk pages, one attack in the final turn, 2–9 speakers).
- how_scored: alignment, accuracy and recall against the annotated attacker; Δatt = attacker-as-Me minus neutral; cluster-robust SEs.
- result:
  - Blame alignment fell 9.9 points (hidden attack) and 8.2 points (visible) when the attacker was labelled "Me"; attack detection moved within ±1 point.
  - Up to 40.6% of correct blame judgments flipped to incorrect.
  - gpt-4.1-mini, the one API model, shifted least (blame Δatt −1.0 hidden, −2.1 visible).
- limits: one corpus; English; the attack is always in the last turn; small open models carry most of the effect; gpt-4.1-mini was sampled once.
- borrow: the label-only substitution, which changes the speaker label and nothing else. That is the shape of F2's labels cell.
- next_steps_it_suggests: vary grammatical person and social distance; train for perspective invariance.
- predicts_for_us:
  - **F2:** a speaker label alone can move a judgment several points without any content change, so B ≠ 0 is plausible. But the cue here is self-identification ("Me"), not an AI-assistant role, and the API model moved least. Size on Claude 5.5: no prediction.
  - **F1:** no bearing.
- **Head: design to borrow** (F2's label-only cell). It also confirms that labels are a live carrier, not a long shot.

### Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations — Miller, 2024, arXiv 2411.00640 v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640
- read_depth: full (main text §1–5; appendices on the clustered SE derivation and sample-size formula not read).
- what_tested: a framework, not an experiment. Eval items are treated as draws from a super-population, with the score variance split into the variance of item means and the mean variance of answers within an item. It covers clustered SEs, resampling, paired differences and power.
- on_whom: worked examples, plus real numbers on two evals with Anthropic models (Table 4).
- how_scored: standard errors and confidence intervals.
- result:
  - Clustered SEs can be over 3 times naive SEs (Table 4).
  - Resampling each item K times shrinks only the within-item variance: Var(s_i) = σ_i²/K.
  - Paired differences remove shared item variance.
  - Pooling all K × n answers as independent is "inconsistent".
- limits: no new data; its examples are Bernoulli scores.
- borrow:
  - **Repeats are the right lever for our designs.** sg-b7's audit found the spread of S matched the dry run's call noise (Haiku SD 7.95 against about 8.3 implied; Opus 2.70 against about 2.9), so most of the variance is within-item and K repeats shrink it by about 1/K.
  - Opus on 60 studies once: a half-width of about 1.96 × 2.70/√60 ≈ 0.68 points.
  - Three repeats on 60 studies: about 1.96 × 2.70/(√3 × √60) ≈ 0.39 points.
  - Average the K repeats per study *before* the domain-cluster bootstrap, never pool them.
- next_steps_it_suggests: report n, the cluster count and SEs; plan n from the minimum detectable effect.
- predicts_for_us:
  - **F1:** no outcome prediction. It sets the arithmetic: at the observed Opus S of +0.33, a 60-study single run (±0.7) would still span both 0 and the 0.75 bar, and only about three repeats on 60 studies (±0.4) could read "not the lead-in" at that S.
  - **F2:** three repeats bring Haiku to about ±1.2 points.
- **Head: design to borrow** (for both follow-ups' sizing).
