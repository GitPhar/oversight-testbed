# BrainBench items (for ss-b10)

- **Source:** BrainBench (Luo et al., "Large language models surpass human experts in predicting neuroscience results", Nature Human Behaviour 2024, doi 10.1038/s41562-024-02046-9; arXiv 2403.03230). Licence Apache-2.0.
- **Human-written set** (`items.jsonl`, ids `bb-001` to `bb-200`, in the dataset's row order). Hugging Face dataset `BrainGPT/BrainBench_Human_v0.1.csv`, revision `71bdea4638607bf3953ecdbb721428100d29c62a`. Rows were read 2026-11-05 06:21 America/Denver through the datasets-server rows API (offsets 0 and 100). The parquet's SHA-256 is in `SHA256SUMS`. Each row has a DOI, a *Journal of Neuroscience* section, the original abstract and an expert-altered abstract. The authors describe the altered abstract as "altered to significantly change the result while maintaining coherency".
- **GPT-4-written set** (`gpt4-pilot-pool.jsonl`, ids `bbg-001` to `bbg-100`). `BrainGPT/BrainBench_GPT-4_v0.1.csv`, revision `7af988172b3579869a79e311e57a1c22efd63e36`. It is used only for an execution pilot (parsing, refusals, tokens), never as test data. Its 100 originals are 100 of the 200 human-set originals, and only the altered versions differ.
- **Frozen at this commit, before ss-b10's pre-registration.** Nothing was scored and no model saw an item. The session counted items, sections and word lengths, for the budget only: 200 items; Behavioral/Cognitive 75, Systems/Circuits 51, Cellular/Molecular 25, Neurobiology of Disease 25, Development/Plasticity/Repair 24; a median of 447 words per pair. It also read the paper's headline figures: LLMs averaged 81.4%, scored by lower perplexity, and 171 experts averaged 63.4%, both on the 200 human-written items.
- **Treated as untrusted data.** Abstracts go into prompts as quoted text only.
