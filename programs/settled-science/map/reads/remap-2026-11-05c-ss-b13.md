## 2. Papers read (5 new full reads)

### ForecastBench — Karger et al., 2025, arXiv 2409.19839v5 (28 Feb 2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19839
- read_depth: full (HTML v5, read in two passes: characters 0–100,000 and the remainder with the appendix leaderboards)
- what_tested: LLM forecasts of future real-world events (market and dataset questions), against superforecasters and the public.
- on_whom: many LLMs; 200-question human comparison set; N = 6,389 questions in the full LLM set.
- how_scored: Brier score.
- result: Brier falls with Chatbot Arena score (y = 0.506 − 0.000298x, R² = 0.47, r = −0.68) and with training compute (r = −0.67, p = 0.046, "marginally significant"). Superforecasters 0.096, public 0.121, best LLM (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, scratchpad) 0.122. On the Halawi et al. set (Table 16), small or older models sit near or above a coin: GPT-3.5-Turbo-1106 0.261, Llama-2-7B 0.264, Llama-2-13B 0.268, Mistral-7B-Instruct 0.243, against GPT-4o 0.207 and Claude-3.5-Sonnet 0.178.
- limits: correlational scaling fits with wide intervals; world events, not research findings; no base-rate or shortcut baseline in the results; the leaderboards list the top 50 only.
- borrow: report each model's Brier beside the constant 0.25 and the capability ordering, as ss-b13 does.
- next_steps_it_suggests: track the gap to superforecasters as models improve.
- predicts_for_us: F1 (flattery rater on round 1): no bearing. F2 (Haiku with thinking): no bearing on thinking; it predicts the size gap (Haiku below Sonnet), which ss-b13 already found.
- **already shown:** that a weaker model in a family forecasts worse and can sit near a constant forecaster is in print for world-event forecasting; ss-b13's Haiku-below-Sonnet pattern agrees. It does not test a publication-bias rule, so the Novelty line's new part is untouched.

### Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd — Schoenegger, Tuminauskaite, Park and Tetlock, 2024, arXiv 2402.19379v6 (22 Jul 2024), https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19379
- read_depth: full (HTML v6)
- what_tested: whether a median of 12 LLMs' forecasts matches a human crowd on a Metaculus tournament; whether GPT-4 and Claude 2 update on the human median.
- on_whom: 12 LLMs, 31 binary questions, 1,007 forecasts; 925 human forecasters.
- how_scored: Brier, against a 50% no-information benchmark (pre-registered).
- result: ensemble Brier 0.20, human crowd 0.19, 50% benchmark 0.25 (ensemble vs benchmark t(30) = −2.35, p = 0.026). Mean LLM forecast 57.35% while 14 of 31 questions resolved yes, which the authors call an acquiescence-like bias. Per model: GPT-4 0.15, Claude 2 0.21, Mistral-7B-Instruct and Qwen-7B-Chat 0.24, GPT-3.5-Turbo-Instruct and Llama-2-70B 0.25, Coral 0.38.
- limits: 31 questions; models queried through web interfaces; model versions changed mid-study; no research findings.
- borrow: a lean statistic (mean forecast against the base rate of the favoured outcome). For us: how often Haiku's pick agrees with the good-news tag, against how often the tag is right (the ss-b13 audit computed this for Sonnet: 70% against 74%); $0 from the archive, a reported row for F1.
- next_steps_it_suggests: more models, better calibration, test whether models beat simple averaging with humans.
- predicts_for_us: F1: a positive lean is the expected failure mode of weaker models, so the flattery rater is the right rival (no direct prediction of the rule). F2: no bearing.
- **rival named:** a "lean toward the favoured side" bias is documented in LLM forecasts and is what the good-news rule and the flattery rater stand in for; Haiku's miss fits it, and F1 tests it on every item instead of the 38 tagged ones.

### Predicting Field Experiments with Large Language Models — Chen, Hu and Lu, 2025, arXiv 2504.01167v3 (21 May 2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01167
- read_depth: full (HTML v3)
- what_tested: whether LLMs pick the reported conclusion of an economics field experiment among the original, a reversed and an unrelated variant.
- on_whom: 276 field experiments, 1,261 conclusions, from four top economics journals 2000–2024; GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4o; 20 repeats.
- how_scored: conclusion accuracy and paper accuracy.
- result: basic prompt / chain-of-thought: GPT-3.5 Turbo 61% / 68%, GPT-4 Turbo 66% / 76%, GPT-4o 75% / 78%. Chain-of-thought gains +7, +10 and +3 points: the weaker same-family models gained most. Topic alone had no significant effect; sentiment-by-topic interactions did (ethnicity significant on all models).
- limits: one field; Claude did the extraction and labelling; no majority-class or positive-result baseline; mostly pre-cutoff papers (2024 papers scored higher, which the authors read as no memorisation).
- borrow: the basic-versus-reasoning contrast inside one model, on the same items.
- next_steps_it_suggests: none beyond stating practical constraints.
- predicts_for_us: F2: predicts Haiku gains with thinking on (pass direction; whether by enough to clear ss-b13's 0.01 margin, the paper cannot say). F1: no bearing.
- **rival named:** ss-b13's Haiku and Sonnet ran with thinking off and Opus could not, so "Haiku is smaller" and "Haiku did not reason" are confounded; this paper's same-family reasoning gains name the rival. The cheapest test that excludes it is F2.

### Identifying Non-Replicable Social Science Studies with Language Models — Saynova, Hansson, Bruinsma, Fredén and Johansson, 2025, arXiv 2503.10671v1 (10 Mar 2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10671
- read_depth: full (HTML v1)
- what_tested: whether LLM-simulated samples flag which behavioural studies fail to replicate.
- on_whom: 14 Many Labs 2 studies (9 replicated, 5 not); Llama 3 8B, Qwen 2 7B, Mistral 7B, GPT-4o; 1,000 responses per setup; temperatures 0.1–1.5.
- how_scored: F1, precision, recall.
- result: best F1 0.77 (Mistral 7B at temperature 1.0), GPT-4o 0.67 at 0.5; low temperatures gave effect sizes up to 21.6 when no Many Labs 2 effect exceeded 2.0.
- limits: 14 studies; no constant or base-rate baseline; synthetic participants, not direction prediction.
- borrow: nothing for these follow-ups.
- next_steps_it_suggests: larger study sets, other model types, variance control.
- predicts_for_us: no bearing on F1 or F2.
- **changes nothing:** 14 studies and a simulated-participant design; the small models matching GPT-4o there says nothing about a direction-and-probability bettor against a shortcut rule.

## 4. Novelty

Novelty line stands:

**Novelty:** partly new. Published work already shows that a model one tier down from the top forecasts research results about as well as the top one, and that much smaller models do worse. BrainBench (Luo et al., 2024, arXiv 2403.03230; Nature Human Behaviour) found that 7B models "performed comparably to larger models", while TinyLlama (1.1B) was "falling much behind the 7B models". SciPredict (arXiv 2604.10718, 2026) has Claude Sonnet 4.5 at 22.55% against Opus 4.5 at 23.05% and Llama 3.1 8B at 14.65%. Hewitt et al. (2024, working paper) found that accuracy "improved steadily and markedly across generations of LLMs". Ours agrees on the size pattern: Sonnet is not shown different from Opus (Brier 0.168 against 0.163; paired difference +0.004, interval −0.018 to +0.027) and Haiku is weaker (0.208; paired difference from Opus +0.044, interval +0.012 to +0.076). What we found in no paper is a test of a same-family smaller model against a publication-bias rule ("bet on the paper's good-news side") or a "which version flatters the paper" rater. Wen et al. (2025, arXiv 2506.00794) do not mention publication bias, and CUSP (Wu et al., 2026, arXiv 2605.22681) has always-No, chance and mean-date baselines but no publication-bias one; Jin et al. (2024, arXiv 2411.17595) found GPT-4o-mini predicting every clinical trial a success, a smaller same-family model collapsing onto a positive-outcome rule, but report no such rule as a baseline. So the result that Haiku beats a coin but is not shown to beat the good-news rule by the 0.01 margin, and that neither smaller model beats the flattery rater on round 2's items (a reported row), is new.
