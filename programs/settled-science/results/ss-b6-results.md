# ss-b6 results: does round 2 reduce to "picks the flattering version"?

Pre-registration `c9787f9` (`experiments/ss-b6.md`). Design review `ledger/design-reviews/ss-b6.md`: fix-first, then go after D32–D38. The run followed `03d134c`. No new random draw: the items and option order come from the ss-b4 seed `e8f921e`. **Unaudited**: the engine session draws the plant and launches the audit next.

## In plain English

In round 2 the bettor picked the published sentence over its one-word-flipped twin 93% of the time. The audit pointed out a simpler explanation it had not ruled out: published sentences usually report the paper's win, so "pick whichever version makes the paper look good" might do as well, without knowing any result.

To test that, a fresh session of the same model, with no tools, saw the same 92 sentence pairs and was asked only which version is *better news for the method the paper itself proposes*. It was told to treat both as hypothetical. **That flattery rule alone picked the published sentence 90% of the time** (Brier 0.106), against the bettor's 93% (Brier 0.070). **So most of round 2's result is explained by picking the version that flatters the paper.**

The bettor did slightly better, and that clears the pre-registered bar, but only just:
- **Raw comparison:** the bettor wins by 0.036 (interval 0.016 to 0.057).
- **Recalibrated comparison** (both sides given the "higher / more / better" word and fitted the same way): it wins by 0.027 (interval 0.002 to 0.054). The upper bound sits a hair below zero.
- **Fragile:** removing any one of three items (r2-056, r2-059, r2-063) turns it into a null, and so does dropping either the interpretability or the model-psychology query bucket. Removing the single most favourable item flips it.
- **The rater was noisier than the bettor.** Re-asked on 46 items with the options reversed, the rater gave the same side 91% of the time, against the bettor's 98% on the same items. Its average inconsistency was 5.0 points against 4.2. The design review warned that a noisier rater can lose to a bettor that knows only flattery, a false pass about 8% of the time at the bettor's own noise, and more beyond it. **So this narrow, fragile pass is weak evidence that the bettor knows anything beyond what flatters the paper.**

**The bettor and the rater both ran as claude-opus-5-5 with adaptive thinking at effort low, with reasoning hidden; the result is about that setting.** (D18, D20.)

## What it cannot do

- **The rater is the same model as the bettor.** It may share the bettor's sense of which version is real. G3 found 8 rationales that reasoned that way, below the threshold of 10.
- **"Better news for the paper's method" is one prompt's judgment.** A "more striking finding" (publishability) rival is not tested by this prompt (D32).
- **It covers only the round-2 template items.** It says nothing about round 1.
