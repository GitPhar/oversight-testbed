# Site walk, 2026-11-04 evening (before changes)

Live site at https://gitphar.github.io/oversight-testbed, screenshotted at 1366×900 and 390×844 (phone) with Playwright, before any change on branch `site-evening`. Written as a stranger who has never heard of this project.

**Home.** The top sentence tells me what this is in one breath, and the three numbered panels answer the questions I would ask next (what is it, what does the person do, why a quantum chip). Then the diagram undercuts the page. Two lines of small print run out of their boxes: under "COCKPIT" the gold line spills past both edges, and under "PRE-REGISTER → DESIGN REVIEW" the green line is wider than the box. It looks unfinished. The diagram also carries numbers that do not match the rest of the site: "Brier 0.277 on 7" for the model referee and "0.091 on 2" for the chip, while the calibration page says 14 bets. They were typed into the picture by hand and have gone stale. The chip line under panel 3 says 46 of 420 seconds, and the instruments page says 58. On the phone the page reads well, but the tile says "3 programs" while the diagram names four.

**Map.** This is the most striking page. Paths fan out from a seed and the crosses and dots read at a glance, and the replay bar is right there. On a phone the drawing shrinks to type I cannot read, but the list underneath saves it. Only three program tabs exist, though the home diagram and the record mention a program 4 (settled science) with real results, so I wonder where it went.

**Cockpit.** The "Next to run" strip at the top is the most useful thing on the page: it shows what happens next without telling me the machine's odds. The cards ask one plain question each, and I like that. But the page never tells me what the human has actually done so far. The phrase "27 of 28 still need your odds" does not tell a visitor whether any human number has ever been given. (None has, but I only learn that on the instruments page.) The path-dependence card says "Program 2 has 2 settled bets so far; this opens at 10", which is a number written into the card by hand, and I can't see the count moving. On a phone the page scrolls sideways: one long cost label ("$0 API; an hour of a fresh session; opens at program 2's 10th settled bet") is wider than the screen.

**Instruments.** The gauges are dense but honest, and each one says where its number came from, which I trust. The "Referees" section is wrong in a way a careful reader will catch. It says "the same engine, two referees" and puts program 4's three bets, which the published record graded, in the "AI-graded" column (5 of 11 came true). But program 4's whole point is a third referee. The chip-seconds gauge (58 s) disagrees with the home page (46 s).

**Calibration.** The chart is clear: odds across, hits on top, misses below, diamonds for buckets. But there is an orange "Human" dot among the misses at about 10%, and the key says "Human". Every other page says no human odds were ever recorded. As a stranger I now distrust the whole page. The dots are all one blue, so I can't see which referee graded which bet, and the prose (from `brier.md`) says the referees differ a lot (0.263, 0.062, 0.388). The one fact I want, where the hits sat, is buried in the fourth paragraph below the chart.

**Kill log.** The idea is good: nothing deleted, newest first, each with a reason. But the top six cards, everything closed today, say "Why it died: No diagnosis recorded yet." This sits right under a headline that says "40 directions closed so far, each with its diagnosis", so the page contradicts itself on its first screen. The reasons do exist somewhere, because the instruments page and the result lines hint at them, but they are not shown here.

**Record.** A long, careful timeline of program 1, but only program 1. Programs 2, 3 and 4 ran today, and none of them appears. In particular, program 4's first three results passed. Those are the only passes since the founding week, and the record does not mention them. Nor does it say what the auditor said about them: that they passed once, on one model and one item set, and are not replicated. A stranger reading this page would think the project stopped on 4 November morning. On a phone it is very long (about 25,000 px) but readable, with no sideways scroll.
